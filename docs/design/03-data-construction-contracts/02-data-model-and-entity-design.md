# Data Model and Entity Design — AI ahaMatic

## 1. Purpose and Reading Order

This document realizes `03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` in full — the core data-model rules that elaborate INV-04 (§3 there), entity and schema definition (§4), the validation contract (§5), relationships and referential integrity (§6), migration-safety constraints (§7), backward-compatibility for stored data (§8), and temporal integrity and history (§9) — as concrete, physical mechanism. It answers **how** a builder defines an entity and a schema, what makes a definition complete and a write valid, how a relationship stays referentially sound across its full lifecycle, how a schema evolves without breaking what already exists under it, and how stored builder data survives change and time.

It is the second document of the Data, Construction & Contracts layer, depending directly on `01-application-construction-design.md` (H19). It also settles four storage-shape obligations five already-written documents bound to this document by name, and makes one explicit determination on an obligation H19 attempted to hand here but which this document declines. A later consolidated amendment (`BACKLOG.md` §1j and §1h-destination) discharges two further obligations two downstream documents handed here after they were written: the process-definition catalog and instance anchor `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` names as this document's own representability constraint, and a fourth Entity Access Gateway check `05-api-contract-design.md` names as the destination for the still-open half of end-user-role-scoped record access. The two are discharged together, with sight of each other, because a process step's own Gateway calls raise a question neither obligation resolves alone (§9.7).

It is structured as a pyramid: first the physical mechanism that realizes entity and schema definition, including the process-definition catalog and instance anchor (§3); then the validation contract and the single mediation point that carries it, including the fourth check for end-user role-scoped record reach (§4); then relationships and referential integrity (§5); then migration-safety (§6); then backward-compatibility (§7); then temporal integrity and history, including the erasure mechanism it must carry (§8); then the storage-shape obligations this document discharges or declines, one by one (§9); then the bidirectional boundary against `02-platform-data-model-design.md` (§10); then composition with tenant isolation and authentication (§11); then the evidence this mechanism produces (§12); then the design decisions this document records (§13); then its boundaries, precedence, and binding rules (§14–§16).

**Verified versus reasoned (`PROCESS.md` §12.3).** Every finding in this document is **reasoned** — from the cited specification and the cited, already-written upstream design documents. No time-sensitive ecosystem, product, or vendor claim is made anywhere in this document.

## 2. Scope and What This Document Does Not Own

This document owns: the physical mechanism that realizes entity and schema definition, complete against the five definitional elements the specification fixes (§3), including the process-definition catalog and instance anchor `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §4.1 binds here (§3.5); the validation contract and the single mediation point through which every write and read of builder-defined entity data passes (§4), including a fourth check bounding end-user role-scoped, construct-mediated record reach (§4.4); referential-integrity enforcement across a relationship's full lifecycle, including the INV-04 particulars for builder-defined data (§5); migration-safety as a concrete, all-or-nothing mechanism (§6); the backward-compatibility determination for a schema evolution (§7); the physical realization of temporal integrity and history, including the mechanism able to purge personal-data content from every retained version (§8); and the explicit discharge or decline of every storage-shape obligation handed to this document (§9).

This document does **not** own, and does not decide:

- **The platform's own persistent schema, fixed at build time.** `02-platform-data-model-design.md` owns the tenant → application hierarchy, the two isolation strengths, the platform registry, and the two platform-owned tables (`application_metadata`, `application_key`) that already exist inside a constructed application's schema before any builder-defined content exists. This document adds structures *inside* that same schema; it does not redefine the schema boundary itself (§10).
- **The construction action, the configuration model, or the closed construct/binding-kind vocabulary's own conceptual shape.** `01-application-construction-design.md` owns all three in full. This document represents constructs and bindings physically, where its own mechanism is capable of doing so (§3.3), but does not redecide what a construct or a binding *is*, and does not enumerate the vocabulary of kinds — a determination made explicitly, not by default, in §3.4.
- **Which invariant runs which check, where each check runs, or what a violation looks like at the point of halt.** `01-invariant-enforcement-design.md` fixes all of this for INV-04 already; this document supplies what that check inspects for builder-defined data, never a second check location (§4.3).
- **Tenant isolation, connection-scoping, or authentication.** `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` and `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` own both in full; every mechanism this document designs runs inside the identity and connection those documents already establish (§11).
- **Region-of-record on an actor, tenant, or application; the AI-suggested/builder-approved provenance attribute; the numeric retention ceiling; audit-event record shape, storage, or tamper-evidence.** Each is explicitly discharged to, or declined toward, the document that owns it (§9, §14).
- **The generic administrative interface (C-27), process and workflow modeling (C-18), the cross-system data layer (C-24), or AI-assisted builder tooling (C-19).** Each is a distinct, later document's concern, building on this one; none is designed here. This document does, however, own the physical catalog realizing a process definition's identity, version, and step graph, and the instance anchor tracking an in-flight process instance's lifecycle (§3.5) — a representability obligation only, exactly as it owns representing a construct without owning C-06's configuration model (§3.3); the process model itself, its four boundary-crossing step kinds, and the adopted engine's own execution semantics remain `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md`'s in full.
- **Which records an end-user role may reach through a construct that presents entity content.** `01-application-construction-design.md` owns the access-binding vocabulary (View, Invoke) and the behavioral-binding mechanism this document's fourth Gateway check (§4.4) reuses; this document does not redesign either, and does not decide how a Surface's presented content is bound to the entity data underlying it in the first place — a distinct, still-open question named and left open at §9.6.

## 3. Entity and Schema Definition — the Physical Mechanism

### 3.1 Where Builder-Defined Structures Are Stored

Every builder-defined entity, schema, and relationship exists inside the per-application schema `tenant_<tenant_id>_app_<application_id>` that `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.3 already fixes, alongside — never in place of — that document's two platform-owned tables. This document adds three catalog structures and, per entity, one physical instance table, all within that same schema boundary; it opens no new schema, no new isolation mechanism, and no structure outside the boundary already fixed there.

### 3.2 The Schema Catalog — the Five Definitional Elements, Physically Represented

`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §4 fixes that a schema definition must establish Structure, Constraints, Relationships, Identity, and a Version marker, and that a schema is complete only once all five are established. This document realizes each as a catalog row, never as a convention a consuming component must remember to honor:

| Catalog | Realizes | Key Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `entity_catalog` | The entity itself, as a named, addressable unit within the application. | `entity_id` (UUIDv7, PK); `application_id`; `name`; `current_schema_version_id`; `created_at`. |
| `schema_version_catalog` | The **Version marker** element, and the anchor every migration (§6) and backward-compatibility determination (§7) is evaluated against. | `schema_version_id` (UUIDv7, PK); `entity_id` (FK); `version_marker` (monotonic per entity); `created_at`; `backward_compatible_with_prior` (the §7 determination, recorded at the moment this version is proposed). |
| `field_catalog` | The **Structure** and **Constraints** elements — the attributes an instance holds and the shape and conditions each must satisfy — and the **Identity** element, where one or more fields are flagged as identity-bearing. | `field_id` (PK); `schema_version_id` (FK); `name`; `type`; `constraint_definition`; `identity_role` (boolean); `data_governance_category`; `classification_tier`; `consent_basis_relationship_id` (nullable FK → `relationship_catalog`, §9.2). |
| `relationship_catalog` | The **Relationships** element — how the entity relates to another, and what referential integrity that relationship requires (§5). | `relationship_id` (PK); `schema_version_id` (FK, the referencing entity's own version); `target_entity_id` (FK → `entity_catalog`); `cardinality`; `on_change_behavior` (resolve or refuse, §5); `kind` (§3.3–§3.4). |

**A schema is complete, and governs no data, until every row required by all four catalogs exists for it.** `field_catalog` must carry at least one identity-bearing field before `schema_version_catalog`'s row is eligible to become an entity's `current_schema_version_id`; an entity with an incomplete definition accepts no instance write — the deny-by-default rule the specification's own §3 fixes for data integrity, applied here to definitional completeness itself.

### 3.3 Constructs and Bindings, Represented Through the Same Mechanism

`01-application-construction-design.md` §6 constrains this document to make its physical structures capable of representing, without loss: a construct and its kind; the structural bindings that compose constructs into an application; the behavioral bindings attached to a construct; and the access bindings mapping a tenant role to a construct and a bounded action class. This document discharges that constraint by reuse, not by a second mechanism: a **construct** is represented as an entity instance whose governing schema's `entity_catalog` row is flagged as build-surface content, with its `kind` drawn from whatever closed vocabulary governs it (§3.4); a **structural, behavioral, or access binding** is represented as a `relationship_catalog` row whose `kind` column takes one of the three binding-kind values that same vocabulary fixes.

This is a reuse of the entity/schema/relationship mechanism §3.2 already defines, not a parallel structure invented for build-surface content specifically — the same discipline every document in this layer and the one before it applies when a new requirement can be met by extending an existing mechanism rather than building a second one. This document does not thereby assert that "construct" is a synonym for "entity," or that "binding" is a synonym for "relationship": canonical naming remains `03-software-and-architecture/02-domain-glossary.md`'s to fix, per `01-application-construction-design.md` §2's own working-term caveat. What this document fixes is only that the physical mechanism serving one serves the other without a second one existing.

### 3.4 The Construct/Binding-Kind Enumeration — an Explicit Determination

`01-application-construction-design.md` §4.2–§4.3 fixes that the vocabulary of construct and binding kinds is a closed, platform-owned enumeration a builder instantiates but never extends, and defers naming its concrete members here, on the reasoning that the members depend on how they are physically represented. `BACKLOG.md` §1f identifies this deferral's destination as wrong, and this document makes the determination that item calls for, rather than proceeding as though the question were settled.

**This document declines ownership of the enumeration, and states why.**

- **Representation is not enumeration.** Knowing how a kind is stored — as a `kind` column value on `entity_catalog` or `relationship_catalog`, per §3.3 — does not determine which kind values exist. §3.2–§3.3 already discharge the representation question in full, independent of what the vocabulary's concrete members turn out to be; nothing about the storage design above requires the members to be named to be built.
- **The dependency runs the wrong way, and enumerating here would not fix that.** `implementation-document-map.md` fixes that this document depends on `01-application-construction-design.md`, not the reverse. A document cannot resolve, on its upstream dependency's behalf, a question that dependency itself left open — doing so here would mean this document is retroactively deciding part of what H19 already fixed as closed and platform-owned (§4.2 there), which is a decision about the *build surface's own configuration model*, not about a structure a builder defines. That is the opposite kind of artifact from what this document owns (`implementation-document-map.md`'s own charge for this row: "builder-defined entities, schemas, relationships... structures a builder defines").
- **The map does not assign it here.** This document's charge is structures a builder defines; a fixed, finite, platform-owned enumeration that a builder only ever instantiates and never extends is, by `01-application-construction-design.md` §4.2's own definition, not builder-defined content at all.

**This document declines the enumeration to `01-application-construction-design.md`, by targeted amendment, not to a third document.** `BACKLOG.md` §1f names this as the available alternative if the enumeration's destination is reconsidered: "if the lead prefers it stay with the construction document, that is a one-section amendment to `01-application-construction-design.md` instead." This document takes that alternative as the correct resolution — the vocabulary is platform-owned, closed, and conceptual in exactly the register §4.1–§4.2 of that document already occupies; naming its concrete members is a continuation of that document's own §4.3, not a new document's obligation. This document does not perform that amendment itself — amending another document's content is outside this ticket's scope, and outside what a design document may do to a sibling design document without explicit direction (`PROCESS.md` §3) — it states the determination and the destination so the deferral is claimed rather than left dangling, exactly as `BACKLOG.md` §1f asks.

**What this document holds, regardless of where the enumeration lands.** §3.3's representation stands independent of this determination: whichever document eventually names the concrete kind values, `entity_catalog.kind` and `relationship_catalog.kind` are the columns those values populate, and no change to this document's own schema is required when that enumeration is finally written.

### 3.5 The Process-Definition Catalog and Instance Anchor

`04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §4.1 binds this document to represent, without loss: a process definition's own identity and version marker; its step graph, including each step's kind and, for an interaction or action step, the opaque construct reference it carries; and a determination — analogous to §7's own backward-compatibility determination — of whether a new version leaves every in-flight instance of the prior version able to continue (§8.1 there). It additionally requires an **instance anchor record** (identity, version binding, lifecycle status) that never depends on reading the adopted engine's own internal state (§4.2 there). That document explicitly declines to design the physical structures itself, on the identical constrain-without-owning precedent its own §4.1 cites from `01-application-construction-design.md` §6 — the same precedent §3.3 above already follows for constructs and bindings.

**This is not a second instance of §3.2's mechanism, and is not forced into it.** `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §11's own Alternatives Considered already examined, and rejected, extending the entity/relationship mechanism to represent a process definition: doing so "would require widening a vocabulary that document has already closed against exactly this content" (`01-application-construction-design.md` §4.2.2 excludes a process's shape from the construct-kind vocabulary by name), and a process's own runtime notion of "currently at this step" has no field a static entity/relationship pair can hold. This document takes that rejection as settled and discharges the constraint through a **distinct catalog family**, physically separate from §3.2's four entity catalogs but inside the identical per-application schema §3.1 already fixes — never a schema this document does not own, and never a widening of `entity_catalog` or `relationship_catalog` to content they were already found unable to correctly represent.

| Catalog | Realizes | Key Columns |
|---|---|---|
| `process_definition_catalog` | The process definition's own identity, within the application. | `process_definition_id` (UUIDv7, PK); `application_id`; `name`; `current_version_id`; `created_at`. |
| `process_definition_version_catalog` | The **version marker**; the complete step graph, stored as an opaque, engine-consumed document; and the in-flight-continuation determination `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §8.1 requires. | `version_id` (UUIDv7, PK); `process_definition_id` (FK); `version_marker` (monotonic per definition); `graph_definition` (the complete, BPMN-conformant graph content — stored and remaining authoritative here, per that document's §4.1, but never parsed or interpreted by this document beyond the step extraction below); `in_flight_compatible_with_prior` (the §8.1 determination, recorded at the moment this version is proposed); `created_at`. |
| `process_step_catalog` | The closed set of four boundary-crossing step kinds `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §3.2 already fixes, and, for an interaction or action step, the opaque reference to the Surface or Command construct it invokes — extracted from `graph_definition` at authoring time so that the Entity Access Gateway (§4.4) and the access-binding model reach a step's kind or construct reference without parsing the engine-specific graph format. | `step_id` (PK); `version_id` (FK); `kind` (Interaction / Action / Automated-rule / Human-task — the closed vocabulary that document's §3.2 already fixes, never widened here); `construct_reference` (nullable FK → `entity_catalog.entity_id`, populated only for an Interaction or Action step — an ordinary same-schema foreign key, because a construct's own `entity_catalog` row already lives in this same per-application schema, §3.3). |
| `process_instance_anchor` | The instance anchor `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §4.2 requires: an instance's identity, its version binding, and its lifecycle status, independent of the adopted engine's own internal execution state. | `instance_id` (UUIDv7, PK); `process_definition_id` (FK); `version_id` (FK → `process_definition_version_catalog` — the version this specific instance is bound to for its entire remaining execution, per that document's §8.2); `lifecycle_status` (running / completed / terminated); `correlated_record_reference` (nullable, an opaque reference to the builder-defined record or records the instance operates over, where one exists); `started_at`; `ended_at`. |

**This anchor never depends on reading the engine's own internal state, and is never a second authoritative store for anything this document or `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` already holds authoritative elsewhere.** `graph_definition` and the process definition's own identity are authoritative here, on this schema, exactly as that document's §4.1 requires; `process_instance_anchor` is authoritative only for the facts it names — identity, version binding, lifecycle status, and the correlated-record reference — never for an instance's own running variables, its current step, or any timer state, which remain the adopted engine's own execution model (`04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §4.2, §5.1) and are represented in no column above.

**Version retirement is a query over this catalog, never a new mechanism.** `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §8.2 fixes that "a version is retired only once no instance remains bound to it," determined by "querying every instance's own recorded version binding and lifecycle status" — exactly `process_instance_anchor.version_id` and `.lifecycle_status` above, requiring no additional structure.

**The in-flight-continuation determination is recorded once, on the version row, on the identical discipline already fixed for the analogous schema determination.** This document does not design how the determination is computed — `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §8.1 fixes that the platform, not the engine, makes it, and states its own deny-by-default treatment of uncertainty; this section supplies only the column the recorded outcome populates, the same discipline `field_catalog.classification_tier` and `schema_version_catalog.backward_compatible_with_prior` already establish for a determination made and recorded once, never re-derived downstream.

## 4. The Validation Contract and INV-04's Particulars for Builder-Defined Data

### 4.1 The Entity Access Gateway — the Single Mediation Point

No existing mechanism in the design library mediates every read and write of builder-defined entity data the way the Registry Accessor mediates every read of the platform registry (`02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §3.1) or the connection-acquisition function mediates every scoped connection (that document's §4.2). This document creates that mediation point — the **Entity Access Gateway** — as the sole path through which any component reads or writes a builder-defined entity's catalog rows or instance data, inside a connection `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §5.1 has already scoped (§11 below).

The Gateway is the single chokepoint this document runs every check named below through, in fixed order, never as parallel or independently-reachable gateways:

1. **The Validation Engine** (§4.2) — INV-04's particulars for builder-defined data.
2. **The Consent and Minimization Check**, where the access touches personal or sensitive data (§9.3) — the write-mediation gateway `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §5.2 required and named as not yet existing.
3. **Classification-tier and data-governance-category resolution** (§9.1–§9.2), folded into the connection's already-resolved obligations, never re-derived by a second mechanism.
4. **The End-User Record-Reach Check** (§4.4), where the invoking identity is a resolved end-user role reaching entity content through a construct — the destination `05-api-contract-design.md` §11 names for the still-open half of `BACKLOG.md` §1h.

**This is the single, reused location this document's own storage-shape obligations discharge through**, not several separately-invoked checks at several separately-reachable points. A component that wants to read or write a builder-defined entity has no function available to it that bypasses the Gateway — the same discipline `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §4.2 already applies to connection acquisition, extended here one layer deeper, to the entity itself.

### 4.2 What the Validation Engine Inspects, Concretely

`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §3 fixes that committed data must be valid, consistent, and referentially correct, together and at once. The Validation Engine, invoked inside the Gateway before any write is committed, inspects each concretely:

| Property | What the Engine Inspects |
|---|---|
| Validity | The candidate instance's field values against the entity's `current_schema_version_id`'s own `field_catalog` rows — every field's `constraint_definition` evaluated against the candidate value, and every identity-bearing field present and unique within the scope its own constraint fixes. |
| Consistency | Whether the candidate instance contradicts other committed data governed by the same schema version or the same relationship — evaluated against the entity's own current instances and any instance a `relationship_catalog` row binds it to. |
| Referential correctness | Whether every reference the candidate instance holds — via a `relationship_catalog` row — resolves to a target instance that exists and itself currently satisfies its own governing schema (§5). |

**A write that fails any one of the three does not proceed.** This is the specification's own §3 rule, applied here as the Gateway's own refusal path — never a partial commit, never a commit "corrected" afterward. Where the Engine cannot establish that a candidate instance satisfies all three, the write is refused, per the specification's own §5 deny-by-default rule.

**Definitional and instance validation share the Gateway, not two mechanisms.** A schema-definition or schema-evolution action (a new `schema_version_catalog`, `field_catalog`, or `relationship_catalog` row) passes through the identical Gateway, with the Validation Engine's inspection turned toward §3.2's own completeness rule and §7's backward-compatibility determination rather than toward an instance's field values — one mediation point, two modes of the same inspection discipline, never a second gateway for catalog writes.

### 4.3 Composition with `01-invariant-enforcement-design.md`'s Fixed Check Location

`01-invariant-enforcement-design.md` §5.2 already fixes that INV-04's check runs at every commit boundary, at runtime, before data enters the committed state, and at every migration's execution, in the pipeline — and that this document owns what the check inspects for builder-defined data. This document does not relocate that check: the Entity Access Gateway's Validation Engine (§4.2) **is** the runtime commit-boundary inspection that fixed location already anticipates; §6 below is the pipeline-time inspection for a migration. No second check location is introduced, and the halt-and-escalate behavior on a violation is exactly the one `01-invariant-enforcement-design.md` §3.3, §5.2 already fixes — this document supplies the inspection content, never a second halt mechanism.

### 4.4 The Fourth Check — End-User Role-Scoped Record Reach

`05-api-contract-design.md` §11 assessed the still-open half of `BACKLOG.md` §1h — which records an end-user role may reach through a Surface presenting entity content — and declined to resolve it at the contract layer, on the grounds that "by the time a response reaches the contract layer... the Gateway has already bounded what content it may carry." It names this Gateway as the correct destination for a fourth, composing check, on the identical "compose with what exists" discipline `03-data-administration-design.md` §5.4 already applied for its own upstream check. This section is that check.

**What it inspects, and when.** Where the invoking identity is a resolved **end-user role** (`02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §5's end-user personas) reaching a builder-defined entity's content **through a construct** — an already-configured Surface presenting the content, or a Command whose invocation an end-user role triggers, including a human task step's completion (§9.7) — the End-User Record-Reach Check evaluates every `relationship_catalog` row of `kind = Behavioral` (§3.3) attached to that construct whose condition-and-parameter content expresses a record-level narrowing, against the specific record the invocation would read or write. Where a narrowing condition is present and is not satisfied, the access is refused; where none is present, the reach is bounded only by whatever the construct's own configuration and its View/Invoke access binding already expose — the builder's own choice not to narrow further, never a gap this check invents a restriction to fill.

**Why no new grant structure.** `01-application-construction-design.md` §4.1 already defines a behavioral binding as "a generic condition-and-parameter shape attached to a construct, governing what it does," and §4.2.4 already models the identical composition — a condition composed with an access binding — for gating a construct's own invocation. A record-level narrowing on what a construct's own reach returns is the same generic shape applied to a different effect, not a new kind of binding: this document consumes that already-licensed generality, it does not reopen `01-application-construction-design.md`'s closed vocabulary or invent a fifth mediation structure alongside the four §3.2 already fixes.

**Position in the fixed order.** This check runs last (§4.1, item 4), after validity, consent, and classification are already resolved, because it narrows a result already otherwise cleared rather than gating whether the Gateway is entered at all — appending it leaves the first three checks' own fixed order, and every already-recorded finding under it, undisturbed.

**"Engine-originated," "administrative," and "construction-time" each confer no exemption, and neither does the reverse.** Every other caller of the Gateway — the Validation Engine, the Consent and Minimization Check, and classification resolution — applies identically regardless of which caller invokes the Gateway (§4.1); this fourth check adds no exception to that rule for any caller, and, symmetrically, evaluates only where its own precondition — a resolved end-user role, reaching content through a construct — actually holds. §9.7 resolves the one caller this precondition leaves genuinely ambiguous: a process-automated step's own Gateway call.

**What this section does not decide.** How a Surface's presented content is bound to the entity data underlying it in the first place remains the distinct, unowned question `03-data-administration-design.md` §5.5 names (§9.6) — this check presumes that binding exists, however it is eventually designed, and narrows what it returns; it does not itself establish that a Surface presents entity content at all.

## 5. Relationships and Referential Integrity

`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §6 fixes that every reference resolves, none is left dangling, a dependent removal or change does not proceed unresolved, and this holds across a relationship's full lifecycle — creation, change to either end, and removal.

- **A relationship is enforced at the Gateway, not by a native foreign-key constraint alone.** Because a builder-defined entity's instances are versioned rows (§8), a native database foreign key would bind to one physical version row rather than to the logical record a relationship actually targets, and could not, on its own, express that a reference must remain resolvable across supersession. The Validation Engine (§4.2) therefore resolves every `relationship_catalog` reference against the target entity's own *current* instance at evaluation time, on every write to either end of the relationship — a stronger guarantee than a native constraint would give against this document's own versioned physical shape, not a weaker substitute for one.
- **A dependent removal or change is resolved or refused, never left dangling.** Where a write would supersede or mark-not-current an instance another committed instance still references, the Gateway requires that dependency be resolved — by redirecting the reference, by cascading the change under the relationship's own `on_change_behavior`, or by refusing the write outright — before the write proceeds. Uncertainty about whether a dependency is resolved resolves to refusal, per the specification's own §5 rule applied here to relationships specifically.
- **No relationship resolves across a tenant boundary.** Every `relationship_catalog.target_entity_id` is constrained, at definition time, to an `entity_catalog` row within the same application's own schema — there is no column through which a relationship could name an entity outside the schema boundary `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.3 already fixes, which is itself confined to one tenant. This composes with, and does not duplicate, INV-01's own enforcement (`02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §3–§5): the relationship mechanism has no naming path to a foreign tenant's schema at all, the same structural absence that document's §8 already establishes for every other cross-tenant path in this design.

## 6. Migration-Safety

`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §7 defines a migration as a change to a schema's definition, or to data already committed under it, applied after instances already exist under the prior definition.

- **A migration is a new `schema_version_catalog` row plus, where the row's own `backward_compatible_with_prior` determination (§7 below) is false, a transformation of existing instances into conformance with it.** Where the determination is true, no transformation of existing instances occurs at all — every existing version row remains valid exactly as committed, under its own recorded `schema_version_id`, and only new writes are evaluated against the new version (§7). A migration in the backward-incompatible sense exists only where existing instances must actually be rewritten to remain valid.
- **A migration is all-or-nothing.** Where existing instances must be transformed, the transformation runs as a single unit with respect to validity: either every affected instance is rewritten into a new version row conforming to the new schema and the migration completes, or none is, and every affected instance remains exactly as it was under its prior schema version. No migration this mechanism performs leaves an instance satisfying neither its prior nor its new schema.
- **A migration preserves referential integrity throughout.** Every reference a migrated instance holds or is held by is re-evaluated by the Validation Engine (§5) as part of the same unit of work the migration itself is; a migration that would leave a reference dangling, even transiently, does not complete.
- **A reversible path is a structural consequence of §8's own append-only shape, not a separate mechanism.** Because no instance write is ever a destructive update — every version, including one written as part of a migration, is a new row alongside, never in place of, what it supersedes (§8) — the prior valid state remains physically present and addressable by its own `schema_version_id` for as long as the retention rules `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md` set for it, satisfying INV-08's reversible-path requirement as a property this document's own storage shape already carries, not as a second mechanism built to satisfy it.
- **A migration affecting one tenant's or one application's data never touches, depends on, or requires access to another's.** Every migration runs inside the connection `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §5.1 already scopes to the single schema the migration targets; no migration mechanism this document designs opens a second connection to a different tenant's or application's schema.
- **Numeric duration, downtime, and resource ceilings on a migration are `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`'s, cited and never restated here.** This document fixes the conditions under which a migration may proceed at all, never the thresholds by which its performance is judged.

## 7. Backward-Compatibility for Stored Data

`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §8 fixes that stored data remains valid, accessible, and correctly related under schema evolution except along a managed, announced path, and that determining whether a given evolution is backward-compatible is this document's own to make.

- **The determination is made once, at the moment a new `schema_version_catalog` row is proposed**, by the Validation Engine operating in its definitional mode (§4.2): whether every instance already committed under every prior `schema_version_id` for this entity remains valid, accessible, and correctly related without being rewritten under the proposed version. The result is recorded on the new row's own `backward_compatible_with_prior` column — never inferred later, never left for a downstream consumer to re-derive.
- **A determination of `true` permits the evolution to proceed as an ordinary change.** No existing instance is rewritten; new writes are evaluated against the new version; §6's migration mechanism is not invoked, because nothing already committed requires transformation.
- **A determination of `false`, or one the Engine cannot establish with confidence, is treated as backward-incompatible.** The evolution does not proceed as an ordinary schema-version row; it follows the managed, announced path `05-meta-operations/08-change-management-and-evolution-policy.md` and gate G-6 own in full — this document fixes only the determination and the point it is made, never the announcement, scheduling, or human-review mechanics of that path.
- **Uncertainty resolves to backward-incompatible, never the reverse.** Where the Engine cannot establish that every already-committed instance remains valid, accessible, and correctly related under a proposed version, the proposal is refused the ordinary path and routed to the managed one — the specification's own §8 deny-by-default rule, applied here at the exact point the determination is made.

## 8. Temporal Integrity and History

`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §9 fixes effective dating, append-only retention, and full history as a generic, always-on obligation of every builder-defined entity, distinct from the audit trail and never a synchronization mechanism.

### 8.1 The Physical Shape

Every builder-defined entity's instances live in one physical table inside the application's own schema, structured so that every write is an insert, never an update or a delete of a prior row:

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `version_id` | UUIDv7, primary key — unique per version row. |
| `record_id` | UUIDv7, assigned once when a logical record is first created and carried unchanged through every subsequent version of it — the stable identity §9 requires be reconstructable across a record's full history. |
| `schema_version_id` | FK → `schema_version_catalog` — which schema version this specific version row conforms to. |
| `effective_from` | When the values this version holds are true as of — distinct from `recorded_at`, realizing the specification's own "effective dating is distinct from the write." |
| `recorded_at` | When this version was actually written. |
| `superseded_by` | Nullable, self-referencing `version_id` — the version that supersedes this one, where one exists. |
| one column per builder-defined field | Typed and constrained per the governing `field_catalog` rows (§3.2). |

- **No write ever updates or deletes an existing version row.** A change to a record is realized as a new row sharing the prior row's `record_id`, with the prior row's `superseded_by` set to the new row's `version_id` — the row itself, and every field value it held, remains exactly as committed. "Current" state is the row, per `record_id`, whose `superseded_by` is null; this document does not require a separate materialized pointer, though nothing here forbids one as a performance optimization the physical layer may add without changing this shape.
- **Full history is reconstructable by construction, not by configuration.** Every prior version of a record, and the effective date each held, is directly queryable by `record_id` the moment the entity exists — no builder action enables or configures this, and none can disable it, consistent with the specification's own "auditable by default, not by configuration."
- **This is not the audit trail.** This table records what a builder-defined record's own data *was*; it carries no actor attribution, no tamper-evidence mechanism, and is never consulted in place of `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md`'s own record of what the platform or an actor *did* — the two boundaries the specification's own §9 already draws, held here without restatement.
- **This is not synchronization machinery.** No column above exists for conflict resolution or offline-sync purposes; `superseded_by` is a history pointer, never a tombstone in the sync sense `01-technology-stack-design.md` §21 and `DECISIONS.md` D-11 already exclude from this design.
- **Numeric retention on how long history is kept is `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`'s, cited and never restated here.**

### 8.2 Field-Level Erasure Across Every Retained Version

`07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §4.3 requires that a deletion or de-identification obligation reaching a builder-defined entity's personal-data fields be satisfied across **every retained version, current and historical alike** — not the current version alone — and states this as a binding requirement on this document's own design of the history mechanism.

- **Erasure is a distinct write path, targeting content, never a record's existence or its place in history.** Where a field's `data_governance_category` (§9.2) obligates erasure, the mechanism overwrites that field's stored value, in place, on every version row sharing the affected `record_id` — every `version_id`, every `effective_from`, every `superseded_by` link, and every non-personal field's own value remain exactly as committed. Nothing about this operation removes a row, breaks the `record_id` chain, or shortens the record's own reconstructable history; only the erasable field's content changes, and only where the erasure obligation requires it.
- **This does not contradict §8.1's append-only rule, because the specification itself carves out exactly this case.** `03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §9 states directly that the history obligation "meets, and does not override, the erasure rules `02-governance-and-security/06-data-governance-and-privacy.md` owns," and that this document "owns the structure; the privacy document owns whether, when, and how a record is erased." The retained *shape* of history — that a version existed, when, and in what sequence — is what §8.1 protects; the *content* of a specific field a governing document obligates to be erasable is what this mechanism discharges, and the two are not the same guarantee.
- **The audit trail's own reference to the record remains resolvable, unaffected.** `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §7 already resolves the immutability/erasure tension by holding that an audit event's own reference to an entity is an opaque identifier, never the entity's own content, and that erasure "targets the referenced entity's own stored content" without ever needing to reach the audit trail's reference to it. Because this mechanism never touches `record_id` or `version_id` — only field content — every audit event referencing either identifier continues to resolve after an erasure, exactly as that document's own resolution requires.
- **This mechanism does not itself decide whether, when, or how erasure is triggered.** `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md`'s Retention Sweep and consent-withdrawal path (§4.2–§5.3 there) own the triggering condition in full; this section fixes only that, once triggered, the mechanism reaches every retained version, not the current one alone.

This document discharges that destination at §4.4: a fourth check, composing with the existing three in a stated position (last), evaluating whatever behavioral binding `01-application-construction-design.md` §4.1 already lets a builder attach to a construct, where that binding's condition-and-parameter content expresses a record-level narrowing and the invoking identity is a resolved end-user role reaching entity content through that construct.

**This closes `BACKLOG.md` §1h in full** — both the professional-builder half ADR-030 already closed, and the end-user half `§1h-destination` named as claimed but not yet realized.

**The two halves compose without overlapping or contradicting, because they run at different points against different actor classes.** `03-data-administration-design.md` §5.4's Administrative Authorization Check runs *upstream* of the Gateway, gating a professional-builder actor's own direct reach through the C-27 interface — an interface that excludes every construct-flagged `entity_catalog` row by construction (that document's §3.3) and so never invokes a construct at all. The fourth check here runs *inside* the Gateway, at its end, and its own precondition (§4.4) requires a resolved *end-user* role reaching content *through a construct* — the professional-builder actor §5.4 governs is never that precondition's subject, and the construct-mediated reach this check governs is never §5.4's. Neither check evaluates the other's caller or the other's trigger condition; each is silent where the other applies.

**What remains genuinely open, stated honestly.** `03-data-administration-design.md` §5.5's own framing names a more foundational, still-unowned mechanism: how a Surface's presented content is bound to the entity data underlying it in the first place. This document does not design that binding — doing so would be `01-application-construction-design.md`'s own configuration-model concern, or a future document's, not this one's. §4.4's check presumes that binding exists, however it is eventually designed, and narrows what it presents; it does not itself establish that a Surface presents entity content at all.

### 9.7 Composition: Process-Automated Gateway Calls and the Fourth Check

The two obligations above meet at exactly one point, which is why they were discharged together rather than independently. `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §6 fixes that an automated rule step reaches builder-defined entity data as "a caller of the Entity Access Gateway... on identical terms to any other caller," and that document's §5.2 fixes that "no engine-originated identity" ever substitutes for the Authenticated Actor Identity already resolved for a request. Neither statement, on its own, says whose reach the fourth check (§4.4) evaluates when a process step — not a live end-user request — is what invokes the Gateway. This section resolves it.

**The fourth check applies only where its own precondition holds — a resolved end-user role reaching content through a construct — and an automated rule step never satisfies that precondition.** `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §3.2 fixes an automated rule step as one that evaluates "a condition or executes logic against builder-defined entity data, without human involvement" — by definition, no end-user role is the invoking identity, whether the step is triggered by the engine's own timer, its own scheduling loop, or an earlier step in the same instance. The fourth check's precondition is therefore never met for an automated rule step's own Gateway calls, on the identical conditional-applicability discipline the Consent and Minimization Check already establishes for itself ("where the access touches personal or sensitive data," §4.1) — this is an application of an existing pattern to a caller this document had not yet considered, not a new carve-out invented for it.

**An interaction step, and an action step whose Command an end-user role invokes — including a human task step's completion — do satisfy the precondition, and the fourth check runs exactly as it would for any other construct-mediated end-user reach.** `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §7 already fixes that completing a human task step "is, mechanically, an ordinary Invoke on the Command or Surface construct the step references" — the fourth check composes with that Invoke on identical terms to any other, because a process step contributes no exemption and no privileged path, the same "engine-originated confers no exemption" discipline that document's §5.2 and §6 already state for the other three checks.

**This is not a gap a builder can route around.** An automated rule step's own authority to reach entity data derives from the process definition's own construction-time authoring — a builder-level act, checked when the definition or its version is authored (§9.5, `process-definition-authoring`) — never a run-time impersonation of an end-user's own reach. The other three Gateway checks (Validation Engine, Consent and Minimization, classification/category resolution) apply to an automated rule step's calls exactly as to any other caller, unaffected by this section; only the fourth, end-user-specific check is inapplicable to it, because there is no end-user role in play for that check to evaluate.

Reversing or materially changing any of the three does not merely change code: every already-committed instance, and every historical version already retained beneath it, is stored against this shape, and a schema-shape reversal requires migrating that entire stored history under a corrected model rather than changing a check or a policy going forward. This is the specification's own most expensive rung (`03-software-and-architecture/03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md`'s own INV-04/INV-08/INV-09 elaboration exists specifically because this shape, once populated, is this costly to move), and is stated honestly at that grade rather than understated to look cheaper than the document it is the design-phase counterpart to (`02-platform-data-model-design.md`, graded identically for the platform's own equivalent decision).
  - **Amended, `BACKLOG.md` §1j (process-definition catalog and instance anchor added).** `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §4.1 bound this document to a representability constraint for a process definition's identity, version, and step graph, and for an instance anchor independent of the adopted engine's own state, declining to design the physical structures itself on the same constrain-without-owning precedent this decision's own §3.3 already follows for constructs and bindings. §3.5 discharges that constraint: a distinct catalog family — `process_definition_catalog`, `process_definition_version_catalog`, `process_step_catalog`, `process_instance_anchor` — inside the identical per-application schema this decision already fixes, never inside a schema `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` owns (it has none) and never by widening the four entity catalogs this decision already fixed to content that document's own §11 Alternatives Considered found they cannot correctly hold. ADR-021 through ADR-027 remain Provisional and are not assumed settled; this decision depends on none of their approval status.
- **Verified vs. reasoned:** Reasoned throughout. No time-sensitive ecosystem, product, or vendor claim is made; every finding derives from the cited specification and the cited, already-written upstream design documents' own content.
- **Question this answers:** Given a platform that must let a builder define an entity and a schema for any subject matter, with no domain presumed, and given that entity data must survive migration and evolution without breaking a guarantee already made to it, carry effective dating and full history generically, and remain purgeable of personal-data content on demand across every retained version — what is the smallest physical mechanism that realizes all of this, mediates every read and write of it through one reused chokepoint rather than several, and correctly resolves the storage-shape obligations five upstream documents already bound here, including the one this document is positioned to decline?
- **Criteria applied, and how each resolved:**
  1. *One physical table per entity, versioned by insert-only rows, versus a shared, generic value store.* Decisive for one table per entity — a shared generic (entity-attribute-value) store cannot carry native, typed constraints per field, complicates the referential-integrity check §5 requires, and gives the typed query builder `01-technology-stack-design.md` §14.4 already commits to nothing to compose against; a table-per-entity design, generated from the catalog at definition time, keeps every later mechanism (validation, referential integrity, migration, erasure) operating on ordinary typed columns.
  2. *One reused mediation point (the Entity Access Gateway) for validation, consent/minimization, and classification/category resolution, versus separate, independently-reachable checks.* Decisive for one Gateway — the discipline every document in Layers 2–3 has applied consistently (the Region Boundary Check inline in connection acquisition; the Consent and Minimization Check's own required structural placement, `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §5.2); a component with more than one path to builder-defined data is a path some check could be bypassed through.
  3. *Field-level erasure in place versus row-level or version-level removal.* Decisive for field-level, in place — `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §4.3 requires purging personal-data content from every retained version, not removing the version itself, and removing a version row would break the `record_id` chain §8.1's own reconstructability guarantee depends on and the audit trail's own reference to that version (`08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §7).
  4. *Decline the construct/binding-kind enumeration, and decline the provenance attribute, versus accepting both.* Decisive for declining both — `BACKLOG.md` §1f's own analysis (representation is not enumeration; the dependency runs the wrong way) applies with equal force to the enumeration, and the provenance attribute describes tooling-internal suggestion state, not builder-defined domain content, so accepting either would store platform-owned or tooling-internal content inside a mechanism built for builder-defined content specifically — a category error this decision avoids by declining, with grounds, rather than silently absorbing.
- **Context:** This is the second consolidation-shaped ticket of the design phase, after `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md`'s reconciliation of twenty-two inherited evidence rows. This document reconciles four-to-five inherited storage-shape obligations into one entity model, on the same discipline: no obligation is silently dropped, and where one cannot be discharged as handed, that is stated explicitly, with the destination named (§9).
- **Decision:** (1) Every builder-defined entity is realized as one physical, insert-only, versioned table per entity inside the application's own schema, keyed by a stable `record_id` and a per-version `version_id`, with `superseded_by` carrying supersession and no column ever updated or deleted (§8.1). (2) A four-catalog structure (`entity_catalog`, `schema_version_catalog`, `field_catalog`, `relationship_catalog`) physically represents the specification's five definitional elements, and represents constructs and bindings through the identical mechanism rather than a second one (§3.2–§3.3). (3) The Entity Access Gateway is the single, reused mediation point for the Validation Engine, the Consent and Minimization Check, and classification-tier/data-governance-category resolution — never three separately-reachable checks (§4.1, §9). (4) The construct/binding-kind enumeration is declined to `01-application-construction-design.md`, by targeted amendment, on the grounds that it is platform-owned, closed, conceptual content — the opposite kind of artifact from what this document owns (§3.4). (5) The provenance attribute is declined to `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`, on the grounds that it describes tooling-internal suggestion state, not builder-defined domain content (§9.4). (6) Region-of-record on a tenant or application is declined to `02-platform-data-model-design.md`, stated as an open obligation on that document's own schema, not discharged here (§9.1). (7) Personal-data erasure reaches every retained version's field content, in place, never a version's existence or its history-chain identifiers (§8.2).
- **Alternatives considered:** *A shared, generic entity-attribute-value store instead of one table per entity* — rejected under criterion 1; it would weaken every downstream mechanism this document also owns. *Three or more independently-reachable checks instead of one Gateway* — rejected under criterion 2; a defect bypassing one check would leave the others intact but the property none of them alone guarantees (that every access clears all three) false. *Row-level or version-level deletion for erasure* — rejected under criterion 3; it breaks reconstructability and the audit trail's own reference discipline. *Accepting the construct/binding-kind enumeration here, or accepting the provenance attribute here* — both rejected under criterion 4, as a category error importing platform-owned or tooling-internal content into a mechanism built for builder-defined content.
- **Consequences:** Binds `03-data-administration-design.md` and `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` to read and write builder-defined entity data only through the Entity Access Gateway, never through a second access path of their own (`implementation-document-map.md`'s own dependency listing). Binds `01-application-construction-design.md` to receive, by targeted amendment and on the grounds stated in §3.4, the construct/binding-kind enumeration this document explicitly declines. Binds `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` to fix the provenance attribute's own storage shape, per §9.4. Binds `02-platform-data-model-design.md` to close the region-of-record obligation on its own schema, per §9.1 — an obligation this document states as open, not as discharged on that document's behalf. Adds no obligation to `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md`'s record shape, storage, or tamper-evidence mechanism — only three new `event_type` values within its existing model (§12), and concrete integration for two event types that document already owns. Adds no obligation to `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` or `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` — both are consumed exactly as already designed, never extended. **Amended, `BACKLOG.md` §1j:** discharges `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §4.1's representability obligation in full (§3.5) — adds no obligation back to that document, and adds three further reused `event_type` rows (§12) rather than a new one.

### 13.2 ADR-036 — The Fourth Entity Access Gateway Check and Its Composition With Process-Automated Reach

- **Status:** Team-Approved.
- **Cost to reverse:** **Moderate** (`PROCESS.md` §12.1). The fourth check adds a conditional evaluation step at the Gateway, reusing `01-application-construction-design.md`'s own behavioral-binding mechanism and `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md`'s existing `authority-refusal` event type; it introduces no new stored catalog, no new column, and no migration of any already-committed instance. Reversing or narrowing the check's own trigger condition, or its position in the fixed order, is a change to Gateway logic and to this document's own text, not a change to any physical schema or any already-recorded row — materially lower than the Brutal grade ADR-028 carries for the storage shape this decision composes with rather than alters.
- **Upstream decisions assumed:** `01-application-construction-design.md` §4.1, §4.2.3–§4.2.4 (the behavioral-binding mechanism this decision reuses, and the access-binding's own construct-scoped View/Invoke vocabulary this decision composes with rather than reopens); §3.2–§3.3 above (the `relationship_catalog` mechanism a behavioral binding is represented through, and the Gateway itself, §4.1); `03-data-administration-design.md` §5, §5.5 (the record-scoped-access determination for professional-builder actors, and the explicit naming of the end-user half as a distinct, unresolved question this decision answers); `05-api-contract-design.md` §11 (the destination this decision realizes); `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §3.2, §5.2, §6, §7 (the four boundary-crossing step kinds, the "no engine-originated identity/exemption" discipline, and the human-task-as-ordinary-Invoke framing this decision's composition analysis in §9.7 depends on); `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.3 (the `authority-refusal` event type this decision reuses, already extended by `06-integration-and-extensibility-design.md` and `07-cross-system-data-layer-design.md` to further source mechanisms). ADR-028 through ADR-035 remain Provisional and are not assumed settled; this decision depends on none of their approval status beyond the mechanisms they already fix.
- **Verified vs. reasoned:** Reasoned throughout. No time-sensitive ecosystem, product, or vendor claim is made; every finding derives from the cited specification and the cited, already-written upstream design documents' own content.
- **Context:** `BACKLOG.md` §1h surfaced this question at H20a's close; H21 (`03-data-administration-design.md`) resolved its professional-builder half and named the end-user half open with no destination; H23 (`05-api-contract-design.md`) assessed and declined it, naming this document's Gateway as the correct destination but performing no amendment, per `PROCESS.md` §3. This ticket is the amendment `§1h-destination` and `§1j` both name as owed, run as one consolidated pass per `BACKLOG.md` §1j's own recommendation, so that this obligation and the process-definition catalog obligation are designed with sight of each other rather than independently — the two meet at exactly one point (§9.7), which no prior document had reason to notice because no prior document held both obligations at once.
- **Question this answers, phrased for reuse:** Given a single, ordered mediation point already fixed for every read and write of a builder-defined entity, and a still-open question of which records a resolved end-user role may reach through a construct-mediated Surface or Command, what is the smallest fourth check that closes that gap by composing with a mechanism already fixed rather than inventing a new grant structure — and, given a second, concurrently-discharged obligation to store a process definition whose steps may themselves invoke the Gateway without any end-user actor present, whose reach does that fourth check evaluate when the invoking party is a process step rather than a live end-user request?
- **Criteria applied, and how each resolved:**
  1. *A dedicated new grant structure (a record-scope table, analogous to the administrative-scope grant) versus reusing the existing behavioral-binding mechanism.* Decisive for reuse — `01-application-construction-design.md` §4.1 already defines a behavioral binding as "a generic condition-and-parameter shape attached to a construct, governing what it does," and §4.2.4 already models exactly this composition ("a condition that must hold... composed with an access binding") for invocation-gating; a record-level narrowing condition is the identical generic shape applied to what a construct's own reach returns rather than whether it may be invoked at all, and inventing a second condition-carrying structure would duplicate a mechanism already capable of carrying it.
  2. *Position in the fixed order: last, versus first or interleaved.* Decisive for last — the check narrows what has already cleared validity, consent, and classification; placing it earlier would require re-deriving those three against a not-yet-bounded candidate set, and the existing three checks' own fixed order (ADR-028) is not disturbed by appending rather than interleaving.
  3. *Whether the check's precondition should be unconditional (evaluated on every Gateway call) or conditional (only where an end-user role invokes a construct).* Decisive for conditional — `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §3.2 fixes that an automated rule step acts "without human involvement," so an unconditional check would need to evaluate a role-scoping condition against a caller that has no role to evaluate, forcing either a vacuous pass (indistinguishable from not checking) or a fabricated default identity `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §5.2 already forecloses ("no engine-originated identity"). A conditional trigger, on the same shape the Consent and Minimization Check already uses, avoids both.
  4. *Whether an automated rule step should inherit the resolved identity of whatever request originally started its process instance, versus carrying no end-user role at all.* Decisive for no inherited role — an automated rule step may be triggered by the engine's own timer long after the originating request has ended, per `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §6; treating a stale, no-longer-live request's identity as the current invoker would be exactly the kind of caller-supplied, non-resolved scope `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §4.2 already forecloses for connection acquisition, applied here to the Gateway's fourth check specifically.
  5. *A new `event_type` versus reusing `authority-refusal`.* Decisive for reuse — the check's own refusal shape ("a role reaching beyond a resolved grant's own bound") is the identical shape `authority-refusal` already exists to describe, already extended twice (`06-integration-and-extensibility-design.md`, `07-cross-system-data-layer-design.md`) by `source_mechanism` rather than by a new type; a third reuse follows the established pattern, and minting a new type here would be the third redundant-type risk `BACKLOG.md` §1l already cautions against.
- **Decision:** (1) A fourth Gateway check, the End-User Record-Reach Check, runs last in the fixed order, evaluating any behavioral binding attached to the invoked construct whose condition expresses a record-level narrowing, where the invoking identity is a resolved end-user role reaching entity content through that construct (§4.4). (2) The check's precondition is conditional, not unconditional, on the identical pattern the Consent and Minimization Check already establishes for itself. (3) An automated rule step's own Gateway calls never satisfy that precondition, because such a step acts without human involvement by the specification's own §3.2 definition, and carries no inherited end-user identity from whatever request originally started its process instance (§9.7). (4) An interaction step, and an action step or human-task completion an end-user role invokes, do satisfy the precondition and clear the fourth check exactly as any other construct-mediated end-user reach would. (5) The check's refusal is captured by the existing `authority-refusal` event type, extended to a third `source_mechanism`, minting no new type (§12).
- **Alternatives considered:** *A dedicated end-user record-scope grant table, modeled on the administrative-scope grant* — rejected under criterion 1; it would duplicate a condition-carrying mechanism `01-application-construction-design.md` already provides and already licenses for this exact generic purpose. *Running the fourth check first, ahead of the Validation Engine* — rejected under criterion 2; it gains nothing and disturbs an already-fixed order for no stated benefit. *An unconditional fourth check, refusing outright wherever no end-user role is resolved* — rejected under criterion 3; it would refuse every automated rule step's own legitimate, builder-authorized reach, a regression `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` §6 already forecloses by fixing that a Gateway caller's terms do not vary by caller kind for the other three checks, which this decision does not disturb. *Inheriting the originating request's identity for an automated step's later Gateway calls* — rejected under criterion 4; the identity may no longer be live, and treating it as current would be exactly the caller-supplied-scope failure mode `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` already forecloses elsewhere. *A new, dedicated event type for the fourth check's refusal* — rejected under criterion 5; `authority-refusal`'s own shape and its existing extension precedent already fit.
- **Consequences:** Binds no new obligation onto `01-application-construction-design.md` — the behavioral-binding mechanism this decision reuses is consumed exactly as already designed, and its closed construct/binding vocabulary is not reopened. Closes `BACKLOG.md` §1h in full, both the professional-builder half (already closed by ADR-030) and the end-user half (`§1h-destination`, closed here). Adds no new obligation onto `04-workflow-and-process-automation-design.md` — that document's own "no engine-originated identity/exemption" discipline is read, not extended, to resolve §9.7's composition question; this decision states the resolution on that document's own already-fixed terms. Leaves open, and does not resolve, the more foundational question `03-data-administration-design.md` §5.5 names — how a Surface's presented content is bound to the entity data underlying it in the first place — which remains a distinct, unowned question this decision's own check presumes resolved elsewhere, never itself resolves. Adds no new `event_type` to `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md`'s model — `authority-refusal` is extended to a third `source_mechanism` instead (§12).

## 16. Binding Rules

These rules hold for every entity, schema, relationship, and change subject to this model and are subordinate to the charter.

- **Every builder-defined entity is a physical, insert-only, versioned table inside its own application's schema.** No write ever updates or deletes an existing version row; a change is a new row sharing the prior row's `record_id` (§8.1).
- **A schema is complete, and governs no data, only once all five definitional elements are established across the four catalogs.** An incomplete definition accepts no instance write (§3.2).
- **Every read and write of builder-defined entity data passes through the Entity Access Gateway, with no second path.** The Validation Engine, the Consent and Minimization Check, classification/category resolution, and the fourth, end-user role-scoped record-reach check each run inside that single mediation point, in fixed order (§4.1, §4.4).
- **A write that fails validity, consistency, or referential correctness does not proceed.** Uncertainty resolves to refusal (§4.2).
- **No relationship resolves across a tenant boundary, and no dependent removal or change proceeds unresolved.** Referential integrity holds across a relationship's full lifecycle (§5).
- **A migration is all-or-nothing, and a reversible path is a structural consequence of the append-only shape, never a second mechanism.** No migration leaves an instance satisfying neither its prior nor its new schema (§6).
- **A schema evolution's backward-compatibility is determined once, at the point of proposal, and uncertainty resolves to backward-incompatible.** No existing instance is rewritten under a compatible evolution (§7).
- **Personal-data erasure reaches every retained version's field content, never a version's own existence or its history-chain identifiers.** The audit trail's reference to a record remains resolvable after erasure (§8.2).
- **The construct/binding-kind enumeration and the AI-suggested/builder-approved provenance attribute are explicitly declined, not silently absorbed.** Each is routed to the document positioned to own it, with grounds stated (§3.4, §9.4).
- **No storage-shape obligation handed to this document is silently dropped.** Each is discharged, partially discharged with the remainder named, or declined with grounds (§9), including the two obligations a later consolidated amendment added (§9.5–§9.6).
- **A process definition is never represented through the entity/relationship mechanism, and an end-user's record reach through a process step is evaluated by the identical rule that governs any other construct-mediated reach.** The process-definition catalog is a distinct catalog family (§3.5); the fourth Gateway check applies only where a resolved end-user role invokes a construct, never to an automated rule step acting without human involvement (§9.7).
- **Everything remains domain-neutral and platform-level.** No catalog, column, or mechanism in this document encodes the characteristics of any single domain; all remain valid for any software built on the platform.
- **This document records exactly two ADRs.** ADR-028 and ADR-036 (§13) are the genuine, independent decisions this document makes; every other design choice above consumes a mechanism, boundary, or invariant the cited specification and cited upstream design documents already fix, or directly applies a decision an upstream document already made (§3.5's amendment to ADR-028).
