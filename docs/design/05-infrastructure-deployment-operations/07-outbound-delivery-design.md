# Outbound Delivery Design — AI ahaMatic

## 1. Purpose and Reading Order

This document realizes one obligation and one dependency, and nothing wider. The obligation is `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §6's delivery bullet: *"The platform makes every routed request reach the human it names, through some means sufficient for them to learn of it. This document fixes the obligation that delivery occurs; it does not fix the channel, interface, or tool through which it is carried out, and it fixes no resolution-time figure — each remains outside this document's own scope."* The dependency is the one `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 names and deliberately leaves open: its Path A enrollment grant "must reach the person it names over a channel the issuing authority does not itself read," and until such a channel exists that mechanism is, in its own words, inert for the first owner of every tenant the platform admits.

It answers **how** a specific human is reached, and it re-decides nothing either source already fixes: not which human a request is routed to, not what an approval request must carry, not what a resolution means, not what an enrollment grant is or what it may authorize. It names **no product, vendor, provider, or tool**, and fixes no timeliness figure.

This is the seventh document of the Infrastructure, Deployment & Operations layer, and the first in this library whose two consumers make asymmetric demands of the same mechanism — a fact §3 establishes before any mechanism is designed, because conflating the two is the whole design trap here.

It is structured as a pyramid: first the document-shape determination this ticket asked to be made rather than assumed, and the boundaries it fixes (§2); then the two consumers and what each actually needs (§3); then the mechanism itself (§4); then delivery failure and what is and is not knowable about it (§5); then the storage check (§6); then the residency and privacy treatment of an outbound handoff (§7); then the four adjacent concerns this document does not design and where each boundary falls (§8); then the evidence delivery produces (§9); then the one design decision it records (§10); then its boundaries, precedence, and binding rules (§11–§13).

**Every `§N` cited below was resolved against the section it names before being relied upon, per `BACKLOG.md` §1t's standing rule, and no string appears in quotation marks unless read verbatim in the cited file.** Three resolutions are recorded because they qualify what this document could rely on. (1) `ADR-REGISTER.md` lists **ADR-067** as its highest entry, and a library-wide grep of `docs/design/` independently returns **ADR-067** as the highest minted identifier — the two agree, so the next genuinely free identifier is **ADR-068**, used at §10. (2) `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.2 twice cites a `§6.4` that document does not have (its §6 ends at §6.3) — the known defect `BACKLOG.md` §1ay records. Nothing below is built on that citation; §9 relies instead on §4.2's own base-record rule, read verbatim. ## 2. Scope, the Document-Shape Determination, and What This Document Does Not Own

### 2.1 Why One New Layer 5 Document, and Not an Extension to Either Named Anchor

The ticket authorizing this work named two candidate extensions and left the shape to be determined by investigation. Both were read in full, including their scope-exclusion sections and their ADRs. **The determination is one new Layer 5 document, for four reasons stated here rather than in a handoff alone.**

- **Each candidate's own stated boundary excludes this content, in its own words, so either extension would reverse a boundary rather than occupy free space.** `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §2 excludes "The notification channel, approval interface, or routing tool through which a human is actually reached," and its §14 repeats the exclusion for "any notification or interface mechanism." `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` §2 excludes "How an escalation is requested, routed, recorded, or resolved," assigning all of it to the human-in-the-loop protocol, and that document's own §7.3 restates the same disclaimer against that protocol's §6 and §7 directly. Neither exclusion is a gap awaiting content; each is a stated ownership boundary, and appending this mechanism to either would require reversing it and re-opening ADR-054's or ADR-049's own Consequences to do so.
- **The observability candidate additionally fails on a mechanical boundary its own document enforces, not merely on a scope statement.** `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` §9 fixes that "A metric is not evidence, and an audit event is not a health signal," that its alert-evaluation mechanism "reads only the metrics, logs, and traces §3 defines," and that "the two mechanisms consume disjoint inputs by construction, not merely by convention." Delivery's inputs are exactly the other kind: a `platform.pending_approvals` row and an enrollment artifact — control-plane state, never a measurement of runtime behavior. An alert is a signal *about the platform*, evaluated by whoever watches it; a delivery is an act *toward one named person*. Housing delivery there would make that document's own alert-evaluation mechanism read an input its own §9 forbids it to read.
- **The human-in-the-loop candidate would additionally force a dependency direction the map does not authorize.** `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` is a Layer 6 document, and `implementation-document-map.md` fixes that "Each layer may depend only on the layers beneath it," with exactly one recorded cross-layer exception — `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`, authorized because C-19 explicitly inherits the Meta-Operations guardrails. This mechanism's second consumer is `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4, a **Layer 2** document. Placing delivery in Layer 6 would put the mechanism a Layer 2 consumer waits on five layers above it, and would need a second cross-layer exception no specification authorizes. **One qualification is reported rather than relied on:** that map's own Layer 2 table currently gives `11-ai-model-governance-and-output-quality-design.md` a dependency on `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`, a Layer 3 document, without recording it as a further exception — a tracker defect noted at §11, and not a licence this determination takes for itself. Layer 5 avoids the question entirely: the seam this document reaches the approval consumer through is `platform.pending_approvals`, a **Layer 1** structure (`02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1), so no dependency on the Layer 6 document's own mechanism is required at all.

Nothing here is a decision that document had to wait for; everything here is a decision that had to wait for it. A document that consumes two already-complete mechanisms and supplies neither's reasoning belongs beneath neither of them in derivation order. Layer 5's charge — how platform work itself operates, on an infrastructure substrate configured per environment and region, with injected credentials — is the shape this mechanism actually has, and Layer 2's charge is not: Layer 2 holds the guardrails that *constrain* build, and this document is a mechanism guardrails constrain, not one of them.

**The precedent is immediate.** H70 determined that neither of its two candidate anchors could absorb its content without reversing a stated boundary, and wrote a new document rather than treating the extension as free (`11-ai-model-governance-and-output-quality-design.md` §2.1). This is the same shape of determination, reached against two anchors whose exclusions are, if anything, more explicit.

Second, `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md`'s three "no governing document fixes one" disclaimers are now stale in their **ground** while remaining correct in their **content**, and that document is not editable by this ticket: the exclusion itself stands (this document, not that one, owns the channel), but its stated reason no longer holds, and it now has an owner to point at. The correction is recorded as owed at §11, naming the target, exactly as H69 and H70 recorded their own out-of-scope debts.

## 3. Two Consumers, Asymmetric — Established Before Any Mechanism

### 3.1 What Each Consumer Actually Needs

Two already-written mechanisms depend on delivery, and they are not two instances of one need.

| | **Approval notice** (`03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §5, via `platform.pending_approvals`) | **Enrollment grant** (`03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4, Path A) |
|---|---|---|
| Who the recipient is | A human whose grant covers the decision, resolved by that document's §5.3 routing determination and recorded in the record's `required_authority` column | The one `platform_user_id` the grant artifact names — for the load-bearing case, the first owner of a tenant admitted moments earlier |
| Can the recipient authenticate? | **Yes.** Holding decision authority presupposes an established identity: `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §5 fixes that "an actor whose identity is not established holds no entry in this matrix and no permission it would otherwise carry" | **No, and this is the whole point.** The recipient is in the zero-factor state — no non-revoked row in any of the three factor structures — and holds no session, no assurance level, and no permission |
| Can a pull surface serve it? | **Yes in principle.** A recipient who can sign in can read a surface that lists what awaits them | **No, not even in principle.** There is no way to sign in to read it. A surface reachable only after authentication cannot serve a consumer whose entire problem is that it cannot authenticate |
| Must the notice be self-sufficient? | **No.** The recipient can reach the platform, so the notice need only make them aware | **Yes.** The artifact *is* the thing that must arrive; a notice that only announces an artifact leaves the recipient with nothing they can act on |
| Is the notice's content sensitive? | The record it points at is; the pointer need not be (§3.3) | **Inherently.** The artifact authorizes a first-factor write against one identity |

**The second column is why `DECISIONS.md` D-54's option space cannot simply be read as satisfied by either of its members.** That decision fixes the platform specifies "push notification, a steward-facing dashboard, or both," and that this is platform-core rather than deployment-time configuration. It speaks to awareness of a pending approval, and for that consumer either member of the option space could serve. For the enrollment consumer only the push member can, and not as a preference — a dashboard is *structurally* unable to reach a person who cannot sign in. **Push is therefore the necessary member of D-54's option space, and the dashboard is the optional one** — and the optional one is an interface this ticket does not design (§8).

### 3.2 The Determination: One Mechanism, Two Notice Classes

**One mechanism, with two notice classes, and the split is at exactly two properties.** Everything else the two consumers need is identical, and identical in mechanism rather than merely in shape: resolving a named recipient to a person, resolving that person to an address, handing a rendered notice to a configured channel, recording the handoff, and never treating a handoff as a receipt. Building two mechanisms would duplicate all five and would produce two places for the same failure posture to drift apart.

The two properties that differ are:

| Property | Pointer class (approval) | Self-sufficient class (enrollment) |
|---|---|---|
| **Content sufficiency** | The notice carries an identifier and nothing more; acting on it requires reaching the platform | The notice carries the artifact itself; nothing further is required to act |
| **Channel readability** | Unconstrained by this document — see §3.3 for why that is safe here and not a concession | **Constrained absolutely**: the channel must be one the issuing authority does not itself read (§3.3) |

A notice's class is fixed by its caller at the point delivery is invoked, from the consumer it belongs to; it is never inferred from the notice's content, and there is no third class and no per-notice override. This matters because the two classes are not equally safe on the same channel, and a mechanism that let a class be chosen late — or defaulted — would be one refactor away from putting a credential-bearing artifact through a channel chosen for a pointer.

### 3.3 The Same Risk, Closed Two Different Ways — and Why Only One Class Can Close It the Cheaper Way

Both classes face the identical risk: someone other than the intended recipient reads what was delivered. The mechanism closes it twice, differently, and the reason is structural rather than stylistic.

- **The self-sufficient class closes it by constraining the channel, because it cannot constrain its content.** The artifact must travel in full or the consumer is unserved, so its exposure cannot be reduced by carrying less. `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 already fixed why the channel constraint follows and this document consumes rather than re-derives it: an authority holding a usable factor for a tenant owner's identity *can* authenticate as that owner, which is precisely the act `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §4's Tenant-admission boundary forbids — "Cannot act as, or in place of, a tenant owner once a tenant is admitted." Delivery adds one precision that mechanism had no occasion to state, because it is a property of delivery rather than of enrollment: **the constraint is on reading, not on addressing.** An issuing authority may supply the address a notice is sent to and still be unable to read what arrives there, and the two are different capabilities — which is what makes the constraint satisfiable at all rather than a requirement no channel could meet.
- **The pointer class closes it by constraining the content, which is the closure available to it.** Its recipient can reach the platform, so the notice does not need to carry anything that would matter if read: an opaque identifier reaches nothing without an authenticated session, and §4.5 fixes that nothing else travels — no tenant, application, actor, action, or trigger name. The class therefore needs no channel-readability property, and this document asserts none for it. That is a consequence of the closure it already has, not a relaxation: **content-minimality is the pointer class's only closure, which is exactly why §4.5 admits nothing beyond the identifier**, including facts a recipient might reasonably want.

Stated as one finding: the enrollment class must trust a channel because it cannot make its notice safe; the approval class makes its notice safe so that it need not trust a channel. Neither closure is available to the other consumer, and that asymmetry — not the delivery act — is what the two-class split exists to hold.

## 4. The Delivery Mechanism

### 4.1 Where It Runs — a Shared Library, Never a Component Module

The mechanism must be reachable from more than one place: from whatever raises a pending approval, and from the Enrollment Gate `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 places inside `components/isolation-and-trust`. That second caller settles the placement question by itself, because `03-architecture-realization-design.md` §4.1's first row is **Full**-coverage and admits no exception: "A rule forbidding any import path from `isolation-and-trust` into any other component module." Delivery therefore cannot live in **any** `components/*` module and remain reachable by the Gate.

It does not need to. That same section fixes the positive form of the import rule as: "no import in `components/*` resolves outside `components/*`, `guardrail/`, and the platform's own shared libraries." **The delivery mechanism occupies the third of those positions** — one of the platform's own shared libraries: importable from any component module without being one, holding no capability of the primitive families (`BACKLOG.md` §1ar's own verified closure records that no new capability is warranted for this concern, "since this is a Meta-Operations obligation on the agent's own conduct, not a builder-facing primitive"), and creating no forbidden direction, because a shared library is not a component and an import into one is not a cross-component edge. This adds no eighth component, places nothing inside any component's boundary, and amends `03-architecture-realization-design.md` §3.1's module table not at all. Whether that document's own §3.1 table should additionally enumerate shared-library positions is its question, not this document's, and is raised at §11 rather than decided here.

### 4.2 Recipient Resolution: From an Authority to a Person

`platform.pending_approvals.required_authority` holds, in `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1's own words, "the resolved authority that document's own §5.3 routing determination names" — an **authority**, not a person. Delivery cannot hand a notice to an authority, so resolving one to the people who currently hold it is genuine work this document owns. It is a read, never a determination: **which** authority is `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §5.3's, already made.

The resolution uses structures that already exist, and the discriminator is the role vocabulary itself rather than any heuristic:

- **`platform.platform_role_definitions`** is fixed as "A fixed, platform-defined enumeration of the roles named in `01-business-and-ux/04-personas-and-roles.md` §5 and `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §4–§5 (the four steward roles, and the six tenant-scoped builder roles)." That enumeration tells the resolver which structure holds a given role's bindings, with no inference required.
- **A steward-plane authority** resolves against `platform.steward_role_bindings`, which "Binds a `platform_user_id` to one of the four steward roles" — a platform-global read reaching no tenant schema. This is the path taken for the residual, generically-classified rows `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §5.3 assigns to "the platform steward authority the access-control model's own least-privilege default already reserves for undelegated platform-layer decisions."
- **A tenant-scoped authority** resolves against `tenant_<id>.tenant_users`, which binds a `platform_user_id` to one of the six tenant-scoped roles with a `revoked_at` column, in the schema the record's own `tenant_id` names. Where the resolved role is tenant-scoped and `tenant_id` is `NULL`, the recipient is **unresolvable** and the delivery is refused (§5.1) — never resolved to a default tenant, a platform-plane fallback, or the raising actor, matching the deny-by-default posture `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §3 fixes and the identical refusal-on-unresolvable discipline `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 already applies to a `NULL` `region_of_record`.
- Only **non-revoked** bindings resolve. A revoked binding is not a holder.

Two consequences follow, and both are stated rather than smoothed:

- **Where an authority is held by more than one person, the notice goes to every current holder.** This is not a routing decision and not a fan-out policy: choosing among the holders *would* be a routing decision, and routing is not this document's. Delivering to all of them is the only reading of "reach the human it names" available to a mechanism that may not narrow an authority to an individual. It discloses nothing beyond each recipient's own reach, because every holder already holds the authority the notice concerns and the notice itself carries nothing else (§4.5).
- **Where an authority is currently held by no one, the notice reaches no one.** The resolution returns an empty set, the delivery is refused, and no notice is rendered or handed off. The obligation is not discharged, and §5.2 states why nothing worse than that follows.

For the self-sufficient class no resolution step is needed at all: the artifact names exactly one `platform_user_id`, so the recipient is the anchor that identifier resolves to, and delivery reads no role structure. That the anchor exists is `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md`'s concern, not this one's — and for a person admitted under that document's §6.1 nothing yet writes it, the gap `BACKLOG.md` §1aw(a) already tracks. Delivery inherits that gap and does not restate it as a new one: an unresolvable recipient is refused by the paragraph above, whatever the reason it could not be resolved.

### 4.3 Address Resolution, and Why It Precedes Rendering

A recipient identity is not an address. Delivery resolves each recipient to the endpoint the configured channel class addresses — the one structure this mechanism owes, described structurally at §6 and placed by no decision here.

Resolution is ordered ahead of rendering, and the ordering is load-bearing rather than tidy: **a notice is rendered only once every recipient has resolved to an address.** For the pointer class this saves nothing in particular. For the self-sufficient class it means the grant artifact is never rendered into a notice that has nowhere to go — no partially-prepared notice holds a credential-bearing artifact while an address lookup is attempted, and a refusal at this step produces no rendered content at all.

One observation is offered to the consumer that issues that artifact, and it is an observation rather than a condition this document adds to a mechanism it does not own: `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 states plainly that an outstanding stateless grant "cannot be revoked before its own expiry, because there is no record to revoke," and that reissuing after a lost delivery leaves the earlier artifact live. A caller that asked delivery to resolve an address **before** issuing a grant would leave no live grant that was never deliverable. Whether to order it that way is that document's own sequencing to fix; this document fixes only that an unresolvable address is a refusal producing no handoff.

### 4.4 The Channel Port — Medium-Independence as a Structural Property

Delivery hands a rendered notice to a **channel port**: a single, configured outbound endpoint through which every notice leaves. The port is defined by the properties a conforming channel must have, never by a medium, and **no medium, service class, protocol, or tool is named anywhere in this document.** Medium-independence is not a stated aspiration here; it is the mechanism's shape — the port is the only place in the mechanism that knows anything about a channel at all, and everything in §4.2, §4.3, §4.5, §5, and §9 is written against the port rather than against a channel.

Four properties are required of a conforming channel:

| Property | What It Requires | Why It Is Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Out-of-platform reachability** | It reaches a person who holds no session and no platform identity in use | Without it the self-sufficient class is unserved, which is the consumer that made this mechanism necessary (§3.1) |
| **Recipient-exclusive readability** | For the self-sufficient class only: the issuing authority cannot read what is delivered to the endpoint | Consumed from `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 and grounded in `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §4's Tenant-admission boundary (§3.3); addressing is not reading, and only reading is constrained |
| **Configured, never branched** | Which channel class is in force is an injected configuration value, not a conditional in core code | The identical discipline `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §4.1 fixes for tier and region, and `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` §5 applies to tier applicability; it is also what keeps the port medium-independent in fact rather than in intent |
| **No receipt claim** | The channel is not relied upon to establish that a person received or read anything | §5.1: receipt is not knowable, and a channel-reported acknowledgement does not make it knowable |

The port's own configuration reaches it through paths that already exist and are not redesigned: the channel class and its endpoint parameters are non-secret configuration values injected at process start per `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §4.2, whose closing rule is "No third path exists"; any credential the channel requires is a secret with no exemption, injected per that document's §5.1 from the store scoped to the `(tier, region)` intersection its §5.2 fixes — "Each `(tier, region)` intersection... has its own dedicated secret-storage scope, never a single, platform-wide store partitioned only by naming convention." One property follows from that scoping without this document adding a rule: the channel a notice leaves through is the one provisioned for the region the process is operating in. §7 states precisely how far that reaches and how far it does not.

### 4.5 The Notice: What May Travel, per Class

**Pointer class.** The notice carries the `approval_id` and the bare fact that a decision awaits the recipient. It carries **nothing else** — no tenant identifier, no application identifier, no actor reference, no target reference, no matched-trigger name, no element of the request's own content, and no link. Two independent reasons fix this floor, and either alone would:

- The request's content is bounded by `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §6's own rule that a request never exposes a secret (INV-03), discloses another tenant's state (INV-01), or violates residency (INV-07) "in the act of asking." A notice leaving the platform is a weaker position than a request assembled inside it, so nothing that rule bounds inside may travel outside it.
- Content-minimality is this class's only closure against a channel with no readability guarantee (§3.3). Admitting the matched trigger's name — the most defensible candidate, since every recipient already holds that authority — would trade the closure for a convenience, and would replace a structural property with a per-field disclosure judgment this document would then have to keep making.

**The notice carries no link, and that is a boundary rather than an oversight.** A link would name a surface, and naming a surface is designing the approval interface — which `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §2 excludes and this ticket does not authorize. The identifier is therefore what travels, and reaching the record it names remains an authenticated act on a surface neither document has designed. This is stated plainly at §8 rather than left for a reader to discover.

**Self-sufficient class.** The notice carries the grant artifact and nothing added to it. The artifact already names exactly one `platform_user_id`, an issuance time, a short expiry, and a nonce (`03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4); delivery adds no recipient name, no tenant name, no instruction naming a surface, and no second copy of anything the artifact carries. The artifact's own content, expiry, and single-use property are ADR-067's and are not touched here.

### 4.6 The Delivery Act — Caller-Invoked and Synchronous, Never a Sweep

Delivery runs when a caller invokes it, as part of the act that produced the outstanding state: the mechanism that writes a `platform.pending_approvals` row, or the mechanism that issues an enrollment grant. It is **not** a scheduled sweep over outstanding records, and the reason is not economy:

- A sweep would need to know which records had already been notified, which is the only thing a delivery-attempt ledger would be for — and §6 finds that ledger unwarranted, for reasons stronger than storage cost.
- A sweep would need a cadence, and no document in either library fixes one: the realized specification "fixes no resolution-time figure," `BACKLOG.md` §1ar records that no non-functional requirement sets a resolution-time floor for a pending approval, and numeric figures are `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`'s. Designing a sweep would mean inventing the figure that governs it.

The act is five ordered steps, and a refusal at any of the first three produces no handoff: resolve recipients (§4.2); resolve each recipient's address (§4.3); render the notice for its class (§4.5); hand it to the channel port (§4.4); record the outcome (§9). Delivery writes nothing else — it does not touch the `platform.pending_approvals` row it read, does not transition any status, and does not write to any factor structure. `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §5.4's rule holds untouched: "No scheduled process, timeout, or default transition ever flips it."

## 5. Delivery Failure, and What Is and Is Not Knowable

### 5.1 Three States, and the One That Is Not Knowable at All

A channel can fail, and the states a failure can leave are not equally observable. Naming which is which is more useful than a retry posture would be, because everything in §5.2–§5.4 follows from it.

| State | Knowable? | What the Platform Does |
|---|---|---|
| **Handoff refused** — no recipient resolved, no address resolved, or the port did not accept the notice | **Yes**, mechanically and locally | Records the refusal, naming which of the three resolution steps failed (§9). The obligation is **not** discharged, and is never recorded as discharged |
| **Handoff accepted** — the port accepted the notice | **Yes** | Records the acceptance. This is the strongest fact the platform ever holds about a notice, and it is neither delivery nor receipt |
| **Received by the named person** | **No — not by any mechanism in this design, and not by any channel report** | Nothing. No field records it, no check reads it, and no rule anywhere depends on it |

**Delivery confirmation is not achievable in this design, and handoff confirmation is what is achievable instead.** The reason is structural rather than a gap awaiting a better channel: any acknowledgement a channel could return attests to an endpoint's behavior, never to a person's awareness, and the obligation this document realizes is stated in terms of the person — "through some means sufficient for them to learn of it." A mechanism that recorded an endpoint acknowledgement in the field where a person's awareness belongs would be recording a different fact under the right name. This is stated on the model H70 used for its own intake-only trigger and H71 for its own inert write path: named as a limit, not softened into a weaker version of the guarantee.

One corollary is worth stating because it is easy to reach for: **the platform never upgrades a handoff acceptance into a delivery, and never upgrades a delivery into a receipt.** Where a channel reports more than acceptance, the additional report is not read, because reading it would make the mechanism's own guarantee depend on a channel property this document has deliberately not asserted.

### 5.2 An Undelivered Notice Never Decays Into Approval

`05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §6 fixes that "Approval is never assumed, implicit, or inferred from silence," and that "a timeout, a non-response, or an ambiguous signal is never treated as consent." A failed delivery is the purest possible source of silence, so this is where that rule is most at risk — and it is closed structurally, by mechanisms that already exist, rather than by a rule this document adds:

- The `platform.pending_approvals` row is untouched by delivery (§4.6) and its `status` is fixed by `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 as "`pending` until an explicit resolution write, and never auto-transitioned," reinforced by `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §5.4. A notice that never arrived leaves a record that stays `pending` indefinitely — and an indefinitely pending record is the safe direction, because the gated action does not proceed without an affirmative resolution.
- For the self-sufficient class the equivalent is `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4's own condition: an undelivered grant simply expires, and the identity remains in the zero-factor state — which is the state that permits a fresh grant to be issued. Nothing about a failed delivery moves the identity toward holding a factor.

**Both consumers' failure modes converge on the same outcome: the outstanding state persists.** Neither has a decay path, because in neither case does delivery write the state that would have to change for one to exist.

### 5.3 A Delivery Failure Has No Non-Circular Escalation Path

The natural response to a failed delivery would be to tell a human about it. That response is circular: telling a human is this mechanism, and it is the mechanism that just failed. A retry inside the same port faces whatever refused the handoff; a second port would be a second channel this document does not have; and escalating through the approval mechanism would raise a record whose own notice would need delivering.

**This document therefore designs no escalation path for its own failure**, and states the circularity as the reason rather than leaving the absence unexplained. What it does instead is record the refusal as evidence (§9) and leave the outstanding state visible where it already is (§5.2) — both of which are facts a person reading platform state can find, and neither of which requires delivery to reach them.

The remaining question — what operational surface a person actually reads that state on — is not this document's, and the answer today is that none exists: `DECISIONS.md` D-54's own finding, reached before that decision was made, is that there is no way, push or pull, for a human to learn a pending-approval record exists, and the pull half of its option space is an interface both this ticket and ADR-060's own scope exclude. This document does not bind `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` to surface a delivery refusal, does not add a metric, and does not classify one — that document's §9 keeps its inputs disjoint from control-plane state by construction, and asserting a signal on its behalf would breach the boundary §2.1 used to exclude it as an anchor. The gap is named at §11 for a tracker, not closed here by overreach.

### 5.4 Re-Notification, Without a Cadence This Document May Not Fix

Re-notifying is permitted and is nothing more than a fresh invocation of §4.6 against a state that is still outstanding — a `platform.pending_approvals` row still `pending`, or an identity still in the zero-factor state. It is not an inference, a reminder policy, or a state transition, and it is the opposite of inferring approval from silence.

**It is never automatic**, because automating it requires a cadence no document fixes (§4.6) and a ledger §6 finds unwarranted. The honest consequence: a notice whose handoff was refused is not retried by the platform, and the outstanding state simply persists until some caller acts again. That is a real limit on how far this mechanism discharges `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §6's obligation in the failure case, and it is stated as one rather than presented as a design property.

## 6. Where Delivery State Lives — Checked

Per the standing discipline every document in this library applies, every structure this mechanism might need was checked against `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 before any new one was proposed. The check reaches a different answer for each of the two candidates, and the second answer is the more interesting one.

**One structure is owed: a delivery address per recognized identity.** Nothing in the platform's schema carries an endpoint at which a person can be reached. `platform.platform_users` carries "identity attributes" without enumerating them, and no other platform-global or per-tenant structure carries a contact endpoint at all — so the fact delivery cannot proceed without is genuinely absent rather than present under another name. Described structurally, and placed by no decision here:

- **One current delivery address per identity**, keyed to the `platform.platform_users` anchor. One rather than several, for a reason specific to this mechanism: the channel class in force is a single injected configuration value (§4.4), so a second address would either be unreachable or would widen the self-sufficient class's exposure to more endpoints than the notice requires.
- Its **data-governance category is personal data**, resolved at the point the value is created rather than inferred by a consumer — the requirement `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §3.1 fixes for every element its mechanism governs, read here and not restated as a rule of this document's own.
- It carries **nothing else**: no channel credential (a secret, and secrets reach a running process only through `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §5.1's injection path, never through a stored row), no delivery history, and no rendered notice.
- **Physical placement, column type, nullability, and whether the value sits on the anchor row or in a structure beside it are `02-platform-data-model-design.md`'s to fix**, not this document's — the identical restraint ADR-026, ADR-064, and ADR-066 each applied, and which `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §3.2 states as an established pattern rather than a new liberty.
- **This joins `BACKLOG.md` §1av as its fourth member.** That entry exists so the accumulation of structurally-described, physically-unplaced records is caught at three rather than at five, and it instructs the next scoping pass not to proceed before checking whether a fourth has arrived. One has.

Which act captures the value is not this document's either: it is a fact about an identity, so it belongs to whichever act writes that identity's anchor — `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §6.3 for the tenant-owner case, and, for the builder-admission case, the anchor write `BACKLOG.md` §1aw(a) records that nothing performs. Delivery inherits that open gap exactly as §4.2 does and adds no new entry for it.

**No delivery-attempt record, retry queue, or per-notice status is owed, and the reason is correctness rather than economy.** Three findings, each independently sufficient:

- **The question such a ledger would answer is already answerable.** `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §10.1's argument for `platform.pending_approvals` was that finding what is outstanding, and for whom, is not derivable from an append-only stream. That structure now exists and answers exactly that; a delivery-attempt row would add no fact that changes what is outstanding, because a notice's fate never changes whether a decision still awaits a human. The self-sufficient class's equivalent index is the zero-factor state itself, which `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 already defines as a read over three existing structures.
- **Nothing needs it to avoid duplicate work.** De-duplication is a sweep's problem, and there is no sweep (§4.6).
- **For the self-sufficient class it would be the storage choice ADR-067 already rejected, reached by a different route.** A record that made a resend possible would have to hold the rendered notice — which for that class is the grant artifact. `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 rejected storing a grant not on grounds of cost but because a stored grant row would take the identity out of the zero-factor state before it held any real factor, opening the recovery path to an identity that had enrolled nothing: "a forbidden weakening reached by a storage choice." A delivery ledger holding the same artifact under a different column name would be that weakening with an extra step, and it would additionally place a secret in stored state against `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.2's own posture toward secrets.

**Finding: delivery owes exactly one structure and no ledger.** The mechanism is stateless with respect to its own attempts, and the only durable fact it needs is one it cannot compute — where a person can be reached.

## 7. The Residency and Privacy Treatment of an Outbound Handoff, Stated Honestly

An address is personal data and a handoff leaves the platform, so two already-designed regimes bear on this mechanism. Both are read; neither is extended.

**Privacy handling is inherited, not invented.** With the address's data-governance category resolved as personal (§6), `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §3.2's Resolved Handling Obligation attaches without this document adding anything: the redaction obligation that category sets is "Redacted from any log, output, or artifact not authorized to hold it," which is the already-existing rule that keeps the address out of every audit event §9 produces. §9's exclusion of the address is therefore an application of an obligation that already binds, and not a rule this document mints. The same document's deny-by-default treatment of an unclassifiable category applies unchanged, and nothing here creates an exemption for an operational value.

**Residency reaches the reads and not the handoff, and that is a structural fact about where the check sits.** `06-compliance-and-data-residency-design.md` §3.4 places the Region Boundary Check inline within connection acquisition — "the sole gateway through which a request reaches a scoped database connection" — so it already evaluates delivery's reads of `platform.pending_approvals`, of the role-binding structures, and of the address, with no addition from this document. **It does not evaluate the outbound handoff**, because a handoff to an external channel is not a scoped-connection action and never passes through that gateway. No other check in either library evaluates it either.

This is stated as an honest limit rather than as coverage, in the same register that document already uses about itself: its own §3.5 bounds its structural claim to "the platform's own code path" and states that it is "not a claim about where bytes are physically stored, replicated, or routed at the infrastructure layer." An outbound handoff is on the far side of exactly that boundary.

What this document can contribute without inventing a residency criterion — which would be redesigning a mechanism it does not own — is one property that falls out of the injection mechanism rather than from a new rule: because the port's configuration and credentials are scoped to the `(tier, region)` intersection (§4.4, `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §5.2), the channel a notice leaves through is the one provisioned for the region the process is operating in, and a channel provisioned for another region is not reachable from that deployment's own injection path. **Whether that satisfies a resolved residency obligation for a given notice is `06-compliance-and-data-residency-design.md`'s to determine and `08-multi-region-distribution-design.md`'s topology to make good on** — named here, decided by neither this document nor this ticket, and raised at §11 so it is tracked rather than assumed closed.

## 8. The Four Adjacent Concerns, and Where Each Boundary Falls

Delivery sits between four mechanisms it is easily conflated with. Each boundary is stated from this document's side and is bidirectional: neither side absorbs the other's concern.

| Concern | Who Owns It | Where the Boundary Falls |
|---|---|---|
| **Routing** — which human holds the authority to decide a request | `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §6 and the role-and-permission matrix `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §5, realized by `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §5.3 | Routing ends at a resolved authority recorded in `platform.pending_approvals.required_authority`. Delivery begins by reading that value and resolving it to the people who currently hold it (§4.2). Delivery never selects, narrows, substitutes, or re-derives an authority, and where an authority is held by several people it delivers to all of them precisely because choosing one would cross this boundary |
| **The approval interface** — the surface a human acts on | Nobody, today. `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §2 excludes it, and this ticket excludes it | Delivery discharges **awareness**, which is what that specification's §6 obligation is stated in terms of. Acting on a delivered notice requires a surface that does not exist, which is why the notice carries an identifier rather than a link (§4.5). The approval consumer is therefore served for awareness and not for action — the mirror image of the honest limit `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 states about itself, and stated here rather than left to be inferred |
| **Recording** — the immutable, attributable record of what happened | `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` in full | Delivery populates that document's base record and mints no type (§9). It designs no record shape, no storage, and no tamper-evidence mechanism, and it records nothing about a resolution, which is not its act |
| **Resolution** — what a human's decision means and how autonomy resumes | `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §7, realized by `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §6 | Delivery writes nothing on the record it read and transitions no status (§4.6). A delivered notice confers no decision, and an undelivered one confers no approval (§5.2) |

One further boundary is worth stating because it was tested and found intact rather than assumed: `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` §2's exclusion of "How an escalation is requested, routed, recorded, or resolved" **needs no correction from this ticket.** Delivery is none of those four acts, so that document's disclaimer remains accurate as written, and this document's existence does not stale it. That is the opposite of the finding at §2.1 for `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md`, whose own disclaimer's stated ground does stale, and the two are distinguished deliberately rather than corrected as a pair.

## 9. Evidence Produced

Checked against `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5's standing index — the place that section fixes as "what a ticket checks — before minting a type, and to locate one already minted" — and read in full at its current count of **68** rather than grepped for a token.

**No `event_type` is minted.** The collision-and-fit test was run rather than the convention inherited, and the nearest candidates are poor fits rather than close ones:

- **The Authorization and grant family** — including `builder-admission`, `approval-resolution`, and every sibling — describes a grant's creation, narrowing, or authorization. A delivery creates no grant, confers no standing, and authorizes nothing; landing it there would file it under a fact §5 of that document fixes for a different kind of act, on the identical reasoning `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 used to reject the same family for a first-factor write.
- **`autonomous-change`** describes a change the agent applies to the system. A handoff changes no platform state at all — §4.6 fixes that delivery writes nothing but its own record.
- **The Residency and compliance family** — `residency-transfer` most plausibly — describes a transfer evaluated at the Region Boundary Check. §7 establishes that a handoff is not evaluated there, so recording one under that family would assert an evaluation that did not occur.
- No existing type names a delivery, a notification, a dispatch, or a handoff, so there is no collision to reconcile either.

**Two capture points populate the base record (§4.2 of that document) directly, discriminated by `source_mechanism`** — the field that document's §4.5 already establishes as the discriminator among paths within one shape, and the identical treatment `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` receives under that section's own documented absence for the Identity and session family ("captured here as a documented absence, not an omission"):

- **Handoff accepted** — `source_mechanism` the delivery mechanism, `target_reference` the opaque reference to the recipient identity, `what_was_inspected` the resolution steps that succeeded, `outcome = proceeded`. Never `applied`: nothing was applied, and the acceptance is not a delivery (§5.1).
- **Handoff refused** — the same shape at `outcome = refused`, with `result` naming which of the three resolution steps failed — recipient, address, or port — at the granularity that document's §4.2 `result` field already admits for each mechanism's own vocabulary.

**No third capture point exists for receipt**, because receipt is not knowable (§5.1). An absent record here is the honest form; a record populated from a channel acknowledgement would name a fact the platform does not hold.

Three fields are fixed by rules that already bind, not by this document's choice. `mandatory_category` is **read from the notice's own originating event** — the event that recorded the act producing the outstanding state — never assigned here, so delivery creates no new landing place for a fact already categorized and adds no ninth category — the set is the specification's own eight, which `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5 organizes its index by ("The table is organized by the eight mandatory categories §4–§5 fix."). `actor_kind` and `actor_reference` are likewise the originating act's, read and not re-derived: the delivery is a consequence of that act, not an independent actor's initiative. And **no field carries the address, the rendered notice, or the grant artifact** — the address because that document's §4.2 admits no "directly-identifying personal or sensitive-data content" and `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md` §3.2's redaction obligation independently requires it (§7); the notice and the artifact because that same base-record rule is absolute: "No field above stores directly-identifying personal or sensitive-data content, and no field stores a secret."

**`08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5 therefore needs no new row and its count remains 68**, and the conditional second target this ticket permitted on the condition of a minted type is correctly not triggered.

  5. *Does delivery owe any state of its own?* **Decisive for exactly one structure and no ledger** (§6) — the address is genuinely absent from the schema and cannot be computed, while an attempt ledger would answer a question two existing structures already answer and would, for the self-sufficient class, be the storage choice ADR-067 rejected on correctness grounds reached by a different route.
  6. *Is delivery confirmable?* **Decisive for no, with a weaker fact substituted honestly** (§5.1) — handoff is confirmable and receipt is not, because any channel report attests to an endpoint rather than to a person's awareness. Recorded because a criterion that fails to separate the options is itself a finding.
- **Context:** This is the seventh document of Layer 5 and the forty-sixth of the design library. It is the first document in the library written to serve two consumers in different layers whose requirements are asymmetric in kind rather than in degree, and the first whose own primary deliverable — reaching a person — has a failure mode that cannot be escalated by the mechanism that failed.
- **Decision:** (1) One new Layer 5 design document, because both named candidates disclaim this content and one would additionally force a Layer 2 consumer to depend on a Layer 6 document (§2.1). (2) One delivery mechanism with two notice classes, fixed by the caller and never inferred, differing only in content sufficiency and channel readability (§3.2). (3) The same disclosure risk is closed twice, differently: the self-sufficient class constrains its channel because it cannot constrain its content; the pointer class constrains its content and therefore requires no channel property — and the constraint on the channel is on **reading**, never on addressing (§3.3). (4) The mechanism runs as one of the platform's own shared libraries, the position `03-architecture-realization-design.md` §4.1's own import rule already admits; no component module and no eighth component is added (§4.1). (5) An authority is resolved to the people currently holding it, through the role enumeration and binding structures that already exist; delivery to every holder is not a routing decision, whereas choosing among them would be, and an unresolvable or unheld authority is a refusal, never a default (§4.2). (6) Address resolution precedes rendering, so no credential-bearing notice is ever rendered without somewhere to go (§4.3). (7) The channel is a medium-independent port defined by four required properties, configured and credentialed through the existing injection paths, and no medium is named (§4.4). (8) A pointer notice carries an identifier and nothing else — not even a link, because a link would name an interface this document may not design (§4.5). (9) Delivery is caller-invoked and synchronous, never a sweep, because a sweep would need both a ledger and a cadence no document fixes (§4.6). (10) Handoff is confirmable, receipt is not, and nothing in the mechanism rests on receipt; an undelivered notice never decays into an approval, structurally rather than by policy (§5). (11) One structure is owed — a single current delivery address per identity, described structurally, placed by `02-platform-data-model-design.md`, joining `BACKLOG.md` §1av as its fourth member — and no attempt ledger, retry queue, or per-notice status is owed at all (§6). (12) No `event_type` is minted; two capture points populate the base record directly, and the standing index's count stays at 68 (§9).
- **Alternatives considered:** *Two separate mechanisms, one per consumer* — rejected under criterion 2; everything but two notice properties is shared, and two mechanisms would duplicate the failure posture that §5 exists to state once. *A steward-facing dashboard as the mechanism* — rejected under criterion 3; it cannot reach a recipient who cannot authenticate, and it is an interface this ticket and ADR-060's own scope both exclude. *Extending `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md`* — rejected under criterion 1; it reverses that document's own thrice-stated exclusion and forces a Layer 2 → Layer 6 dependency needing a second cross-layer exception no specification authorizes. *Extending `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md`* — rejected under criterion 1; delivery's inputs are control-plane state, which that document's §9 forbids its own alert-evaluation mechanism to read, and an alert is a signal about the platform rather than an act toward one person. *A delivery-attempt ledger with bounded retry* — rejected under criterion 5; it would need a cadence no document fixes, would answer a question `platform.pending_approvals` and the zero-factor state already answer, and for the enrollment class would store the artifact ADR-067 refused to store. *Treating a channel-reported acknowledgement as receipt* — rejected under criterion 6; it attests to an endpoint's behavior and would record a different fact under the name of the one the obligation is stated in. *Escalating a delivery failure to a human* — rejected as circular (§5.3); escalating is delivering. *Admitting the matched trigger's name into the pointer notice* — rejected under criterion 2's own closure; content-minimality is that class's only protection, and trading it for a convenience would replace a structural property with a recurring disclosure judgment.
- **Consequences:** Confirms, and alters none of, `03-authentication-and-identity-design.md` §3.4 and ADR-067 (the grant, its properties, and the required channel property are consumed exactly as written, and the observation at §4.3 is offered to that document, never imposed on it); `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md`'s trigger-enforcement, request, record, resolution, and no-auto-transition mechanisms; `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1's `platform.pending_approvals`, `platform.steward_role_bindings`, and `tenant_<id>.tenant_users`, each read and none amended; `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md`'s event model and its standing index, whose count is unchanged at 68; `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md`'s configuration and secret-injection paths; `06-compliance-and-data-residency-design.md`'s Region Boundary Check; `07-data-governance-and-privacy-design.md`'s handling matrix; and `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md`, which this decision binds to nothing at all. **Binds `02-platform-data-model-design.md`** to the single delivery-address structure named at §6, for discharge in a future, separately-ticketed amendment, on the identical shape ADR-026, ADR-064, and ADR-066 each used — never designed or provisioned here, but named precisely enough to be actionable — and adds it to `BACKLOG.md` §1av's accumulation as its fourth member. **Binds `implementation-document-map.md`** to one Layer 5 row, made in this ticket under its own authorization; no traceability row is owed, for the reason §2.1 states. **Leaves owed, and does not discharge:** `03-human-in-the-loop-design.md`'s three "no governing document fixes one" disclaimers, whose stated ground no longer holds while their content stands (§2.1, §11). **Adds no `event_type`, no capability, no invariant, no role, no matrix entry, no trigger, no numeric figure, and no residency or classification criterion.**

## 13. Binding Rules

These rules hold for every element of this document and are subordinate to the specification and the charter.

- **Routing determines who; delivery determines that they were reached, and the two are never conflated.** Delivery reads an already-resolved authority and never selects, narrows, or substitutes one; where an authority is held by several people every holder is reached, because choosing among them would be routing.
- **A notice's class is fixed by its caller, and there is no third class and no override.** A self-sufficient notice never travels on a channel lacking recipient-exclusive readability, and a pointer notice never carries content beyond its identifier.
- **The channel constraint is on reading, never on addressing.** An issuing authority may supply the address a notice is sent to; it may never be able to read what arrives there.
- **No medium, product, vendor, provider, or tool is ever named.** The mechanism is defined against a port and four properties; a conforming channel is described, never identified.
- **Handoff is confirmable; receipt is not, and nothing rests on receipt.** No field records it, no check reads it, and no channel report is ever read as establishing it.
- **A refused handoff is never recorded as a discharged obligation.** The refusal is recorded as a refusal, naming which resolution step failed.
- **An undelivered notice never becomes an approval.** Delivery writes no status, transitions nothing, and leaves the pending record untouched; the outstanding state persists, which is the only safe direction.
- **A delivery failure is never escalated by delivering.** No escalation path is designed for this mechanism's own failure, because every candidate re-enters the mechanism that failed.
- **Re-notification is an explicit act against a still-outstanding state, never an automatic one.** No sweep, no queue, no cadence, and no timeout exists anywhere in this mechanism.
- **Delivery holds no state but the one fact it cannot compute.** One current address per identity, described structurally and placed elsewhere; no attempt ledger, no retry record, no per-notice status, and never a stored notice or artifact.
- **No secret and no directly-identifying value ever enters an audit event this mechanism produces.** The address, the rendered notice, and the grant artifact are each excluded by rules that already bind, not by a rule this document mints.
- **Everything here remains domain-neutral and platform-level.** No recipient resolution, notice rule, port property, or failure rule encodes the characteristics of any single domain, and none governs a flow a builder configures inside their own built artifact.
