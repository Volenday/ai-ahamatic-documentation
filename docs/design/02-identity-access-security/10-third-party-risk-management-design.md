# Third-Party Risk Management Design

## 1. Purpose and Reading Order

This document realizes `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` §3–§6 as concrete mechanism: where a vendor or dependency's criticality tier is established and recorded (§3), how ongoing monitoring detects each of the five re-assessment triggers — stated honestly about which are mechanically discoverable and which are not — and where an open trigger is held until resolved (§4), and how an unresolved trigger becomes a blocking check that composes with the platform's existing gate and release mechanisms rather than opening a second one (§5). It answers **how**, never re-deciding the tier definitions, the trigger list, or the release-blocking rule the specification already fixes.

This is the tenth document of the Identity, Access & Security layer, and the closest sibling to `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md`, which it follows in shape — a tiering/admission check, a tracked record, an ongoing re-evaluation trigger, a blocking-check composition — without copying its content: the two mechanisms tier and monitor different things, on different cadences, using different vocabularies, and this document does not force one onto the other.

It is structured as a pyramid: first the tiering mechanism and where a tier is recorded (§3); then the monitoring obligation and the honest limit of what it can detect (§4); then the blocking check the whole model protects (§5); then the evidence this document's controls produce (§6); then the one design decision this document records (§7); then its boundaries, precedence, and binding rules (§8–§10).

**Verified versus reasoned (`PROCESS.md` §12.3).** Every claim in this document is **reasoned** — from the cited specification and the structural properties of the cited, already-written design documents. No claim rests on a vendor's, tool's, or feed's current state; where this document states that a trigger cannot be mechanically detected, that is a structural argument about what a platform-controlled scan can discover, not a claim about any specific vendor's present behavior.

## 2. Scope and What This Document Does Not Own

This document owns: the tiering check as a mechanism, its admission-point placement, and the tracked-record shape holding a vendor's or dependency's resolved tier (§3); the re-assessment monitoring obligation, the mechanical detectability of each of the five named triggers, and the open-trigger record (§4); and an unresolved trigger as a blocking check, including how it composes with the platform's existing Merge Gate, Deploy Gate, and release mechanisms (§5).

This document does **not** own, and does not decide:

- **The four criticality tiers and their defining characteristics, the five re-assessment triggers and why each requires re-assessment, and the release-blocking rule itself.** `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` §4–§5 fixes each; this document consumes them and never restates their content as though it were this document's own rule.
- **License category, combination, attribution, and the license-incompatibility blocking check.** `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` owns all of it in full; this document's tiering check composes with that document's own admission chokepoint (§3.1 below) without redesigning, narrowing, or duplicating anything that document already fixes. A vendor's license status and its criticality tier are independent judgments, exactly as the specification's own §6 states.
- **The technical bounding of a granted capability's reach.** `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/06-integration-and-extensibility-design.md` §5.5 (ADR-033) owns it; this document governs whether a vendor should be trusted at all, never how far a granted scope can technically reach.
- **The four vulnerability-severity classes, the severity-translation step, and the Merge/Deploy Gate's own pass/fail evaluation.** `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1–§4.2 owns the classification step and its composition discipline (a classification step feeds an existing gate, never builds one) — cited here as the discipline this document's own blocking check follows, never as a table this document adds rows to; `02-ci-cd-pipeline-design.md`, `03-testing-and-quality-gates-design.md`, and `05-release-and-rollback-design.md` own the gate and release mechanics themselves in full.
- **The pending-approval record's own shape, storage, and cross-tenant findability.** `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 owns `platform.pending_approvals` in full; this document reuses it and does not redesign any column, its placement, or its update discipline.
- **The enumerated Human-in-the-Loop trigger classes and how a request is routed, recorded, and resolved.** `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §5 owns the closed trigger vocabulary `platform.pending_approvals.matched_trigger` is bound to; this document reasons about which existing trigger class a third-party re-assessment trigger falls under (§4.4 below) and does not add one.
- **The audit-event record shape, storage, immutability, and tamper-evidence mechanism.** `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.2–§4.3 owns all four; this document's evidence (§6) populates that record and extends its event-type index (§4.5), never redesigns either.
- **Numeric thresholds — any monitoring cadence, staleness window, or re-tiering frequency this document's rules might otherwise imply.** `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md` owns any such figure; none is invented here.
- **Escalation routing, recording, and resolution mechanics.** The Meta-Operations documents the specification itself names own this; this document fixes only the condition that halts and escalates.
- **The builder/built separation and generality invariants themselves (INV-06, INV-05).** `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` and `01-business-and-ux/01-vision-and-charter.md` own both; this document preserves them and never restates or narrows them. Every mechanism below governs only what the platform's own primitives, modules, SDK-distributed components, and marketplace offerings incorporate — never a builder's own third-party choices for a built artifact, exactly as the specification's own §3 draws the line.

## 3. The Tiering Mechanism

### 3.1 Composing With the Existing Admission Chokepoint, Never a Second One

The specification requires that every third party it governs (§3 there) is tiered **before incorporation** (§4), at the point a change would incorporate it into the platform's own primitives, a module, an SDK-distributed component, or a marketplace offering. The specification's own §4 states this directly of the extension and marketplace boundary: "the trust boundary a security review protects is the same boundary this tiering protects for vendor risk" — and it is also, by name, the identical boundary `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3–§4 already checks for licensing risk: "a change to the platform's own dependency manifest, and a module, SDK-distributed component, or marketplace offering crossing the extension boundary," the same trust boundary the platform's own security review already protects (that document's own §4, "composing with it rather than opening a second admission point").

**This document's tiering check runs at that identical chokepoint, alongside the license-category check, never at a separate point of its own.** No new admission-time hook is introduced; the tiering determination is evaluated at the same moment a dependency-manifest change or an extension/marketplace submission is already being checked for license category (`09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3) and vulnerability content (`04-security-controls-design.md` §4). A dependency or vendor that has not been tiered does not advance past that same chokepoint — the mechanism-level form of the specification's own binding rule (§7 there): "No third party enters the platform's own primitives, a module, the SDK, or a marketplace offering without a criticality tier (§4) first assigned."

**What the check resolves.** For the specific vendor or dependency being admitted, the check resolves a criticality tier from the four the specification's own §4 table fixes — Foundational, Significant, Limited, or Minimal — by evaluating the consequence of that vendor's compromise, discontinuation, or acquisition against the defining characteristic of each tier. This document does not redefine any tier's own characteristic; it fixes only where and how the resolution runs.

**Uncertainty resolves to the more critical tier.** Where the check cannot establish a tier with confidence, the more critical of the plausible tiers is assigned — the specification's own §4 rule, and the identical deny-by-default discipline the license-category check applies to an unresolved license (`09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3: "uncertainty resolves to the most restrictive applicable category"). No judgment call weighs a vendor's reputation or apparent trustworthiness against this default; an uncertain tier is never assumed to be the less critical one.

### 3.2 The Tracked-Record Shape

For as long as a vendor or dependency remains incorporated, the platform maintains a record of its resolved criticality tier, the basis on which that tier was resolved (including whether it was resolved by default under §3.1's uncertainty rule), and the specific incorporation point it was tiered against (a platform-primitive dependency, a module, an SDK-distributed component, or a marketplace offering) — mirroring, in shape, the generic tracked-dependency record `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §4 already fixes for license category: read by monitoring (§4 below) and by re-tiering (§3.3), never a separate ledger either mechanism maintains independently.

This document does not fix this record's physical schema placement. `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §4's own tracked-dependency record was, at the point that document was written, described structurally without a committed schema table — physical placement was left to whichever ticket next revisited `02-platform-data-model-design.md`, the same pattern every platform-global or per-tenant structure named by a document outside that one's own scope has followed since (`02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1's own recurring "closes the obligation [an upstream document] and ADR-... 's Consequences hand this document" placement pattern, repeated for `platform.pending_approvals`, `platform.marketplace_offerings`, and every sibling structure named there). This document's own tracked-tier record follows the identical restraint, for the identical reason: schema placement is `02-platform-data-model-design.md`'s to fix, not a decision this document's ticket authorizes it to make.

### 3.3 Re-Tiering an Already-Incorporated Vendor

The specification fixes that a vendor's tier is re-evaluated "whenever a change to how the platform incorporates it — a new privileged reach, a new data access, or a new degree of embedding — would plausibly move it to a different tier," and that "a tier assigned at one point is never assumed to hold unchanged as the incorporation itself changes" (both §4). This is a **material change to what is incorporated** — the identical condition the specification also names, by the same words, as one of the five re-assessment triggers (§5's own table: "A new privileged reach, a new data access, or a new degree of embedding can move a vendor to a different tier (§4) and reopens the assessment that set the prior tier").

**One mechanical event, two consequences — not two separate detection mechanisms.** A material change to incorporation is detected exactly the way `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §5 detects a dependency-manifest change: event-driven, at the same admission chokepoint §3.1 above already fixes, whenever that chokepoint fires again for a vendor or dependency already incorporated (a new grant added to `tenant_<id>.extension_registrations.granted_operations`, a new reach recorded against `tenant_<id>.external_system_connections`, or a dependency-manifest entry gaining a materially different linkage). The same event that re-runs the license-category check for the changed incorporation also re-runs the tiering check (§3.1's tier resolution, freshly, never carried forward on assumption) **and** opens a re-assessment-trigger record (§4.4 below) for the "material change" row of the specification's own §5 table — one mechanical detection, feeding both consequences the specification names for it, exactly as the specification's own cross-reference between §4 and §5 already states they are linked.

**No periodic sweep is needed for re-tiering itself.** Unlike license category — which `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §5 (ADR-012's own demonstration) shows can change with zero change to the consumer's own manifest entry, requiring a periodic License Sweep independent of any event — a vendor's criticality tier is, by the specification's own §4 definition, a function of *how the platform incorporates it*, not of the vendor's own external state. Nothing about incorporation changes without an event at the chokepoint that incorporates it; there is no analogous "silent tier drift" the way there is a "silent license drift," so this document adds no periodic tier-resweep. (A vendor's own external state — its trustworthiness, its ownership, its operational health — is exactly what §4 below's monitoring obligation covers, on its own, differently-cadenced mechanism.)

### 3.4 Builder/Built Separation, Applied to Tiering

This tiering mechanism governs only what the platform's own primitives, modules, SDK-distributed components, and marketplace offerings incorporate. A builder's own choice of third-party vendor for their own built artifact is never tiered, never tracked, and never blocked by any mechanism this document fixes — the identical INV-06 boundary the specification's own §3 draws and `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §4's own closing bullet already applies to licensing. Where the same channel that exposes a candidate dependency's license category to a builder (that document's §4) also exposes a criticality tier this document has already resolved for a platform-supplied primitive, module, or marketplace offering, that exposure is informational only, on the identical terms.

## 4. Ongoing Monitoring and Re-Assessment

### 4.1 Monitoring Obligation Scales With Tier

A Foundational- or Significant-tier vendor is monitored on a standing basis for the five triggers below; a Limited- or Minimal-tier vendor is monitored at a proportionately lighter cadence — the specification's own §5 rule. This document fixes the mechanism; the concrete cadence figure for either monitoring posture is a numeric threshold owned by `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md` and is not invented here.

### 4.2 What Can Be Mechanically Detected, and What Cannot — Stated Plainly

The specification names five re-assessment triggers (§5). This document does not overclaim automated detection where none is plausible — the identical honest-limit discipline `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1 applies to its own scanning layer, which "does not commit to, or assert the current state of, any specific vendor or open-source tool," and `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §5 and §8 apply to a re-check trigger and a detection technique's own real limit.

| Trigger (specification §5) | Mechanically Detectable by This Document's Own Mechanism? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A material change to what is incorporated | **Yes — event-driven, at the existing admission chokepoint.** | §3.3 above: the same event that re-runs the license-category check on an already-incorporated dependency also re-opens tiering and this trigger, structurally, with no external signal required. |
| Uncertainty about the vendor's current trustworthiness | **Yes, by mechanical proxy — never as a direct verification of trustworthiness itself.** | The Vendor Monitoring Sweep (§4.3 below) mechanically detects the *absence* of a sufficiently recent assessment for a Foundational- or Significant-tier vendor — an elapsed-time fact the platform can check — and treats that absence as the trigger, per the specification's own deny-by-default framing of this row ("where it cannot be established that a... vendor remains trustworthy, a re-assessment is required"). The sweep never verifies trustworthiness itself; it verifies only that a determination has not gone stale, and treats staleness as exactly what the specification already names it as: unresolved uncertainty. |
| A vendor breach disclosure | **No.** | A disclosed compromise of a vendor's own systems, code, or supply chain is an external fact about that vendor's own environment. No scan the platform runs over its own manifest, code, or infrastructure can discover a fact that occurred entirely outside the platform's own boundary. |
| An ownership or control change | **No.** | An acquisition, merger, or change of control is a corporate fact about the vendor, not a technical property of anything the platform incorporates. No mechanism this document designs observes it. |
| An extended service outage | **No, as a general matter — detectable only for a vendor whose service the platform's own operational monitoring already observes as a live runtime dependency, and this document does not design that monitoring mechanism or assume it exists for every vendor.** | A vendor supplying a library, module, or asset with no runtime service the platform calls has no outage the platform could observe; a vendor supplying an integration or connector the platform's own observability layer already health-checks (owned elsewhere, not this document's mechanism) could in principle surface an outage signal to this document's monitoring, but this document does not build that observation capability and does not claim it exists. |

**The honest limit, stated once rather than re-deferred at each row.** This document's own monitoring mechanism reduces the uncertainty it can see — a material change to incorporation, and staleness against a Foundational- or Significant-tier assessment obligation. It does not, and is not claimed to, close the uncertainty it cannot see: a breach disclosure, an ownership change, or an outage affecting a vendor the platform does not itself observe at runtime. For those three, the mechanism this document designs is an **intake path**, not a detection path (§4.3 below) — exactly the same "detects only what its comparison corpus actually contains" honesty `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §8 states for its own content-similarity technique, applied here to a structurally different, but equally real, detection boundary.

### 4.3 The Vendor Monitoring Sweep and External-Signal Intake

**The sweep.** A periodically scheduled sweep — the **Vendor Monitoring Sweep** — evaluates every currently tracked, incorporated vendor or dependency (§3.2) against two mechanically checkable conditions: whether a material change to its incorporation has occurred since it was last tiered (§3.3, event-driven, already caught independent of the sweep, but re-confirmed here for the same reason the License Sweep re-confirms a category that a manifest-change trigger might have missed); and, for a Foundational- or Significant-tier vendor specifically, whether the elapsed time since its last assessment exceeds the standing-monitoring cadence `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md` fixes. A Limited- or Minimal-tier vendor is swept at its own, lighter cadence, per §4.1.

**External-signal intake.** A vendor breach disclosure, an ownership or control change, and an outage affecting a vendor the platform does not itself observe at runtime are never discovered by the sweep. Each enters this document's mechanism only through an external report — a human actor, a threat-intelligence or vendor-status feed the platform integrates as an ordinary external system connection (`07-cross-system-data-layer-design.md`'s own mechanism, cited and not redesigned here), or an escalation raised through the platform's existing Human-in-the-Loop channel. This document fixes only that such a report, once received by any channel, opens the identical trigger record the sweep itself opens (§4.4 below) — it does not design the reporting channel, the feed integration, or the reporting actor's own interface.

### 4.4 The Open-Trigger Record

**Reusing `platform.pending_approvals`, not a parallel structure.** `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 already fixes the platform-global, cross-tenant-findable record of a human-in-the-loop approval gate or escalation still awaiting resolution — "updatable, unlike the append-only audit stream, because it must remain findable and independently resolvable by a human who was never party to the session that raised it, for as long as it remains unresolved." This is exactly the shape a re-assessment trigger needs held open until resolved, and this document reuses it rather than inventing a second ledger: `approval_id`, `tenant_id` (nullable, populated only where the vendor's incorporation is tenant-scoped — an Extender-authored extension or a tenant's own marketplace obtainment — and `NULL` for a platform-primitive dependency, on the identical nullable-scoping convention that table already establishes), `source_event_reference` (the mechanical event — the Vendor Monitoring Sweep, the material-change re-tiering event, or the external report — that raised the trigger), `required_authority` (the resolved authority owning the vendor-risk decision), `status` (`pending` until an explicit resolution write), and `resolution_event_reference` (nullable; populated once resolved).

**The `matched_trigger` fit, reasoned rather than assumed.** `platform.pending_approvals.matched_trigger` is fixed as "the specific one of the twelve trigger rows `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §5 fixes... a closed vocabulary never platform-configurable." That protocol's twelve enumerated trigger classes do not name a third-party vendor-risk trigger by its own title. This document does not add one — doing so would edit a closed vocabulary neither this document's ticket nor `02-platform-data-model-design.md`'s authorizes it to touch. Reasoned from what both documents already state: this specification's own §1 frames its blocking-check obligation as an operationalization of "the general blocking-check and halt-and-escalate rule of `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §3 for vendor operational risk, a category of harm the enumerated invariants do not separately name," and the Human-in-the-Loop protocol's own "Invariant breach or possible breach" trigger row is itself keyed to that same general rule — "`02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §3, §4," not §4 (the nine numbered invariants) alone. On that textual basis, a third-party re-assessment trigger is recorded under the **"Invariant breach or possible breach"** trigger class as the closest existing fit. **This is stated as a reasoned judgment, not a settled fact**: the Human-in-the-Loop protocol's own enumerated vocabulary does not itself name vendor risk, exactly the imperfect-fit situation `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.3 records rather than forces silently elsewhere in this library. Whether that protocol's own trigger table should carry a distinct row for vendor risk is a question this document does not resolve and does not have scope to resolve; it is flagged here, not fixed, for the same routing `ADR-REGISTER.md` live issue 6 already applies to a comparable gap.

**What the record does not itself decide.** Which specific one of the five re-assessment triggers fired, and for which vendor, is captured in the audit event the record's `source_event_reference` links to (§6 below), not as an additional column on `platform.pending_approvals` this document is not authorized to add. The record's own `status` field is the fact §5 below reads.

## 5. An Unresolved Trigger as a Blocking Check

### 5.1 Blocking in Any Lifecycle Phase

A change that would newly incorporate, or leave incorporated, a vendor or dependency for which a re-assessment trigger has fired and not been resolved does not proceed — the specification's own §5 rule, the halt-and-escalate rule of `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §3 applied to vendor operational risk, in whatever lifecycle phase the trigger is found: design, execution, a module or marketplace submission, or a self-correction attempt. Where an autonomous agent process is the actor attempting the change, this halt is captured under `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §9's minimum-traceability gate exactly as any other halt-and-escalate condition is, without this document redesigning that gate.

### 5.2 An Unresolved Trigger on a Foundational- or Significant-Tier Vendor Blocks Release

**The specification's own vocabulary, never policy §6's.** The release-blocking condition is a direct read of the tracked tier (§3.2) and the open-trigger record's own `status` (§4.4): where any unresolved trigger record names a vendor currently tiered Foundational or Significant, release is blocked. This is the specification's own four-tier vocabulary (Foundational/Significant/Limited/Minimal), evaluated directly and in full by itself — **it is never translated onto, or forced into, `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.2's four vulnerability-severity classes (Critical/High/Moderate/Low)**, the way `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §7 deliberately does translate its own findings onto that scale. That translation was correct for licensing because the specification governing licensing explicitly instructs mapping onto "the existing severity classes, never a new scale" (`02-governance-and-security/08-legal-and-licensing-constraints.md` §7, realized at `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §7). This specification carries no equivalent instruction; its own four tiers already fully determine which unresolved-trigger condition blocks release (Foundational/Significant) and which is tracked, not blocking (Limited/Minimal), with no further classification step needed and no severity axis this document could add without inventing one the specification never asked for.

**An unresolved trigger on a Limited- or Minimal-tier vendor is tracked, not release-blocking.** It is recorded and remediated on a managed path — the same `platform.pending_approvals` record, left `pending` until resolved — and, per the specification's own §5, "may not accumulate into a Foundational- or Significant-tier condition left unresolved." This document does not design the accumulation check as a mechanism distinct from ordinary re-tiering: because §3.3 already re-tiers a vendor fresh on every material change to its incorporation, and because uncertainty about tier resolves to the more critical tier (§3.1), a Limited- or Minimal-tier vendor whose circumstances have genuinely changed enough to threaten accumulation into a higher tier is caught by the same re-tiering mechanism, not a second one.

### 5.3 Composing With the Existing Merge/Deploy Gate and Release Mechanisms — a Further Input, Never a New Condition

**The composition, mirroring licensing's own restraint.** `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §7 states, of its own blocking check: "This document stops at producing the classified finding — the gate's own pass/fail evaluation and the rollback trigger a post-release Critical or High finding calls are `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/03-testing-and-quality-gates.md`'s and `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/05-release-and-rollback-protocol.md`'s own mechanics, cited, never redesigned here." This document's own blocking check stops at the identical point: the binary fact §5.2 above establishes — whether an unresolved trigger exists on a Foundational- or Significant-tier vendor — is a further input the platform's already-designed Merge Gate, Deploy Gates, and release mechanism already consume, exactly as the residency clause and every other check `02-ci-cd-pipeline-design.md` §4–§6 already places at those gates is a further, independent input to a closed-list bullet or an already-existing composite condition. This document does not design, relocate, or add a bullet to any gate's own mandatory-check list; the gate's own pass/fail evaluation, the ordering of checks at it, and the rollback trigger a post-release finding calls remain `02-ci-cd-pipeline-design.md`'s, `03-testing-and-quality-gates-design.md`'s, and `05-release-and-rollback-design.md`'s own mechanics in full — realizing, respectively, `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/02-ci-cd-pipeline-spec.md`, `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/03-testing-and-quality-gates.md`, and `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/05-release-and-rollback-protocol.md`.

**Never a new gate condition of its own — the ADR-022 trap, avoided by name.** `ADR-REGISTER.md` live issue 6 records the exact failure this document does not repeat: a design document's Consequences clause claiming to *bind* a frozen specification's own closed mandatory-check list to grow a new bullet it does not already carry — an authority no design document holds (`CLAUDE.md`'s two-phase rule). This document's own Consequences (§7 below) binds no such thing. It states, and only states, that the Foundational/Significant unresolved-trigger fact is available as an input; consolidating that input into a specific bullet of any gate's own closed list — the same act `02-ci-cd-pipeline-design.md` §4–§6 already performed, one document at a time, for the ten documents it consolidated before this one existed — is that document's own task to take up when it is next revisited, not an obligation this document imposes on it by name.

### 5.4 Escalation and Audit Are Owned Elsewhere

How the halt is carried out, how escalation is requested and routed once the trigger record of §4.4 above is opened, and how the finding and its resolution are logged are owned respectively by the Meta-Operations documents the specification names in its own §6 and by `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` — this document defines the condition that blocks (§5.1–§5.2), not the mechanics that follow it.

## 6. Evidence Produced

`08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5 is the standing, current index of every `event_type` the design library mints — sixty-four rows as of this ticket (2026-08-18). Checked against it under the H26c test before minting anything: a tier assignment and a triggered-and-unresolved re-assessment are each a genuinely distinct fact from anything already indexed — the closest neighbors by name, `tier-default-applied` and `tier-reclassification` (both `06-compliance-and-data-residency-design.md`'s own data-*classification*-tier mechanism), and `dependency-admission-check`/`dependency-license-resweep`/`license-incompatibility-finding` (all `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md`'s own license-category mechanism), each classify a different property of a different thing, under a different vocabulary, than a vendor's *criticality* tier. Neither is reused; two new types are minted, both landing under **Security events** — the same category `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.3 already gives `dependency-admission-check` and `vulnerability-classification`, because a vendor's compromise, discontinuation, or acquisition is, like theirs, an operational-security-relevant finding about a third party the platform incorporates, even though — unlike theirs — it is not classified through `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.2's severity-translation step (§5.2 above).

| `event_type` | `source_mechanism` | `result` records | `outcome` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vendor-tier-assignment` | The tiering check (§3.1), at admission or at a material-change re-tiering event (§3.3). | The resolved tier (Foundational/Significant/Limited/Minimal), whether it was resolved by default under the uncertainty rule (§3.1), and — for a re-tiering — the prior tier it supersedes. | `proceeded` (tier resolved and recorded) — this check never itself refuses an admission; an untiered vendor is refused by the fact that no tier was assigned, not by this event. |
| `vendor-reassessment-trigger` | The Vendor Monitoring Sweep, the material-change re-tiering event, or an external report (§4.3), whichever raised it. | Which of the specification's five §5 triggers fired, and the vendor's tier at the moment it fired. | `held-pending-approval` (Foundational/Significant tier, per §4.3's open-trigger record) or `proceeded` (Limited/Minimal tier, tracked without blocking, §5.2). |

**Resolution reuses an existing type, minting none.** Once a trigger record (§4.4) is resolved, that resolution is captured by `approval-resolution` (`03-human-in-the-loop-design.md` §11, already minted, landing under Authorization and grant events) — the same event type that closes any other `platform.pending_approvals` entry. This document adds no third type for resolution; the record's own `status` transition from `pending` and its `resolution_event_reference` are exactly what that existing event type already captures.

No row above stores the vendor's own identifying or licensed content; `target_reference` identifies the vendor or dependency by opaque reference, per `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.2's own field discipline.

**`08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5 is updated by this ticket** to carry both new rows, per that section's own binding obligation that "a ticket minting a new `event_type` is not complete until a row is added to this section, and this document authorizes that amendment as within its own scope for such a ticket, regardless of which document is the ticket's stated primary subject" — the amendment is made directly to that document's Security events family table, named as this ticket's second `Document(s)` target.

## 7. Design Decision Records

### 7.1 ADR-064 — Vendor-Risk Tiering Composed at the Existing Admission Chokepoint, and the Re-Assessment Trigger Detection Limit, Stated Honestly

- **Status:** Team-Approved.
- **Cost to reverse:** **Low** (`PROCESS.md` §12.1's cheapest rung). Every mechanism this decision fixes is a policy check over a tracked vendor/dependency set, composing with an already-existing admission chokepoint and an already-existing pending-approval record; reversing it removes a check location, a trigger-detection technique, or a record-field mapping, and at worst leaves a vendor that must be re-tiered or re-assessed under the ordinary policy this document already designs. No mechanism this decision fixes destroys, transforms, or irreversibly commits any data or external state.
- **Upstream decisions assumed:** `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` §3–§6 (authoritative, consumed in full); `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3–§4 (the admission chokepoint this decision composes tiering with, never duplicates); `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 (`platform.pending_approvals`, reused as the open-trigger record); `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1 (the "reasoned, not verified" honest-limit discipline this decision applies to detectability, and §4.2's composition discipline — a classification step feeds an existing gate, never builds one — cited as the discipline followed, not as a table this decision adds rows to); `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §5 (the closed twelve-trigger vocabulary `matched_trigger` is bound to, and the reasoned, not stated, fit this decision records for it); `02-ci-cd-pipeline-design.md`, `03-testing-and-quality-gates-design.md`, `05-release-and-rollback-design.md` (the gate and release mechanics this decision's blocking check composes with, never redesigns); `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5 (the event-type index this decision extends by two rows). ADR-026 (`09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §10.1) remains the structural precedent this decision follows in shape, not in content.
- **Verified vs. reasoned:** Entirely reasoned. No claim here is time-sensitive; every finding — the admission-chokepoint composition, the tracked-record restraint on schema placement, the per-trigger detectability table, the Vendor Monitoring Sweep design, the `matched_trigger` fit reasoning, and the direct (untranslated) evaluation of the specification's own tier vocabulary at the blocking check — is reasoned from the cited specification's and cited design documents' own structural properties. No scanning tool, feed, or vendor is named or asserted as verified anywhere in this document.
- **Question this answers:** Given a specification that tiers every incorporated third party by potential harm, names five re-assessment triggers of which several cannot plausibly be discovered by any mechanical scan, and blocks release on an unresolved trigger for its two most critical tiers using its own four-tier vocabulary — never policy §6's four vulnerability-severity classes — what single set of mechanisms realizes tiering, monitoring, and the blocking check without opening a second admission chokepoint, inventing a parallel record to `platform.pending_approvals`, overclaiming detection where none is plausible, or binding a frozen pipeline specification to grow a new mandatory-check condition it does not already carry?
- **Criteria applied, and how each resolved:**
  1. *Compose tiering with the existing admission chokepoint versus open a second one.* Decisive for composing — the specification's own §4 states that "the trust boundary a security review protects is the same boundary this tiering protects for vendor risk," the identical boundary `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §4 already occupies for licensing; opening a second chokepoint would duplicate an already-settled discipline for no realized gain.
  2. *Reuse `platform.pending_approvals` versus a parallel open-trigger ledger.* Decisive for reuse — that table's own fields (`status`, `resolution_event_reference`, cross-tenant findability) already carry exactly the "open until resolved, independently findable" shape the specification's §5 blocking check needs; a second ledger would duplicate `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1's own already-argued case for this table's existence.
  3. *Translate the release-blocking condition onto `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.2's severity scale, mirroring licensing exactly, versus evaluate the specification's own tier vocabulary directly.* Decisive for direct evaluation — licensing's translation is licensing's specification's own explicit instruction ("using the existing severity classes, never a new scale"); this specification carries no equivalent instruction, and its own four tiers already fully determine the blocking outcome without a further classification step. Forcing this specification's vocabulary onto a differently-grounded scale it was never instructed to join would blur two independently defined vocabularies for no realized gain, and is the specific overreach the ticket instructing this document warned against.
  4. *Claim mechanical detection for all five re-assessment triggers versus state the honest limit per trigger.* Decisive for the honest limit — three of five triggers (breach disclosure, ownership/control change, and, for a vendor the platform does not itself observe at runtime, an extended outage) name a fact external to anything the platform's own scan can reach; asserting a detection guarantee the mechanism cannot deliver would repeat the exact overclaiming error `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §8 and `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1 both took explicit care not to make.
- **Context:** This document is the tenth of the Identity, Access & Security layer, realizing `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` in full and composing, by structural necessity, with the two documents most likely to have already claimed the mechanisms it might otherwise duplicate — `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` (the sibling admission-and-re-evaluation mechanism) and `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 (the candidate open-trigger record) — both read and checked for fit before this document committed to its own mechanism, per the ticket's own instruction not to invent either a second admission point or a parallel record.
- **Decision:** (1) The tiering check (§3.1) runs at the identical admission chokepoint `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3–§4 already occupies, never a second one, and resolves one of the specification's own four tiers, defaulting to the more critical tier on uncertainty. (2) The tracked-tier record (§3.2) is described structurally, mirroring the licensing document's own tracked-dependency record, with physical schema placement left open to whichever ticket next revisits `02-platform-data-model-design.md`, exactly as that record's own placement was left open. (3) Re-tiering (§3.3) is event-driven only, at the same chokepoint, with no periodic tier-resweep, because a tier is a function of incorporation, not of the vendor's own external state — unlike license category, which can drift with zero manifest change. (4) Of the specification's five re-assessment triggers, two are mechanically detectable by this document's own mechanism — a material-change event (§3.3) and a staleness proxy for the "uncertainty about trustworthiness" trigger, run by the Vendor Monitoring Sweep (§4.3) — and three are not, entering the mechanism only through external-signal intake, stated honestly rather than overclaimed (§4.2). (5) The open-trigger record reuses `platform.pending_approvals` in full, with a reasoned — not stated as settled — mapping of `matched_trigger` onto the Human-in-the-Loop protocol's existing "Invariant breach or possible breach" trigger class, flagged rather than silently forced (§4.4). (6) The blocking check (§5) evaluates the specification's own tier vocabulary directly, never translated onto `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.2's severity scale, and composes with the Merge Gate, Deploy Gates, and release mechanism as a further input those mechanisms already consume, without binding any of them, by name, to add a new mandatory-check condition — the ADR-022 trap, avoided (§5.3).
- **Alternatives considered:** *A second, dedicated admission chokepoint for vendor-risk tiering, run independently of the license-category check* — rejected under criterion 1; the specification itself names the identical boundary, and a second chokepoint would check the same manifest-and-extension-boundary event twice under two different mechanisms for no stated reason. *A dedicated `platform.vendor_reassessment_triggers` table, purpose-built for this document's own trigger shape* — rejected under criterion 2; `platform.pending_approvals` already carries every field this record needs, and a second ledger would fragment exactly the "one place a human resolves an open gate" property that table exists to provide. *Extending `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.2's severity-translation table with vendor-risk rows, mirroring licensing's own composition exactly* — rejected under criterion 3; that table's four classes measure impact on the platform's own guarantees by a scanner-fed vocabulary this specification was never instructed to join, and forcing a fit would produce a table row with no scanner behind it and a tier system with a redundant, unused second classification. *Claiming the Vendor Monitoring Sweep can detect a vendor breach disclosure or an ownership change, on the reasoning that "some feed might eventually report it automatically"* — rejected under criterion 4; no mechanism this document actually designs performs that detection today, and claiming otherwise would be exactly the overclaiming the specification's own honest-limit discipline, inherited from `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1 and `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §8, forbids.
- **Consequences:** Binds no frozen specification — `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/02-ci-cd-pipeline-spec.md`, `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/03-testing-and-quality-gates.md`, or `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/05-release-and-rollback-protocol.md` — to any new obligation; each is cited as already owning the mechanics this document's blocking check composes with, never redesigned or bound to grow a bullet. Binds `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` to nothing; the `matched_trigger` fit this decision records is a reasoned reading of that document's own existing text, not a claimed edit to it, and is flagged (§4.4) for the Orchestrator's own tracker pass rather than fixed here, on the identical routing `ADR-REGISTER.md` live issue 6 already applies to a comparable gap. Adds two `event_type` values (`vendor-tier-assignment`, `vendor-reassessment-trigger`) to `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.5's Security events family, amended directly by this ticket. Leaves the tracked-tier record's physical schema placement open to a future `02-platform-data-model-design.md` amendment, exactly as `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §4's own tracked-dependency record already does.

## 10. Binding Rules

These rules hold for every actor and every action subject to this model and are subordinate to the charter.

- **A vendor or dependency is tiered before incorporation, at the existing admission chokepoint.** No third party enters the platform's own primitives, a module, the SDK, or a marketplace offering without a criticality tier first assigned; the check runs at the same manifest-change and extension-boundary chokepoint `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3–§4 already occupies, never a second one (§3.1).
- **Tier reflects potential harm, never present trust, and uncertainty resolves to the more critical tier.** No judgment call weighs a vendor's reputation against this default (§3.1).
- **Tier is re-evaluated when incorporation changes, event-driven, with no periodic tier-resweep.** A material change to incorporation both re-tiers the vendor and opens a re-assessment-trigger record in the same mechanical event (§3.3).
- **This document states plainly which of the five re-assessment triggers it can mechanically detect and which it cannot.** A material-change event and a staleness proxy for trustworthiness uncertainty are mechanically detected; a breach disclosure, an ownership or control change, and an outage affecting a vendor not observed at runtime are not, and enter the mechanism only through external-signal intake, never claimed as automated detection (§4.2–§4.3).
- **An open re-assessment trigger is held in `platform.pending_approvals`, reused exactly as `02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1 fixes it, never a parallel structure.** The `matched_trigger` mapping onto that record's closed vocabulary is stated as a reasoned judgment, flagged rather than fixed, never asserted as a settled edit to a document this ticket does not authorize touching (§4.4).
- **A triggered re-assessment is a blocking check in any lifecycle phase; an unresolved trigger on a Foundational- or Significant-tier vendor additionally blocks release, evaluated directly against the specification's own four-tier vocabulary — never translated onto policy §6's four vulnerability-severity classes.** A Limited- or Minimal-tier unresolved trigger is tracked and remediated, never release-blocking on its own, and may not accumulate into a higher-tier condition left unresolved (§5.1–§5.2).
- **The blocking check composes with the existing Merge Gate, Deploy Gates, and release mechanism as a further input those mechanisms already consume, and binds none of them, or the specifications they realize, to grow a new mandatory-check condition.** The ADR-022 trap is not repeated (§5.3).
- **The builder/built separation holds throughout.** This document's mechanism governs only what the platform's own primitives, modules, SDK, and marketplace offerings incorporate; a builder's own choice of third-party vendor for a built artifact is never tiered, tracked, or blocked by anything this document fixes (§3.4).
- **Everything remains domain-neutral and platform-level.** No tier, trigger, or check in this document encodes the characteristics of any single domain, industry, or vendor; all remain valid for any software built on the platform.
- **This document records exactly one ADR.** ADR-064 (§7) is the genuine, independent decision this document makes; every other design choice above consumes a mechanism, record, or boundary the cited specification and cited upstream design documents already fix.
