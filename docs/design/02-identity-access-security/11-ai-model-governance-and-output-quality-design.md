# AI Model Governance and Output-Quality Design

## 1. Purpose and Reading Order

This document realizes `02-governance-and-security/10-ai-model-governance-and-output-quality-standards.md` §4–§5 as concrete mechanism: where the two standing positions that specification requires of a relied-upon AI model are recorded and what keeps them current (§4); how an unresolved model or model-version deprecation is detected — stated honestly about which of its constituent facts are mechanically discoverable and which are not — and how it becomes a blocking check composing with the platform's existing admission chokepoint, gate, and release mechanisms rather than opening a second one (§5); and what the output-quality floor already binds, what would make conformance to it measurable, and why no gate, check, or scoring mechanism is designed for it (§6). It answers **how**, never re-deciding the two positions, the deprecation obligation, the standard's content, or the asymmetry that specification already fixes between an obligation that is a blocking check and one that is deliberately not.

This is the eleventh document of the Identity, Access & Security layer, and the closest sibling to `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md`, whose shape it follows without copying its content — a recorded position, a periodic re-evaluation, an honest per-fact detectability finding, and an unresolved condition composed onto an existing chokepoint. The two mechanisms govern different properties of a differently-defined thing on different evidence, and this document does not force either onto the other.

It is structured as a pyramid: first the document-shape determination this ticket asked to be made rather than assumed, and the boundaries it fixes (§2); then what is already governed about the model dependency and what residue this document actually owns (§3); then the two recorded positions (§4); then deprecation handling as a blocking check (§5); then the output-quality floor and the honest finding about its measurability (§6); then the evidence this document's controls produce (§7); then the one design decision it records (§8); then its boundaries, precedence, and binding rules (§9–§11).

**Every `§N` cited below was resolved against the section it names before being relied upon, per `BACKLOG.md` §1t's standing rule, and no string appears in quotation marks unless read verbatim in the cited file.** One correction is recorded: `ADR-REGISTER.md` lists **ADR-064** as its highest registered identifier, but **ADR-065** is already minted in the library — `02-tenant-isolation-and-access-control-design.md` §10.5, recorded by the tenant-owner-admission ticket and still awaiting its register entry — so the next genuinely free identifier is **ADR-066**, used at §8 below. `01-business-and-ux/06-value-proposition-and-success-metrics.md` §5 was read in full and its guardrail-metric table confirmed as a closed set of seven rows, each mapped to a release gate or to the charter's builder / built separation; that finding is load-bearing at §6.4 and is stated there rather than assumed.

**Verified versus reasoned (`PROCESS.md` §12.3).** Every claim in this document is **reasoned** — from the cited specification and the structural properties of the cited, already-written design documents. **No model, vendor, or agent framework is named anywhere below, and no claim asserts any provider's current interface, announcement practice, deprecation cadence, or model behavior.** Where this document states that a fact cannot be mechanically detected, that is a structural argument about what a platform-controlled check can reach, not a claim about any provider's present conduct.

## 2. Scope, the Document-Shape Determination, and What This Document Does Not Own

### 2.1 Why One New Document, and Not an Extension to Either Named Anchor

The ticket authorizing this work named `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` and `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` as the natural candidate anchors and left the shape to be determined by investigation. Both were read in full, including their own ADRs. **The determination is one new document, for four reasons stated here rather than in the handoff alone.**

- **Each anchor's own stated boundary excludes this content, in its own words.** `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`'s front matter states that "every mechanism fixed here governs the suggestion's *handling*, never its *quality*", and its §2 closing bullet excludes "Any specific model, vendor, or agent framework" from its scope outright. `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.3 states that a misled, low-quality suggestion "remains a possible outcome of a probabilistic interpretation process, and no mechanism in this document eliminates that possibility." and, in the same section, that "The residual risk this design accepts is a bad *proposal*". Appending either obligation to either document would require reversing a boundary that document states about itself, re-opening ADR-056's or ADR-022's own Consequences to do it. The specification realized here reached the same conclusion from the specification side (§2 there), and its reasoning is consumed, not re-derived.
- **One specification document, two obligations, one shared scope statement.** That document's §3 fixes four scope properties shared by both obligations — both attach to C-19 and never to C-26, both are platform-level rather than tenant-differentiated, both bind every lifecycle phase, both are domain-neutral. Splitting the realization across two documents in two layers would restate that shared scope twice and would separate §5's *this is not a gate* finding from §4's *this is a blocking check* finding, where the contrast between them is the clearest thing either has to say.
- **The nearest sibling by mechanism shape is in this layer, not in Layer 3.** Every mechanism §4–§5 below composes with is a Layer 2 or Layer 5 mechanism: the admission chokepoint `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3–§4 fixes and `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §3.1 already occupies, that document's tracked-record restraint, the manifested review-trigger path `04-security-controls-design.md` §5.2 fixes, and the Merge Gate, Deploy Gates, and release mechanism. Nothing below designs a construction primitive, a suggestion, an assistance kind, or a contract operation — the concerns Layer 3's tooling document owns.
- **The precedent is immediate and exact.** `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` realized a newly-written Governance & Security specification document 1:1 as a new Layer 2 design document, even though nearly every mechanism in it composes with a chokepoint, record, or gate another document already owned. This document is the same shape of work on the same layer, one document later.

**One consequence is owed and not discharged here.** `implementation-document-map.md`'s Layer 2 table ends at `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` and carries no row for `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` either; the index is two documents behind. Adding both rows is tracker maintenance under `PROCESS.md` §7, outside a design document's own authority to perform on the index that governs it, and is flagged at §9 rather than silently left.

## 3. What Is Already Governed About the Model Dependency, and What Residue Remains

### 3.1 Four Obligations Already Attach to It, Unchanged

The specification's own §4.3 states that its continuity obligation is "independent of and in addition to" third-party risk management, never a restatement of it. This section states, concretely, what already attaches — so that §4–§5 design only the residue and nothing already built is duplicated.

| Already Governed | By | What It Establishes |
|---|---|---|
| The model provider as an incorporated third party — tiered before incorporation, re-tiered on a material change to how the platform incorporates it, monitored against five re-assessment triggers, and release-blocking while a trigger on a Foundational- or Significant-tier vendor stands unresolved | `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` §3–§5; `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §3–§5 | A model provider whose model powers the AI-assisted builder tooling is a vendor "incorporated into the platform's own primitives" in that specification's own §3 sense, and is already tiered and monitored as one. **This document adds nothing to that mechanism and depends on none of its outputs.** |
| The tooling's model-access credential | `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §6; `04-security-controls-design.md` §2.1–§2.2; `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §5 | A secret with no exemption — never embedded, never emitted, scanned and redacted on the identical paths every other credential is. |
| The mechanism that assembles a request, invokes an underlying model, and structures the tooling's system-level instructions to it | `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §6 | Governed platform-core code, subject to the same access-control, review, and change-management discipline as any other platform-core module, and a **manifested security-review-trigger path** under `04-security-controls-design.md` §5.2 — so a change to it already fires the mandatory security review and already matches a charter §7 approval-required class (`01-agent-runtime-and-control-design.md` §4.1's security-review-trigger row). |
| Every suggestion that mechanism's output becomes | `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §4–§5; `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §4–§5 | Staged, never committed without the mandatory human-builder confirmation, and re-cleared against security validation on the content actually submitted. |

**The residue is exactly three things, and they are what §4–§6 design:** that the *basis* on which a model was judged fit is recorded and kept current; that dependence on one provider is *bounded* by a stated structural position rather than by hope; and that a provider-announced sunset of a relied-upon model or version has a defined transition path *before* its effective date, enforced as a blocking check. Nothing in the table above establishes any of the three, and none of them is one of the five re-assessment triggers the fourth row's mechanism monitors.

### 3.2 The Transition Itself Is Already Gated — Only Its Absence Is Not

A transition to a successor model or version is a change to the tooling's own model-invocation mechanism and configuration. By the third row above, such a change already fires the mandatory security review and already routes to human approval as an ordinary charter §7 match; and if it reaches platform-core source, it clears the identical Merge Gate every other change clears (`01-agent-runtime-and-control-design.md` §9.2). **This document therefore designs no approval path, no gate, and no ceremony for performing a transition** — that path exists and is used unchanged. What no existing mechanism does is notice that a transition is *owed and missing*, and stop other work while that is true. That, and only that, is §5's subject.

### 3.3 The Dependency's Standing Under the Portable-Subset Rule, Stated Rather Than Assumed

ADR-010 (`01-technology-stack-design.md` §20.1) fixes that the platform "adopts no provider-unique managed service for anything on which correctness depends." A dependence on an external model provider invites the question whether that rule is breached, and this document answers it directly rather than leaving it implicit, because the answer is the structural ground §4.3's lock-in position stands on.

**No platform guarantee's correctness depends on the model.** `01-application-construction-design.md` §2 fixes that the build surface "assumes neither the tooling's presence nor its absence," and its §8 fixes that a confirmed suggestion's committed write is "indistinguishable from one the builder performed directly." Every construct, binding, entity, schema element, and process-definition element the tooling can suggest is authorable directly through the identical mechanism, and no invariant, gate, guarantee, or built application's runtime reads anything the model produced (`09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §3). The model dependency is therefore **a capability-availability dependency, not a correctness dependency** — it falls on the same side of ADR-010's own line as the infrastructure configuration that document already accepts as provider-specific-but-necessary, never on the correctness side its subset rule protects.

**This is a narrower finding than portability, and is not stated as one.** ADR-010 records its own stated non-goal at §20.3 there — that its rule "does not make the platform trivially portable between providers", and that any move "is an infrastructure exercise that must be budgeted as such rather than assumed away." The identical qualification holds here and is repeated at §4.3: the reason a model dependency does not breach the subset rule is that C-19's assistance can be unavailable without any platform guarantee failing — which is precisely why the specification requires a transition path rather than treating the dependency as expendable.

## 4. The Two Recorded Positions

### 4.1 Where a Position Is Recorded — the Tracked Model-Dependency Record

For as long as the tooling relies on a given model or model version, the platform maintains a record carrying, at minimum:

- **An opaque reference to the relied-upon model dependency** — the model and version identifier as a recorded value, on the identical opaque-reference discipline `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §4.2 already fixes for `target_reference`. This document records that such a value is held; it names none.
- **The selection-criteria position** (§4.2) — the recorded basis on which the dependency was judged fit, and the date that basis was last confirmed.
- **The lock-in-mitigation position** (§4.3) — the recorded statement of how dependence on the provider is structurally bounded, and the date it was last confirmed.
- **The deprecation state** — `none-announced` or `announced`; where `announced`, the announced effective date and an opaque reference to the intake through which the announcement entered (§5.1).
- **The transition-path state** — whether a defined transition path to a successor model or version is recorded, and an opaque reference to it where one is.

**This document does not fix the record's physical schema placement**, on the identical restraint `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §3.2 applies to its own tracked-tier record and `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §4 applies to its tracked-dependency record: placement is `02-platform-data-model-design.md`'s to fix, in whichever ticket next revisits it, and is not a decision this document's ticket authorizes it to make. This is the third record of that shape to be described structurally and left unplaced, and it is deliberately the third instance of an established pattern rather than a new one.

**Where the record is read and reconciled.** The relied-upon model and version reach a running deployment as ordinary non-secret configuration, injected through the single path `01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §4.2 fixes ("No third path exists"); the credential accompanying them is a secret on §5's path there, unchanged. The record above is reconciled against that injected configuration at the occasion §5.2 fixes — so a model relied upon in configuration but absent from the record, or recorded but no longer relied upon, is a detectable discrepancy rather than a silent one.

### 4.2 The Selection-Criteria Position, Realized

The record's selection-criteria entry states the basis on which the dependency was judged fit for this tooling's purpose, against the criterion categories the specification's own §4.1 names — fitness for the four assistance kinds C-19 fixes, data-handling and confidentiality posture, latency and availability characteristics, and compatibility with the mandatory human-builder confirmation architecture. **The entry records a determination; it is never a score, a ranking, a weighting, or a comparison table**, and this document neither enumerates a complete criteria set nor assigns any criterion a weight — the specification reserves the first to a future criteria-library or design-phase concern and forbids the second outright.

**The honest limit, stated at the point it applies rather than deferred.** What a mechanism can establish about this entry is that it exists, that it addresses each named category, and that its last confirmation has not gone stale (§5.1). What no mechanism this document designs can establish is that the criteria are the *right* criteria, or that the chosen model actually satisfies them — both are human determinations the record holds, never computed results the platform verifies. Claiming otherwise would repeat exactly the overclaiming `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1 and `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §4.2 each took explicit care not to make.

### 4.3 The Lock-In-Mitigation Position, Realized as a Single Invocation Seam

The specification requires a position stated "in structural terms" on how dependence on a single provider is mitigated, and explicitly leaves the abstraction technique to this phase. **The technique is a single invocation seam, with three properties:**

- **Exactly one mechanism in the platform invokes a model.** The mechanism `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §6 already names — the one that assembles a request, invokes an underlying model, and structures the tooling's system-level instructions — is the only such mechanism, and no second invocation path exists anywhere in platform-core code, in `components/*`, or in the agent kernel. A generation action reaches a model through that seam or not at all.
- **Every provider-specific concern is confined behind it.** The request encoding, the endpoint, the credential reference, and any provider-specific parameter shape live inside the seam; nothing outside it holds a provider-specific assumption, and no platform mechanism outside it branches on which provider or model is in use.
- **No provider-unique capability is depended upon for correctness.** This is ADR-010's own subset rule applied at the seam, and it holds for the reason §3.3 establishes independently: no platform guarantee's correctness depends on the model at all, so no correctness dependency on a provider-unique capability can arise through the seam without breaching that finding first.

**Where the seam physically lives is not fixed here, deliberately.** `03-architecture-realization-design.md` §3.1 fixes exactly seven component modules plus `guardrail/`, and its §5.1 three-layer table records builder tooling as "Not a separate module" — a contract-level distinction over three of them, in that row's own words; `01-agent-runtime-and-control-design.md` §9.1 fixes that the agent kernel is not source code inside any `components/*` module and does not become an eighth entry. Assigning the seam a module placement is therefore those two documents' concern, and this document fixes only the singularity and confinement properties above, which hold at any placement either would choose.

**What a seam buys, and what it does not — stated because overclaiming here would be the easiest error in this document.** A seam bounds the *code* a provider change touches: one mechanism's internals, plus the already-existing gates that mechanism's change already clears (§3.2). It does not make a provider change free, and this document does not claim it does. A different model produces different suggestions from the same request and the same assembled context; no interface boundary preserves what a model proposes, only where the call is made from. The transition path the specification requires (§5) is what covers the behavioral half; the seam covers only the structural half, and the two are not substitutes.

### 4.4 Neither Position Is Release-Blocking, and What an Absent or Stale Position Does Instead

The specification's own §4.2 fixes that the selection-criteria and lock-in-mitigation positions "are not, by themselves, release-blocking checks in the same sense — their absence is a governance gap to remediate on a managed path, not a condition that halts an in-flight change on its own." This document does not strengthen that into a block, and does not weaken it into an advisory note either.

**Concretely:** an absent or stale position is found by the evaluation of §5.2, recorded as evidence (§7), and remediated on a managed path — the identical treatment `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §5.2 gives an unresolved trigger on a Limited- or Minimal-tier vendor: that section states it is "recorded and remediated on a managed path" and that document's own §10 binding rule states such a trigger is "never release-blocking on its own". It does not halt an in-flight change, does not enter any gate's evaluation, and does not accumulate into the blocking condition of §5.3 — that condition reads only the deprecation and transition-path states, and no absence of a position can satisfy it.

**One asymmetry is worth naming, because it looks like an inconsistency and is not.** A missing *position* is not blocking; a missing *transition path* against an announced deprecation is. The difference is that the second names a dated, external event already in motion whose arrival will remove the capability whether or not the platform prepared for it, and the first does not. That distinction is the specification's own (§4.1 versus §4.2 there), consumed here rather than re-argued.

## 5. Deprecation Handling as a Blocking Check

### 5.1 What Can Be Mechanically Detected, and What Cannot

The blocking condition the specification fixes — a provider-announced sunset with no defined transition path before the effective date — is not one fact but four, and they do not share a detectability answer. This document states each rather than asserting detection for the condition as a whole, on the identical honest-limit discipline `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §4.2 applies to its own five triggers and `04-security-controls-design.md` §4.1 applies to its own scanning layer.

| Constituent Fact | Mechanically Detectable by This Document's Own Mechanism? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Which model and version the tooling currently relies upon | **Yes — by reconciliation, at the occasion §5.2 fixes.** | Both sides are platform-controlled: the injected non-secret configuration value (`01-environment-and-configuration-design.md` §4.2) and the tracked record (§4.1). A discrepancy in either direction is a comparison, not a judgment. |
| Whether a selection-criteria or lock-in-mitigation position exists, and whether its last confirmation has gone stale | **Yes, by an elapsed-time proxy — never as a verification that the position is sound.** | The same mechanical proxy `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §4.2 uses for its own "Uncertainty about the vendor's current trustworthiness" row: an elapsed-time fact over a record the platform holds. What the proxy establishes is that a determination has not gone stale, never that it was correct (§4.2 above). |
| Whether a provider has announced a deprecation or sunset of a relied-upon model or version, and its effective date | **No.** | An external fact about a provider's own communications, occurring entirely outside every surface a platform-controlled scan reaches — structurally identical to the vendor-breach-disclosure and ownership-change rows `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §4.2 states it cannot detect. Where a provider's own interface happens to expose deprecation metadata, consuming it would be an intake path, not a detection guarantee; this document does not design that consumption, does not claim any provider offers it, and does not treat its possible existence as reducing this row's answer. |
| Whether a defined transition path is recorded before the announced effective date | **Yes — a recorded-fact-and-date comparison over the record of §4.1.** | Once an announcement has entered by intake, both operands are held by the platform: the transition-path state and the announced effective date. This is what makes the blocking check of §5.3 enforceable at all rather than aspirational. |

**The honest limit, stated once and sharply: the one fact that starts the obligation is the one the platform cannot see.** Three of the four constituent facts are mechanical; the announcement that makes them matter is not. The blocking check below is fully enforceable *given* an announcement, and entirely inert without one — so its force rests on the intake path being exercised, not on any detection this document designs. This is the whole of the mechanism's exposure, and it is recorded here rather than distributed across §5.2–§5.3 where it could be read as smaller than it is.

**External-signal intake.** An announcement enters this mechanism only through an external report — a human actor, a provider-status or vendor-communication feed integrated as an ordinary external system connection (`07-cross-system-data-layer-design.md`'s own mechanism, cited and not redesigned), or an escalation raised through the platform's existing Human-in-the-Loop channel. This document fixes only that such a report, received through any channel, sets the record's deprecation state and effective date (§4.1); it designs no reporting channel, no feed integration, and no reporting actor's interface.

### 5.2 The Model Continuity Evaluation, and Why It Is Its Own Occasion

**The occasion.** A periodically scheduled evaluation — the **Model Continuity Evaluation** — runs over every model or version currently recorded as relied upon (§4.1) and establishes the three mechanically detectable facts of §5.1: configuration-to-record reconciliation, position presence and staleness, and, for any dependency whose deprecation state is `announced`, whether a transition path is recorded and whether the announced effective date has arrived. It additionally runs at the admission chokepoint `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §3–§4 fixes and `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §3.1 already occupies, whenever that chokepoint fires for a change that newly incorporates a model dependency or materially changes how one is incorporated — the same event-driven-plus-periodic pairing that document already establishes, never a second admission point of its own. Its cadence is a numeric threshold owned by `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`; none is invented here.

**Why this is its own evaluation and not a condition inside the Vendor Monitoring Sweep, stated plainly.** The model provider is already swept by `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §4.3's Vendor Monitoring Sweep as an incorporated vendor, and folding these reads into that same sweep would be the more economical mechanism. This document does not do it, for one reason: that sweep's conditions are that document's own, and adding one to it is precisely the act `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §5.3 names and refuses for itself — binding another document, by name, to grow a condition its own closed mechanism does not carry. **This document therefore states, and only states, that consolidating the two occasions is an available and legitimate simplification for whichever ticket next revisits that document, and imposes no obligation on it to take that up.** The cost of the restraint is one additional scheduled evaluation; the cost of the alternative would be a binding this phase's own rules forbid.

### 5.3 The Blocking Check — a Further Input to Mechanisms That Already Consume Them

**The condition.** A change does not proceed while any model or version the tooling relies upon has deprecation state `announced` and no recorded transition path. This is the specification's own §4.2 rule — the halt-and-escalate rule of `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §3, applied to AI-model continuity risk — and it holds in whatever lifecycle phase the condition is found: design, execution, a platform-core change, or a self-correction attempt. **Uncertainty resolves toward treating the deprecation as unresolved**, per that same section: where it cannot be established that a transition path exists, none does for this check's purposes.

**The composition, and its deliberate limit.** The condition above is a binary fact read from the record of §4.1. It is a **further input the platform's already-designed Merge Gate, Deploy Gates, and release mechanism already consume**, on the identical composition `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` §5.3 makes for its own unresolved-trigger fact and `09-licensing-and-dependency-compliance-design.md` §7 makes for its own classified finding. This document does not design, relocate, reorder, or add a bullet to any gate's own mandatory-check list; the gate's own pass/fail evaluation, the ordering of checks at it, and the rollback trigger a post-release finding calls remain `02-ci-cd-pipeline-design.md`'s, `03-testing-and-quality-gates-design.md`'s, and `05-release-and-rollback-design.md`'s in full. **The ADR-022 trap is avoided by name:** `ADR-REGISTER.md` live issue 6 records what it costs to have a design document's Consequences claim to bind a frozen specification's closed mandatory-check list to grow an item it does not carry, and §8's Consequences below bind no such thing.

**No sixth kernel precondition, and no new classification row.** Where an autonomous agent process is the actor attempting a change, the halt is the ordinary halt-and-escalate duty already built, captured under `08-audit-and-traceability-design.md` §9's minimum-traceability gate exactly as any other halt-and-escalate condition is. This document does **not** add a sixth precondition to `01-agent-runtime-and-control-design.md` §5.1's five, which are charter §5's and closed, and does **not** add a row to §4.1's classification table, which is charter §6–§7's and closed. Where a change is itself a transition to a successor model, it already matches that table's existing security-review-trigger row through the manifested path §3.1 establishes — an existing match, not one this document creates.

### 5.4 What the Blocking Check Does Not Block

**It does not refuse an individual generation action, and does not disable the tooling.** The specification blocks *changes* — "no release proceeds" — never the tooling's own invocation of a model that is still serving requests. A generation action against a model with an unresolved deprecation still ahead of its effective date remains an ordinary kernel-governed action, clearing exactly the checks `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §6.2 fixes, and nothing in this document adds a precondition to it. Designing a per-invocation refusal would widen an obligation the specification states about change advancement into one about capability availability, and would degrade C-19 in exactly the phase during which the transition is supposed to be prepared.

**What happens after the effective date passes with no transition path is not a mechanism this document invents.** The capability becomes unavailable because the provider stopped serving it — an outcome the platform's own mechanisms cannot prevent and this document does not pretend to. The blocking check exists to make that outcome a discovered-and-halted condition beforehand rather than a surprise at the moment of failure, which is the specification's own stated purpose for it; it does not exist to keep a sunset model serving. Every guarantee the platform holds survives that unavailability, for the reason §3.3 establishes.

### 5.5 The Escalation Route — a Gap Named, Not Forced

A halted, unresolved deprecation escalates. Where an escalation is held open as a findable, independently-resolvable record, the platform's existing structure for it is `platform.pending_approvals` (`02-platform-data-model-design.md` §3.1), whose `matched_trigger` column is bound to the closed trigger vocabulary `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md` §5 fixes — a vocabulary that document states is owned per row by the document named on it, "which alone defines when it fires."

**No row in that vocabulary covers this condition, and this document does not force one onto it.** The three rows that come closest were each resolved and each rejected: the *third-party vendor risk trigger* row's own condition is an unresolved re-assessment trigger on a tiered vendor, and the specification realized here states in its own §2 and §4.2 that deprecation-handling is not among the five triggers that owning document enumerates; the *backward-compatibility break or managed deprecation* row governs the platform's own announced deprecation path under INV-09 and `03-software-and-architecture/04-api-contract-spec.md`, which is a different subject reached by the same English word; and the *invariant breach or possible breach* row does not fire because the specification states plainly that model-continuity risk is "a category of harm the enumerated invariants do not separately name."

**This is a specification gap, routed rather than asserted.** `PROCESS.md` §12.2 fixes that an obligation a document does not already carry is a specification gap to route to a `T##` ticket, never a binding a design document may impose — and the precedent is exact: `BACKLOG.md` §1ap records that `10-third-party-risk-management-design.md` originally recorded a reasoned best-fit mapping onto the *invariant breach* row precisely because no dedicated row existed, and that the mapping was superseded once a specification ticket added one. This document declines to repeat the interim step. **The blocking check of §5.3 does not depend on the resolution:** it reads the record of §4.1 directly, and halts and escalates through the ordinary duty §5.3 names, whose routing mechanics are the Meta-Operations documents' in full. What is owed is a dedicated trigger row so that an escalation of this condition can be held under a `matched_trigger` value that means what it says; that is flagged at §9 for a `T##`, not decided here.

## 6. The Output-Quality Floor

### 6.1 What the Standard Binds Today, With No New Mechanism

The specification's §5.1 standard — that an AI-suggested artifact reflect a good-faith, context-grounded attempt at the request it responds to, reasoned from the builder's own request and the application, construction-primitive, and entity/schema context the generation action assembles, and not knowingly present as complete or fit-for-purpose content its own available context already indicates is inconsistent, incomplete, or non-functional — attaches to the generation action `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §4.2 fixes. **It is inherited by that action as a standing obligation on its conduct, and this document adds no mechanism to make it so**, because two mechanisms already fix everything the standard's own terms refer to:

- **The context the standard measures groundedness against is already assembled, and already sufficient by rule.** `04-prompt-and-context-assembly-design.md` §5 fixes the assembly rule — keyed to the identical declared shape classification reads, gathering the union where a task spans types, with sufficiency held as a floor its §5.3 realizes as the gathering rule's own stopping condition — assembly "gathers no further document merely because it is available or plausibly relevant" — and `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §6.2 fixes which of that document's closed task-type rows a generation action assembles against, plus the design-level construction-primitive content it additionally assembles because "a generation action cannot compose valid candidate content without holding the primitives it is composing in view." A standard stated at the level of "the request-and-context it already has available to it" is therefore stated against an already-fixed, already-bounded input set. Nothing further is needed to give the standard a subject.
- **Missing or stale context is already a halt, not a quality question.** `04-prompt-and-context-assembly-design.md` §8 fixes staleness detection mechanically and its §7.3 fixes that unavoidable overflow halts rather than proceeding reduced. The failure mode in which a suggestion is ungrounded *because its context was absent or stale* is therefore already prevented upstream by a mechanism that halts, and is not a residue this standard has to catch.

**What remains after both is the residue the standard names and the specification declines to close: a suggestion that is poor despite adequate, current context.** That residue is exactly the accepted residual risk `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.3 states in its own words, and the specification's §5.2 is explicit that it "does not eliminate it either, and does not claim to."

### 6.2 Why No Gate, Check, or Scoring Mechanism Is Designed

The specification's §5.3 forecloses a quality gate, a check, an evaluation harness, and a scoring mechanism by name, and its §5.2 states that the standard "is not, and does not become, a second confirmation gate, a scoring mechanism, or a blocking check on any individual suggestion." This document designs none of the four, and the reason is structural rather than deferential:

- **A per-suggestion judgment would be a second correctness authority beside the one gate that owns it.** The mandatory human-builder confirmation gate `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §5 owns is, in the specification's own words, "the sole correctness backstop no output-quality floor may substitute for or weaken." Any mechanism that scored, delayed, or auto-rejected a suggestion before the builder saw it would take a share of that authority — and would take it on the strength of a determination about content, which is the one thing `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.2's channel separation fixes as never authority-bearing.
- **Nothing in the platform is positioned to make the judgment.** Deciding whether a suggestion is a good-faith, context-grounded attempt at the builder's own request is a judgment about the fit between a request, an assembled context, and proposed content. The only actor in the design that holds all three and is not the producer of the third is the confirming builder, which is the gate that already exists.

### 6.3 What Would Make Conformance Measurable, and the Honest Finding

The specification leaves open whether conformance is ever made mechanically checkable, and this document answers the design half of that question directly: **no direct measurement of §5.1's standard is constructible from the standard as stated.** A direct measurement would require evaluating groundedness per suggestion, which §6.2 establishes nothing is positioned to do. This is stated as a finding, not as a deferral.

**What is constructible is a proxy family, measurable entirely from already-designed state and adding no structure.** `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §9.1's `tenant_<id>.ai_suggestions` record already carries, per suggestion, the assistance kind, the disposition (`pending`/`confirmed`/`discarded`, never reopened), the resolving actor and time, and — through the confirmation mechanism of §5.4 there — whether security validation failed on the content actually submitted. Three aggregate quantities follow from those columns with no new field, no new event, and no new mechanism: the confirm-versus-discard split per assistance kind; the share of confirmations whose submitted content differed from the generated content; and the rate at which a confirmation's own security re-validation refuses the submitted content.

**Each of the three is a weak proxy, and each weakness is named rather than glossed.** A discard is not evidence of a quality failure — a builder may discard a sound suggestion because they changed their mind, or confirm a mediocre one after editing it heavily. An edit before confirmation is the workflow behaving exactly as designed, not a defect signal. A security-validation refusal measures a defect category `02-governance-and-security/02-security-policy.md` §3.3 owns, which the specification's own §2 states is "never correctness or fitness for the builder's own request." **The three are therefore directional at aggregate only, attributable to no individual suggestion, and are stated here as what a future ticket would have available if it decided to track the floor — never as a measurement of the standard, and never as a mechanism this document establishes.** No collection, no dashboard, no threshold, and no tracking obligation is designed; whether any of the three is ever tracked is that future ticket's decision, exactly as the specification reserves it.

**Tenant-boundary and neutrality properties, since the record is per-tenant.** Any aggregate over `ai_suggestions` computed across tenants is subject to the identical rule `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` §3.5 already fixes for every aggregate signal — computed only over values carrying no tenant-identifying content, never by summing tenant-scoped values into a shape from which one tenant's contribution could be isolated. And the proxies are structurally domain-neutral: `assistance_kind` is the closed four-value vocabulary `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §7 fixes, and a disposition is a closed three-value vocabulary, so no aggregate over either can carry domain content — the by-construction form of the specification's own §5.4 neutrality rule.

### 6.4 No Guardrail-Metric Identifier, and No Numeric Floor Deferred

Two consequences follow from §6.3 that would be easy to state wrongly, and both are stated precisely.

**The floor is guardrail-metric-*shaped*, and is not a guardrail metric in the library's own enumerated sense.** The specification places it "on the guardrail-metric side of the three-instrument separation `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §2 already draws" — a shape claim, correctly made. But `01-business-and-ux/06-value-proposition-and-success-metrics.md` §5's guardrail-metric table is a closed set of seven rows, each mapped to a release gate G-1–G-6 or to the charter's builder / built separation, and no design document may add a row to it. That has one concrete mechanical consequence: `04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md` §4.1 fixes that every signal's `severity_source` tag names an invariant identifier, a **guardrail-metric identifier from that closed table**, a vulnerability class, a non-functional-target identifier, an anti-metric identifier, or none. **There is no guardrail-metric identifier for this floor to be tagged with**, so were any of §6.3's proxies ever instrumented, it would carry `severity_source` = none — a monitoring-signal-only measurement, never a guardrail-tagged one. Stating it as a guardrail metric in that document's own sense would collapse the three-instrument distinction `PROCESS.md` §11 forbids collapsing, in the direction that looks most like rigor.

**This document defers no numeric value, deliberately.** `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md` §10's table records the numeric floors other documents defer to it, and §9.2 there confirms every enumerated guardrail metric is already quantified at exactly zero. This document adds no row to that table and hands that document no figure, because deferring one would presuppose a measurement this document finds is not constructible (§6.3) — and a numeric floor over a proxy that cannot be attributed to any individual suggestion would be a number with nothing behind it. Should a future ticket decide a quantified floor over a tracked proxy is warranted, the number is that document's to own, on the specification's own §5.3 terms; it is not owed to it now, and its absence here is a finding rather than an omission.

## 11. Binding Rules

These rules hold for every actor and every action subject to this document and are subordinate to the charter.

- **No model, vendor, or agent framework is named anywhere in this document, and every mechanism above is generic** — a record, an occasion, a seam, a check, a floor — never a product-specific integration.
- **A relied-upon model's selection-criteria and lock-in-mitigation positions are recorded and kept current, never recorded once and left stale.** Their presence and currency are mechanically checkable; the soundness of their content is not, and is never claimed to be (§4.2).
- **Every model invocation passes through exactly one seam, behind which every provider-specific concern is confined, and no provider-unique capability is depended on for correctness.** The seam bounds the code a provider change touches; it preserves nothing about what a different model proposes, and is never described as though it did (§4.3).
- **The model dependency is a capability-availability dependency, not a correctness dependency.** No platform guarantee, invariant, gate, or built application's runtime depends on it, which is the finding that keeps it inside ADR-010's rule and the reason the specification requires a transition path rather than treating the dependency as expendable (§3.3).
- **An unresolved model or model-version deprecation is a blocking check in any lifecycle phase, and uncertainty resolves toward treating the deprecation as unresolved.** An absent or stale recorded position is not blocking; it is remediated on a managed path (§4.4, §5.3).
- **This document states plainly which constituent facts of that condition it can mechanically detect and which it cannot.** The provider announcement is not detectable by any mechanism designed here and enters only through external-signal intake; the check is fully enforceable given an announcement and inert without one, and this exposure is never presented as smaller than it is (§5.1).
- **The blocking check is a further input the existing Merge Gate, Deploy Gates, and release mechanism already consume.** It adds no gate, no bullet to any gate's closed mandatory-check list, no sixth kernel precondition, and no classification row, and binds no specification to acquire a clause it does not carry (§5.3).
- **The check blocks change advancement only.** It never refuses an individual generation action, never disables the tooling, and never becomes a per-invocation precondition (§5.4).
- **No existing approval-trigger row is stretched to hold this condition.** The gap in the closed `matched_trigger` vocabulary is named and routed to a specification ticket, never asserted as a mapping (§5.5).
- **The output-quality floor is a floor to sustain — never an invariant, a gate, a release gate, a scoring mechanism, or a per-suggestion check.** It does not block, delay, or auto-reject any suggestion, and it neither relaxes, substitutes for, nor duplicates the mandatory human-builder confirmation gate, which remains the sole correctness backstop (§6.2).
- **No output-quality tracking mechanism, guardrail-metric identifier, or numeric floor is established here.** The proxies §6.3 names are weak, aggregate-only, attributable to no individual suggestion, and stated as what a future ticket would have available — never as a measurement of the standard (§6.3–§6.4).
- **Neither obligation applies to C-26.** Runtime AI automation is Future / Not-Yet-Authorized, and no rule here accommodates it.
- **Everything above remains domain-neutral and platform-level.** No position, criterion category, check, or proxy encodes the characteristics of any single domain, industry, or vendor; every rule holds identically for every tenant, every region, and any software built on the platform.
- **This document records exactly one ADR.** ADR-066 (§8) is the genuine, independent decision this document makes; every other choice above consumes a mechanism, record, chokepoint, or boundary the cited specification and the cited upstream design documents already fix.
