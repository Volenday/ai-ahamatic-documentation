# AI Model Governance and Output-Quality Standards — AI ahaMatic

This document defines two obligations `DECISIONS.md` **D-58** authorizes together as one investigation: **the platform's obligation to govern the AI model(s) its AI-assisted builder tooling (C-19) depends on** — how such a model is selected, how dependence on a single provider is mitigated, and how a provider's deprecation of a relied-upon model is handled ("AI model governance") — and **a correctness standard for what that tooling's output must reflect, beyond the existing binary provenance gate** ("AI output-quality standards"). It states **what** each obligation requires; it does not select a model, name a vendor or agent framework, or design any selection mechanism, abstraction layer, measurement technique, or quality gate.

This is a Guardrails-phase artifact. It inherits its framing from the Vision and Charter and is subordinate to it; where it appears to conflict with the charter, the charter prevails. **The two obligations do not share one relationship to `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md`, and this document states the asymmetry rather than flattening it.** §4 (AI model governance) operationalizes the general blocking-check and halt-and-escalate rule of that document's §3 for AI-model continuity risk — a category of harm the enumerated invariants do not separately name, on the identical non-elaboration relationship `02-governance-and-security/08-legal-and-licensing-constraints.md` and `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` already hold toward that same §3. §5 (AI output-quality standards) elaborates no invariant and states no blocking check at all: it is a floor to sustain and measure, on the guardrail-metric side of the three-instrument separation `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §2 already draws between an invariant, a guardrail metric, and a release gate — never the invariant side. Neither section states or implies a vendor, model, or agent-framework decision; every model-governance rule below is a category or a criterion, never a product, on the identical discipline `docs/criteria/`'s own tool-opinion class already uses (§2 states why that library cannot discharge this document's obligation itself). It cites, rather than re-derives, the canonical capabilities of `01-business-and-ux/02-prd.md` — **C-19 (AI-assisted builder tooling)**, whose own definition this document adds standing obligations to without restating or narrowing it, and **C-26 (runtime AI automation)**, whose Future / Not-Yet-Authorized boundary this document holds absolutely and never touches. Every rule here is **platform-level and domain-neutral** — it holds for any software built on the platform, in any tenant and any region, and is never satisfied or excused by the state of a single application.

This document owns **the model-governance obligation of §4** (selection-criteria disclosure, lock-in-mitigation position, and deprecation-handling as a blocking check) and **the output-quality standard of §5** (a correctness expectation for AI-assisted tooling's suggestion output, held as a floor to sustain, not a gate to enforce). It does not own vendor and dependency criticality tiering or ongoing operational-risk monitoring for an already-incorporated third party (`02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md`), the security-defect categories a generated artifact is validated against or the provenance gate that governs an artifact's handling (`02-governance-and-security/02-security-policy.md` §3.3, §5, §11; `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`; `docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md`), the one-time selection of a tool class for a given engagement (`docs/criteria/criteria-document-map.md`), any numeric threshold this document's obligations might otherwise imply (`03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`), or escalation, review, and audit mechanics (the Meta-Operations documents and `02-governance-and-security/07-audit-and-traceability.md`) — each is owned elsewhere and cited, not restated.

## 1. Purpose and Reading Order

The document answers five questions:

- **What gap this document closes, and why the library's four nearest-adjacent mechanisms each answer a different question** — confirmed by direct reads, not assumed from the coverage audit's own summary.
- **Where this obligation attaches**, and along what dimensions it holds.
- **What AI model governance requires of the platform** — selection-criteria disclosure, a lock-in-mitigation position, and deprecation-handling as a blocking check — stated as categories, never a product.
- **What the AI output-quality standard is**, and how it differs in kind, not only in degree, from the provenance gate it sits beside.
- **Where each obligation's authority ends**, and what remains for a future design-phase ticket to realize.

It is structured as a pyramid: first the gap and its boundary against adjacent mechanisms, then the scope both obligations share, then the model-governance obligation, then the output-quality standard, then the precedence and ownership boundaries that bound the whole.

## 2. The Gap, and Why Four Adjacent Mechanisms Each Answer a Different Question

`docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` states, in its own words, at its front matter and again in §2 and in its own ADR, that it "names no such product" and that "every mechanism fixed here governs the suggestion's *handling*, never its *quality*, which is exactly the residual risk `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.3 already accepts and does not ask this document to close." Read directly, `docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.3 confirms exactly that acceptance: *"This mechanism does not, and cannot by construction, prevent the tooling from being misled into proposing an unintended, low-quality, or ill-advised suggestion — a misled suggestion remains a possible outcome of a probabilistic interpretation process, and no mechanism in this document eliminates that possibility... The residual risk this design accepts is a bad proposal; the risk it closes by construction is a bad proposal becoming a bad effect."* Neither document is wrong to decline the question — each is explicit that the question belongs elsewhere. This document is elsewhere.

Four mechanisms already in this library sit close enough to this gap that each must be checked and distinguished directly, rather than assumed not to already cover it:

| Mechanism | The Question It Answers | What It Does Not Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Third-party risk management (`02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md`) | Given a vendor or dependency **already selected**, how critical would its compromise, discontinuation, or acquisition be, and what ongoing signal requires that judgment to be revisited? | That document's own §6 states plainly: "The one-time selection of a tool class is owned by the relevant third-party tool opinion in `docs/criteria/criteria-document-map.md`; this document governs the vendor's risk after selection, never which vendor is selected." It tiers and monitors an AI model provider exactly as it does any other Foundational-tier dependency once incorporated — but selection criteria, a lock-in-mitigation position, and a defined deprecation-handling obligation are none of the five re-assessment triggers its §5 enumerates, and it says nothing about the correctness of what a tooling built on the vendor's model produces. |
| AI-generated-artifact security risk (`02-governance-and-security/02-security-policy.md` §3.1–§3.3, §5; realized by `docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md`) | Does a generated artifact carry a vulnerability, an embedded secret, an insecure pattern, or license-encumbered or injected content, and is it granted trust before validation? | §3.3's own threat-category row is scoped to a named defect list — vulnerability, secret, insecure pattern, license/injected content — never correctness or fitness for the builder's own request; the design realization quoted above states this exclusion directly, in its own words. |
| Third-party tool opinion (`docs/criteria/criteria-document-map.md`) | Which member of a *class* of tool best fits **a client's own circumstances**, at the point of adoption — a reusable, client-portable criteria set, per that library's own founding admission test ("does this artifact survive being handed to a client?")? | This library is, by its own Precedence section, "an input, never an authority." That section states further that it "may not override, amend, or contradict `docs/spec/`." A reusable selection framework for a future engagement is not a standing obligation this platform's own specification holds itself to — the two are different documents even where their criteria might one day overlap, and this library cannot discharge a specification-level gap on the specification's behalf. |
| Development principles — *Safe* (`docs/criteria/development-principles.md` §3.2) | A candidate practice for one narrow failure-mode class: "Where a class of component's correctness depends on behavior that is hard to verify by reading... prefer an existing, independently-proven implementation of that component over having an AI author one from scratch." | §3.2 names itself as "this document's own derivation... offered as a candidate rather than asserted as settled," and §4 states plainly that *Safe* and *Secure* are "candidates, not settled positions." Its own Precedence section states the whole document is "an input, never an authority." A candidate, not-yet-settled practice, explicitly disclaiming authority, cannot discharge a specification-level standard — the same reasoning `DECISIONS.md` D-58 itself gives for authorizing this document rather than treating the candidate as sufficient. |

**This document's own question is the one none of the four answers:** given an AI model already selected to power the AI-assisted builder tooling (C-19), what standing obligation does the platform hold toward that dependency's *continuity* specifically — as distinct from its general operational risk — and what floor must the tooling's own suggestion output meet that the provenance gate's binary approve/discard decision does not itself require?

## 3. Scope of Both Obligations

Both obligations bind the platform, not any one tenant's use of it.

- **Both attach to the AI-assisted builder tooling (C-19) specifically, and never to C-26.** `01-business-and-ux/02-prd.md` §4 fixes that a Future / Not-Yet-Authorized capability — C-26 (runtime AI automation) among them — "is neither designed nor built, and must not be implemented, expanded, or relied upon by any other capability or by any built software." This document introduces no obligation, hook, or accommodation that a future C-26 capability could occupy, on the identical boundary `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §3 already holds absolutely for the tooling itself.
- **Both are platform-level, not tenant-differentiated.** A generation action is "an action the platform's own autonomous agent takes, at the platform layer..." (`docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §4.2), and the tooling's own model-access credentials are held as platform-core secrets (`docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §6) — the AI model a builder's suggestion is generated from is a platform-provided dependency, not a per-tenant configuration choice; both obligations below hold identically for every tenant and every application, never weakened for one tenant's convenience.
- **Both bind across every lifecycle phase**, exactly as `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §5 requires of the invariant it partly operationalizes (§4) and the guardrail-metric-shaped obligation it does not (§5) — a model dependency selected at design time or an output-quality regression discovered at any later phase is each subject to the obligation, not only at release.
- **Both are domain-neutral.** Neither obligation encodes, favors, or assumes any domain, vertical, or client's own circumstances; each holds for any software the tooling assists in building.

## 4. AI Model Governance

### 4.1 What the Obligation Requires

The platform maintains, for the AI model or models its AI-assisted builder tooling (C-19) depends on, three standing positions — never a one-time record made once and left stale:

- **A documented selection-criteria position.** The criteria by which a relied-upon model is judged fit for this tooling's own purpose are stated and available for review — categories such as its fitness for the four assistance kinds C-19 names, its data-handling and confidentiality posture, its latency and availability characteristics, and its compatibility with the mandatory human-builder confirmation architecture `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §5 already fixes — never a score, a ranking, or a named product. This document states that such criteria must exist and be stated; it does not itself enumerate a complete or weighted set, which is a future criteria-library or design-phase concern, not this document's own content.
- **A documented lock-in-mitigation position.** The platform states, in structural terms, how dependence on any single model provider is mitigated — whether and how the tooling's own invocation surface is held independent of one provider's specific interface, such that a change of provider is a bounded, anticipated event rather than a rebuild. This document requires that such a position be stated and maintained; it does not itself mandate a specific abstraction technique, which is design-phase work.
- **A deprecation-handling obligation.** Where a provider deprecates or announces the sunset of a model or model version the tooling relies on, the platform has, before the deprecation takes effect, a defined transition path to a successor model or version — never a condition first discovered at the point the deprecated model stops serving requests.

### 4.2 Why This Is a Blocking Check, Not an Advisory Position

**An unresolved deprecation — a provider-announced sunset with no defined transition path before the effective date — is a blocking check**, on the identical halt-and-escalate rule `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §3 states and `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` §5 already applies to an unresolved re-assessment trigger on a Foundational-tier vendor: no release proceeds, and the condition halts and escalates, in whatever lifecycle phase the unresolved deprecation is found. This is a narrower, sharper instance of the same general rule that document's own §5 could not itself state, because deprecation-handling is not among the five triggers its table enumerates (§2 above) — it is a continuity obligation specific to a model dependency's own lifecycle, not a general vendor-operational-risk signal.

The selection-criteria and lock-in-mitigation positions of §4.1 are not, by themselves, release-blocking checks in the same sense — their absence is a governance gap to remediate on a managed path, not a condition that halts an in-flight change on its own. **Uncertainty resolves toward the more cautious reading**, exactly as `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md` §4 already states for tier uncertainty: where it cannot be established that a deprecation has a defined transition path, the deprecation is treated as unresolved and the blocking check of this section applies.

### 4.3 What This Section Does Not Do

This section does not select a model, name a vendor or agent framework, mandate a specific abstraction architecture, set a switching-cost budget, or design any mechanism by which a selection criterion, a lock-in position, or a deprecation transition is technically realized, evaluated, monitored, or recorded — every "how" question here is Design-phase work, on the identical boundary every Guardrails-phase document in this library already holds toward the Design phase. It also does not re-tier or re-monitor the model provider as a vendor: that obligation is `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md`'s, in full, and continues to apply to the model provider exactly as it applies to any other incorporated Foundational-tier dependency, independent of and in addition to this section's own narrower continuity obligation.

## 5. AI Output-Quality Standards

### 5.1 The Standard

**An AI-suggested artifact must reflect a good-faith, context-grounded attempt at the request it responds to** — reasoned from the builder's own request and the application, construction-primitive, and entity/schema context the generation action assembles (`docs/design/06-autonomous-agent-implementation/04-prompt-and-context-assembly-design.md` §5; `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §6.2) — and must not knowingly present as complete or fit-for-purpose any content the tooling's own available context already indicates is inconsistent, incomplete, or non-functional. This is a floor on what the AI-assisted builder tooling is expected to produce, stated at the level of the request-and-context it already has available to it — never a claim that a suggestion is correct, and never a guarantee any single suggestion meets it.

### 5.2 How This Differs in Kind From the Provenance Gate, Stated Precisely

Today, an AI-suggested artifact's provenance transitions to `builder-approved` through exactly one path: the mandatory human-builder confirmation, re-clearing security validation on the content actually submitted (`docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §5.4; `docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §4.1). That gate is binary and procedural — it asks whether a human confirmed the artifact and whether it cleared the named security defects (§2 above), never whether the underlying suggestion was any good before the builder was asked to look at it. §5.1 adds a standard the gate itself does not ask for: an expectation on the generation action's own conduct, prior to and independent of whatever the confirming builder happens to catch.

**This standard is not, and does not become, a second confirmation gate, a scoring mechanism, or a blocking check on any individual suggestion.** `docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.3 already states, correctly, that "a misled suggestion remains a possible outcome of a probabilistic interpretation process, and no mechanism in this document eliminates that possibility" — this document does not eliminate it either, and does not claim to. The standard sits on the **guardrail-metric side** of the three-instrument separation `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §2 already draws — a floor to sustain and, where the design or non-functional-requirements layer later finds it measurable, to track over time — never on the invariant side, and never itself a release gate. The confirmation gate of `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §5 remains the sole correctness backstop no output-quality floor may substitute for or weaken.

### 5.3 What This Section Does Not Do

This section does not design a quality gate, a check, an evaluation harness, or a scoring mechanism; it does not state how conformance to §5.1's standard is measured, sampled, or improved; and it does not set a numeric acceptable-error-rate. Whether conformance is ever made mechanically checkable, and by what means, is a future Design-phase ticket's question to answer, never assumed here. Should the design or non-functional-requirements layer later determine that a quantified floor is measurable and warranted, that numeric value is owned by `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`, on the identical deferral pattern that document already applies to every other quality attribute in this library — this document states the standard, not its number.

### 5.4 Domain Neutrality

The standard is stated in structural terms only — context-groundedness and non-contradiction with the tooling's own already-assembled context — never as a domain-specific correctness rule. It holds identically regardless of what a builder is constructing, on the same discipline `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §7 already states for the tooling's own assistance kinds: "there is no parameter, field, or extension point through which a suggestion could carry domain-specific content into the platform core, because every suggestion is confined to instances and configurations of an already-closed, platform-owned vocabulary."

## 6. Precedence and Ownership Boundaries

When a rule in this document meets any other consideration, it is resolved by the fixed precedence of `02-governance-and-security/01-system-invariants.md` §6, which extends the trade-off rule of `01-business-and-ux/06-value-proposition-and-success-metrics.md` §7.

- **The charter prevails.** No rule here is read or applied in a way that contradicts the Vision and Charter; where this document appears to conflict with the charter, the charter governs.
- **These obligations are never degraded to improve a success metric, meet a deadline, or satisfy a request.** The model-governance obligation of §4 and the output-quality standard of §5 are each honored first; success is optimized only in the space that leaves intact.
- **An unresolved deprecation overrides apparent gain.** A change that would leave a model or model-version deprecation unresolved past its effective date (§4.2) is refused regardless of the value it appears to create.
- **C-26 is never designed, implied, or accommodated.** No rule in this document, and no future amendment to it, may introduce a hook, extension point, or accommodation a runtime AI automation capability could occupy without a fresh authorization and a fresh specification ticket.

This document owns the model-governance obligation of §4 and the output-quality standard of §5. It does not own, and none of the following documents' authority is diminished by this one:

- **The capability this document adds obligations to** — `01-business-and-ux/02-prd.md` C-19, and the C-26 boundary it holds absolutely — remains authoritative; this document consumes it and never edits, narrows, or widens it.
- **Vendor and dependency criticality tiering and ongoing operational-risk monitoring**, including for the model provider itself once selected, are owned by `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md`, in full; this document's own continuity obligation (§4) is narrower and additional, never a restatement.
- **The security-defect categories a generated artifact is validated against, the provenance gate, and its enforcement point** are owned by `02-governance-and-security/02-security-policy.md` §3, §5, §11 and realized by `docs/design/02-identity-access-security/05-ai-tooling-security-design.md`, in full; this document adds no defect category and does not touch the provenance mechanism.
- **The suggestion model, the mandatory human-builder confirmation gate, and the tooling's programmatic interface** are owned by `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`, in full; §5 above states a standard that document's generation action is held to and does not redesign any mechanism that document owns.
- **The one-time selection of a tool class for a given engagement** remains owned by the relevant document in `docs/criteria/criteria-document-map.md`, on that library's own terms; this document's obligation is what AI ahaMatic's own specification holds itself to, never a reusable selection framework for a future client.
- **Any numeric threshold this document's obligations might otherwise imply** is owned by `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md`.
- **Audit, attribution, and tamper-evidence** for any model-governance or output-quality-related finding are owned by `02-governance-and-security/07-audit-and-traceability.md`.
- **Enforcement mechanics** — how a halt is carried out and how an escalation is requested, routed, and recorded — are owned by the Meta-Operations documents, chiefly `05-meta-operations/04-human-in-the-loop-protocol.md`, `05-meta-operations/07-self-correction-and-fallback-protocol.md`, and `05-meta-operations/01-agent-operating-charter.md`.

## 7. Binding Rules

These rules hold for every actor and every action subject to this document and are subordinate to the charter.

- **No model, vendor, or agent framework is named as a decision anywhere in this document.** Every model-governance rule is a category or a criterion, on the identical discipline `docs/criteria/`'s own tool-opinion class already uses.
- **A relied-upon model's selection criteria and lock-in-mitigation position are documented and kept current**, never recorded once and left stale.
- **An unresolved model or model-version deprecation is a blocking check.** No release proceeds while a provider-announced deprecation lacks a defined transition path before its effective date; uncertainty resolves toward treating the deprecation as unresolved.
- **The model-governance obligation is additional to, and never a substitute for, `02-governance-and-security/09-third-party-risk-management.md`'s own tiering and monitoring** of the same model provider as an incorporated vendor.
- **AI-assisted tooling's suggestion output is held to a context-grounded correctness standard**, independent of and prior to whatever the mandatory human-builder confirmation gate separately catches.
- **The output-quality standard is a floor to sustain, never an invariant, a gate, or a scoring mechanism.** It does not block, delay, or auto-reject any individual suggestion, and it does not relax, substitute for, or duplicate the mandatory confirmation gate `docs/design/03-data-construction-contracts/09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` §5 owns.
- **Neither obligation applies to C-26.** Runtime AI automation is Future / Not-Yet-Authorized, and no rule here accommodates it.
- **Everything in this document remains domain-neutral.** No criterion, position, or standard encodes the characteristics of any single domain, industry, or vendor.
