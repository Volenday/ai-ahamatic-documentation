# Coverage Audit — BACKLOG.md §2's Seventeen Candidate Areas

Prepared for the project lead. This is a working document for scoping — it establishes what the library already covers, what residue remains, and a keep / extend / new-document / out-of-scope verdict per area, so each area can be ticketed individually without any being specified twice. It recommends; it specifies nothing, mints no term, and edits no file under `docs/`.

## How this was built, and why that matters more than usual

`BACKLOG.md` §1af(a) records that this project shipped a wrong finding once already: an Orchestrator's verification pass read a section through a script that truncated it at 250 characters, the cut landed immediately before the sentence that disproved the claim being made, and "this section says nothing about X" was asserted, written into the backlog, carried into a ticket prompt as fact, and executed against a specification that was already correct. The failure was not carelessness — it was treating a partial read as sufficient evidence for a negative claim. **A partial read can support a positive claim ("this string is here") and can never support a negative one ("this document says nothing about X") — a negative claim is a statement about everything the source doesn't say, and nothing short of reading all of it can establish that.**

Every "not covered" verdict below states the method that established it: the search terms tried, and which documents were read in full (not grepped, not excerpted) before the negative was asserted. Two negative claims of particular consequence — bias/fairness's 58-hit false-positive sweep, and the acceptable-use/AUP zero-hit sweep — were independently re-run and confirmed rather than taken on a single pass. Where a prior backlog note already made a coverage claim (six of the seventeen areas carry one), that note is treated as a claim to verify, not a conclusion to inherit, and each is explicitly confirmed or corrected below.

Findings about what the library currently contains are **verified** — established by reading the cited text. Recommendations about what a future document should contain, or how urgent an area is relative to another, are **reasoned** — judgment applied to the verified facts, flagged as such per `PROCESS.md` §12.3.

**Scope of "the library."** `docs/spec/` holds 37 files (36 documents plus its own index) and `docs/design/` holds 43 (42 plus its own index) — 80 documents total (36 + 42 + the two indexes = confirmed by direct file count at audit time). This matches the "80 documents" figure the area-16 backlog note cites; that figure was independently re-verified here rather than trusted. `docs/criteria/` (4 content documents) was searched and read throughout, but is a distinct, non-authoritative library per `docs/criteria/criteria-document-map.md` — a criteria document informs a specification, it can never substitute for one, so criteria-library content is reported as adjacent evidence, never as closing a gap.

## Summary

**The headline finding: this audit is not primarily a documentation-completeness exercise.** Of the seventeen areas, only three resolve cleanly on the documents alone (two "out of scope," one "extend, no gate"). The other fourteen turn, in whole or in part, on a scope or judgment call only the lead can make — most often the same underlying question in different clothes: **does this technical specification library hold this kind of content at all, or is it a business, legal, or customer-success artifact that sits outside what this library is for?** That single question gates or partially gates tenant provisioning, platform SLA, builder onboarding (its training half), DR/BCP (its non-IT half), platform usage/AUP, AI model governance, AI output-quality standards, bias/fairness, accessibility conformance, UI/UX and design-system coverage, data migration/import capability scope, offline resilience (its web-surface half), and load testing's testing-strategy question. Pricing and go-to-market are the areas where the documents already answer that question decisively — "no" — which is why they alone reach a clean out-of-scope verdict without needing to ask.

**Verdict distribution:**

| Verdict | Areas |
|---|---|

| **Extend, evidence-driven** | DR/BCP (its IT-recovery half); Builder onboarding (its narrow "add a builder to a tenant" half) |

| **New document, evidence-driven** | Third-party risk management |

**What's most urgent.** `DECISIONS.md` D-31 instructs a daily rapid-prototyping loop with screens shown and feedback captured starting the day the App Creator increment begins (per D-34's build order, not day one — see Area 16). The area this created acute pressure on — UI/UX and design-system coverage — has had that pressure **partially relieved since the backlog note was written**: ADR-060 (`docs/design/01-design-foundation/01-technology-stack-design.md` §24, landed 2026-08-14, one day after the note's "most immediately blocking" framing) selected the platform console's component-and-styling foundation. The deeper questions — a committed visual identity, an accessibility-conformance target, the mobile client's foundation, and the future UI generator's foundation — remain open, but the specific thing that would have blocked tomorrow's screen is decided. **The backlog note's own text is now stale relative to the design library and should be corrected the next time §2 is touched** — a finding for the Orchestrator's tracker maintenance, not an edit this report makes.

**What needs a decision before it can move at all.** Bias/fairness is the one area where the evidence doesn't even point toward a likely answer — it's a total, clean absence with no adjacent mechanism to extend and no in-library statement (declared or implied) of why. Application lifecycle is blocked on an internal inconsistency: `01-business-and-ux/05-user-journeys.md`'s "Safe evolution" journey implies C-15/C-17 cover a built application's own deprecation, while the PRD's own C-15/C-17 definitions scope both capabilities to the *platform's* evolution, not a builder's application. No ticket can be correctly scoped here until that's resolved.

## 1. Tenant provisioning

**Coverage (verified).** The schema-level mechanics of what a new tenant *is*, once it exists, are solid: `docs/design/01-design-foundation/04-scalability-availability-and-performance-design.md:143` — *"Onboarding a tenant is a data-layer event under the fixed shape... new rows in `platform.tenants` and `platform.applications`, a new `tenant_<id>` schema and, per application, a new `tenant_<id>_app_<id>` schema, and the corresponding database roles and grants,"* executed as *"an isolated, out-of-band operation, drawn from a dedicated connection allocation"* (line 145). A steward role owns admission at the platform boundary: `docs/spec/02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md:64` — *"Tenant admission | Admits and removes tenants at the platform boundary, instantiating each tenant owner's authority."* A minimal build-time journey exists: `docs/spec/01-business-and-ux/05-user-journeys.md:52` — *"Tenant establishment | Tenant owner | Being admitted to the platform, receiving an isolated tenant boundary, and holding top authority within it before anything is built."* The event is a mandatory audit trigger (`docs/spec/02-governance-and-security/07-audit-and-traceability.md:56`) and has a success-metric anchor (`docs/spec/03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md:137`, time-to-first-operating-artifact measured from tenant establishment).

**Gap (verified).** None of this describes the *process*: how a prospective tenant's request originates (self-service, sales-assisted, or steward-initiated), what's collected, how the first `platform.platform_users` row for the tenant owner comes to exist, whether admission is automatic or requires a human decision and on what criteria, or credential issuance. Confirmed by exhaustive search — `self-service`, `sign-up`/`signup`, `registration form`, `trial`, `invite`/`invitation`, `welcome email`, `activation email`, and `initial credentials` all return zero hits library-wide in a tenant-provisioning sense, and C-01's own PRD definition (`01-business-and-ux/02-prd.md:68`) scopes the capability to isolation mechanics only — provisioning-as-a-process isn't part of its definition.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision on richness, then Extend or New document.** Whether this is a thin administrative action (a steward admits, done — extends `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` and its design counterpart) or a rich business process (self-service trial, billing, KYC — needs a standalone document) is a product decision the documents don't make and this audit can't make for the lead. The mechanism side (schema, isolation, audit) needs no rework either way.

## 2. Platform SLA

**Coverage (verified).** Only content that resembles an SLA if read carelessly, and is explicitly framed otherwise. NFR §6 fixes engineering floors — `03-software-and-architecture/06-non-functional-requirements.md:91`, *"Platform-core availability, per region, measured monthly | ≥ 99.9%"* — under a document whose own header states it "does not describe how any target is measured, instrumented, or enforced in tooling," with breaches framed as release-blocking regressions, never customer remedies. The only literal string "SLA" anywhere in the library is internal alert-routing terminology: `docs/design/05-infrastructure-deployment-operations/04-observability-and-monitoring-design.md:117` — *"the deploy-time configuration for Development or Staging may set a lower urgency (a longer response-time SLA...)"* — a Dev/Staging alert-priority knob, not a tenant-facing commitment.

**Gap (verified).** Total. `uptime` (bare word) returns zero hits anywhere in either library — independently re-confirmed. `customer support`, `help desk`, `support ticket`, `terms of service`, and `master service agreement` all return zero hits. `02-governance-and-security/08-legal-and-licensing-constraints.md`, the document whose title most suggests it might own contractual obligations, was read in full and contains only dependency-licensing content.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision on whether this belongs in the library at all.** An SLA is a commercial/legal artifact backed by financial remedies; this library's own framing (`context-document-map.md:3`) is what an autonomous agent needs to build and operate the platform, not customer-facing legal collateral. If the lead wants it in-library, the natural home is a new document in `docs/spec/02-governance-and-security/` that *derives* customer commitments from the existing NFR floors without restating them — never contradicting them.

## 3. Disaster recovery / business continuity (DR/BCP)

**Coverage (verified).** Deeper than the inherited note implies. Spec §6 fixes six explicit guarantees (recoverability to a valid state, zero data loss on restore, return to last-known-good, no guarantee breach, completion within the migration ceiling, restore itself observable/reversible). NFR provides an MTTR floor — `06-non-functional-requirements.md:104`, *"Mean time to recovery, platform-core incident | ≤ 60 minutes from detection to restoration of the availability floor of §6"* — and a migration duration/downtime ceiling (line 105). The design counterpart (`05-infrastructure-deployment-operations/06-incident-response-and-recovery-design.md`) names its own honest limits rather than overclaiming: a "recovery-point gap" (committed data written after the last backup and before the incident isn't restorable) and that restore "is a terminal instrument, not an infinitely regressing one" (§6.5).

**Gap (verified).** No literal RPO, RTO, "disaster recovery," or "business continuity" terminology appears anywhere — confirmed by library-wide search, zero hits. No whole-region or provider-outage failover scenario is designed (the only region-unavailability discussion in `08-multi-region-distribution-design.md` concerns registry-read latency/routing, not a declared secondary-region activation procedure). No non-IT continuity dimension (staffing, facilities, customer communications during an outage) exists anywhere, and spec §2 explicitly excludes a builder's own application-level incident response from this document's scope.

**Verdict: split.** **Extend** `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/06-incident-response-and-recovery.md` (and its design counterpart) for an explicit RPO figure and a region-failure scenario — natural extensions of the document's own §6, needing no new document. **Needs a lead decision** on whether a genuine non-IT BCP belongs in this library at all; if so, it's organizationally distinct enough (staffing, facilities, vendor-of-the-operator continuity) to warrant its own document rather than an extension.

## 4. Builder onboarding

**Coverage (verified).** Only the definitional edge: who a builder persona *is* (`01-business-and-ux/04-personas-and-roles.md:60`, "professional builders only") and a journey for granting a builder a role within an *already-provisioned* tenant (`01-business-and-ux/05-user-journeys.md:53`, "Access definition").

**Gap (verified).** Everything about how a new builder is actually added, trained, or ramped is absent. No invite/add-member mechanism exists anywhere (confirmed zero hits across roughly fifteen search variants), no tutorial/first-run/certification concept exists, and the asymmetry is itself evidence: the journey table names fifteen journeys including "Tenant establishment" for the tenant owner, but none for adding or training a builder.

**Verdict: two distinct pieces, one clean, one gated.** The narrow slice — the mechanism by which a builder is added to an existing tenant (a `tenant_users` row-creation workflow) — is real, platform-mechanics territory with a total absence and a clear home: **Extend** `02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` §5–§6 and its design counterpart. The broader slice — training, certification, a documentation portal — is very plausibly Customer Success / DevRel material outside this library's own stated charter (parallel to the SLA reasoning above); **needs a lead decision**, and the likely answer, on the documents' own framing, is out of scope.

## 5. Application lifecycle

**Coverage (verified).** `04-operation-reach-distribution/01-application-runtime-and-lifecycle-design.md` was read cover to cover, as the ticket required, and is a sound, deliberately self-limiting document. It defines C-08 by direct citation (line 124: *"the means to move a built application end to end — build, configure, publish, operate — as one continuous, governed flow"*), designs the mechanism (three stable identity anchors, one stored configuration representation read/written identically at every stage), and explicitly hands off adjacent concerns rather than absorbing them: publishing → `04-publishing-and-delivery-design.md`; version control → `03-builder-facing-version-control-design.md`; environment promotion → `02-builder-facing-environment-management-design.md`. The platform schema already anticipates a terminal state — `01-design-foundation/02-platform-data-model-design.md:330`: *"a removed tenant or application is marked with a terminal status rather than deleted outright"* — with a concrete `active`/`suspended`/`offboarding` enum already on the `platform.applications` row.

**Gap (verified), and it is precisely two things.** First: no document owns the *transition* into that terminal status — who may initiate retirement, what preconditions apply, what a builder experiences, or how it interacts with the `published`/`withdrawn` publication toggle `04-publishing-and-delivery-design.md` owns separately (that document cites the status enum only as a structural precedent for its own, unrelated column — it never claims ownership of retirement). Second, and more fundamental: the specification is internally ambiguous about which capability *would* own this. `01-business-and-ux/05-user-journeys.md:66` names a "Safe evolution" journey covering *"changing, maintaining, and deprecating built software over time"* under C-15/C-16/C-17 — but the PRD's own definitions of C-15 (*"the means to change **the platform** over time"*) and C-17 (*"introduce, deprecate, and retire **capabilities and contracts**"*) scope both to the platform's own evolution, not a builder's application. This is exactly the kind of drift `ai-aha-consistency-check` exists to catch; it is reported here as a finding, not resolved.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision before anything else can move.** The runtime-and-lifecycle document itself needs no rework — it correctly scoped itself to four transitions and correctly declined a fifth. But no ticket can be correctly scoped to design built-application retirement until the lead resolves whether C-15/C-17 are meant to reach it (in which case: extend the PRD's own capability definitions, then a new Layer 4 design document) or whether it is a genuinely new, currently-unnamed capability (in which case: a new PRD row before any design work).

## 7. Third-party risk management

**Coverage (verified).** Three candidate documents were read in full and each is confirmed to be a *different* mechanism. `08-legal-and-licensing-constraints.md` and its design counterpart are license-compliance only (`08-legal-and-licensing-constraints.md:7`: *"permitted vs. prohibited license categories, the third-party dependency policy, attribution requirements, and license-incompatibility"*) — the design counterpart's "License Sweep" is a periodic re-check of license *category* drift, never of a vendor's operational risk. `06-integration-and-extensibility-design.md`'s ADR-033 sandboxing is a code-execution confinement mechanism, and states its own honest limit: *"Capability confinement bounds reach; it does not vet intent within a granted reach... a data-flow and intent risk, not a confinement failure"* (§5.5). The two `docs/criteria/` tool-opinion documents are, by the criteria map's own definition, *"the criteria by which a client's circumstances point to one member of [a tool] class over another, not a one-time procurement decision"* — a selection framework, not an ongoing risk process.

**Gap (verified).** Total. `vendor risk`, `third-party risk`, `due diligence`, `vendor assessment`, `criticality tier`, and `vendor monitoring` all return zero hits library-wide. Nothing tiers dependencies/integrations/marketplace vendors by criticality, and nothing requires ongoing (non-license) monitoring of a vendor's security posture, breach history, or service continuity.

**Verdict: New document.** This is the cleanest "new document" call in the audit — the gap is total, the boundary against every adjacent mechanism is confirmed by full reads rather than assumed, and no scope ambiguity comparable to the SLA/AUP cases surfaced: a governance document distinct from license compliance is squarely the kind of content `docs/spec/02-governance-and-security/` already holds seven of. Natural home: sibling to `08-legal-and-licensing-constraints.md`. A narrower fallback exists — generalizing the existing License Sweep pattern from "license category" to "vendor risk tier" as an extension rather than a new document — but the primary recommendation is New document.

## 8. AI model governance

**Coverage (verified).** Present only as explicit *non*-coverage. `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md:45` states under "What This Document Does Not Own": *"Any specific model, vendor, or agent framework. This document records exactly one ADR... and names no such product."* `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` treats model-access credentials as ordinary secrets (§6) but makes no claim about model selection, versioning, or capability, and states so directly (line 31: *"not a claim about any specific model's or provider's current filtering capability"*).

**Verdict: needs a lead decision, before sizing.** The library's repeated, deliberate refusal to name a model or vendor is a defensible domain-neutrality-adjacent stance, but it's in tension with what "vendor lock-in mitigation" or "deprecation handling" would require the document to discuss, even abstractly. That tension should go to the lead before a document is drafted, not be resolved unilaterally here. If pursued, the natural anchor is alongside `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md` and `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md`.

## 9. AI output-quality standards

**Coverage (verified).** Two mechanisms exist and both are explicitly *not* this. The provenance gate (`05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §4.1) is binary — builder confirmation and security validation — and governs *whether* an artifact crosses a boundary, never its correctness. `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md:9` states the boundary itself: *"every mechanism fixed here governs the suggestion's handling, never its quality, which is exactly the residual risk `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` §3.3 already accepts and does not ask this document to close."* Coding standards (`08-coding-standards-and-patterns-design.md`, read cover to cover, 222 lines) apply identically regardless of authorship — zero references to AI or C-19 anywhere in it, confirming it is general code quality, not AI-specific. The self-correction ladder (both spec and design read in full) governs the *agent's own conduct* in response to detected regressions — a process discipline, never a content-quality judgment.

**Gap (verified).** No correctness threshold, acceptable error rate, or quality gate specific to what the AI tooling *produces* exists anywhere — and unusually, the library says so in its own words three separate times (quoted above) rather than being silent on it. `docs/criteria/development-principles.md`'s "Atomic" principle is adjacent (bounding unit size limits the surface an AI can invent unstated behavior on) but is explicitly an input, never an authority, and explicitly declines to fix any numeric threshold (§5: *"a number belongs to an engagement's own configuration... not fixed by this document"*) — it cannot discharge a specification-level quality standard even if the lead wanted it to.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision, leaning New document or an extension anchored in `09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`.** The gap is real and self-disclosed by the library, which makes the "should this exist" question narrower than areas 8 or 10 — but a quality *standard* is a "what," and the design document has nothing to realize until a spec-level statement exists, so a design-only fix isn't available.

## 10. Bias / fairness

**Coverage (verified).** None. A library-wide search for `bias|fair(ness)?|discriminat|disparate|equitable` returns 58 hits; every one was inspected in context (this was independently re-run for this report, not taken on a single pass). ~54 are the technical term "discriminated" (event-type/ADR vocabulary — "a discriminated type," "did not discriminate between the candidates"). The remaining hits are: `07-competitive-landscape.md:119`'s "domain or ecosystem bias" (describing *competing vendors'* market positioning), and two uses of "disparate" in `02-prd.md:98` and `04-api-contract-spec.md:156` describing heterogeneous data sources the cross-system data layer unifies — a data-integration sense of the word, unrelated to fairness. "Fair" and "equitable" as standalone fairness terms return zero genuine hits.

**Gap (verified).** Total. No discussion anywhere of bias in AI-assisted tooling output, disparate treatment from any platform mechanism, or fairness criteria for any automated determination.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision, and this is the one area where the evidence doesn't lean toward an answer.** Unlike areas 8 and 9, there's no adjacent mechanism to extend and no in-library statement — declared or implied — of why this is absent, the way `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md` explicitly disclaims output-quality coverage. It's a genuinely open question whether AI ahaMatic's build-time tooling (generating code/logic/layout, not decisions about people) carries the kind of bias/fairness risk profile most governance frameworks target, or whether — like `development-principles.md` explicitly does for a full security posture and compliance obligations — this is properly left to each client engagement. Domain neutrality is a hard project rule, and bias/fairness is adjacent to named regulatory regimes the project must not appear to adopt; that's a further reason this shouldn't be resolved without the lead.

## 13. Accessibility / internationalization

**The two halves resolve differently, and the prior note correctly kept them adjacent rather than merged.**

**UI localization (verified, settled).** `PROCESS.md` §5 was read in full: *"UI Localization is not planned work... It is not on any roadmap and must not be resurfaced as one."* This is a confirmed, deliberate non-goal, not a gap — and it must not be reopened by this audit. **Verdict: Out of scope**, and settled, not merely reasoned.

**Accessibility conformance (verified, open).** No `docs/spec/` document states any accessibility conformance requirement or standard for the platform, for built applications, or for any actor. This is confirmed at the highest available authority: the design library's own newest relevant ADR states it directly. `01-technology-stack-design.md:981` (ADR-060, §24.2): *"No `docs/spec/` document states an accessibility conformance obligation for AI ahaMatic... The absence of a legal driver is recorded, not papered over; accessible behavior is chosen anyway, as an engineering property rather than a compliance target."* And explicitly, at line 1004: *"Accessibility conformance remains un-scoped — this ADR records an engineering choice that favors accessible behavior, not a conformance target, and does not close that BACKLOG item."* `docs/criteria/ui-component-foundation-tool-opinion.md` (CR04) was read cover to cover and confirmed to supply only an evaluation *criterion* — its own Precedence section (line 164) states: *"Nothing here establishes, lowers, or discharges a conformance requirement; it only supplies the questions to ask of a candidate about one."* Nothing in `04-personas-and-roles.md` or `05-user-journeys.md` (both read in full) adds any accessibility content.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision, then New document.** Whether the platform carries an accessibility conformance obligation is fundamentally a legal/market question (CR04 itself frames conformance as *"frequently a legal obligation in the market a client sells into"*) — not one this documentation review can resolve. If yes, the natural home is either a new NFR-style row in `06-non-functional-requirements.md` or a short new document in `01-business-and-ux/`, since accessibility is a UX/acceptance-criteria matter, not an architectural one; ADR-060 already demonstrates the design layer cannot resolve this on the spec's behalf.

## 14. Data migration / import

**Coverage (verified).** "Migration" in `03-data-model-and-entity-spec.md` §7 is defined precisely: *"A **migration** is a change to a schema's definition..., or to data already committed under it, applied after instances already exist under the prior definition"* — schema/structural evolution of data the platform already holds, not external data entering it. NFR's migration ceiling (§7) quantifies the identical concept. C-27's administrative interface (`03-data-administration-design.md`, read in full) is confirmed single-record only: *"Create | An instance write with no prior `record_id` — the first version of a new logical record"* (line 71) — no batch-write shape anywhere. C-24's cross-system data layer (`07-cross-system-data-layer-design.md`, read in full) is architecturally a live, per-call, validated access surface — every call carries one addressable resource and one action — structurally incompatible with a bulk-load pipeline, and the domain glossary explicitly rejects ETL/data-integration vocabulary for it (a terminology choice, not evidence of a hidden bulk mechanism).

**Gap (verified).** Total. No mechanism anywhere brings a body of pre-existing external data into a built application's entities in bulk — no CSV/spreadsheet upload, no bulk-insert API, no staged/ETL pipeline, no data-seeding step at application creation. Confirmed by reading the two capabilities (C-24, C-27) most structurally likely to carry this in full and finding both explicitly single-record/per-call by design, not merely silent on the point.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision on capability scope, then New document.** This cannot be discharged by extending an existing owner — the data-model spec's "migration" is definitionally schema evolution and redefining it would collide with its own INV scope; C-27 and C-24 are both structurally single-record/per-call by an already-settled design decision. A bulk-import capability, if wanted, needs its own PRD row before a spec or design document can be written against it — this is a capability-scope decision, not a documentation gap fillable by extension.

## 15. Offline resilience

**Coverage (verified).** Deep on mobile, explicitly bounded elsewhere. `05-mobile-application-delivery-design.md` (read in full, including its full ADR) designs a local relational store, a durable write queue with a terminal status vocabulary, and states plainly: *"the standardised query library does not deliver write durability by default"* (line 117) — which is why a bespoke queue was designed. The one genuinely platform-wide (not mobile-only) client-state decision, ADR-011 (`01-technology-stack-design.md` §21, read in full), picks server-authoritative data with optimistic UI, explicitly rejects full bidirectional sync, and — critically — names its own limit rather than hiding it: *"Server-authoritative-with-optimistic-UI is sound as the platform's default; a genuinely offline-first application, built on the platform, may need an escape hatch this decision does not itself design"* (line 857).

**Gap (verified).** No platform-wide (web builder console, or a built application's web runtime) offline-write durability mechanism exists — only mobile has one, and ADR-011 itself says so. `disconnected`, `network partition`, and `degraded connectivity` appear nowhere outside the mobile document's own device-scoped discussion, confirming there is no separate platform-level vocabulary or mechanism for connectivity loss.

**Verdict: Extend, gated on a lead scope decision.** ADR-011 already names exactly the open question — an "escape hatch it does not itself design" — so if web-surface offline resilience is wanted, this is a natural extension of that ADR (or a sibling document to the mobile one), not a new-document decision from scratch. Whether it's wanted at all is a scope call: the platform's charter is web-focused and explicitly declines desktop (`DECISIONS.md` D-05), and how much offline behavior a browser-based builder tool needs is a real question, not a given.

## 16. UI / UX and design-system coverage

**Coverage (verified) — the zero-hit claim re-verified and largely holds, with one dated element.** Re-running `grep -rin "design system\|component librar\|style guide\|wireframe\|visual design" docs/spec docs/design` today returns 3 hits, not 0 — the library changed the day after the note was written. All three are non-substantive: two describe *other products'* component-library ecosystems (`01-technology-stack-design.md:111,113`), and the third is the design library quoting the backlog note back at itself (`01-technology-stack-design.md:982`: *"AI ahaMatic has no established visual identity or design system (`BACKLOG.md` §2, verified zero hits...)"*). A broader synonym sweep (`UI kit`, `design token`, `brand guideline`, `layout system`, `typography`, `color palette`) adds nothing. So the substance — no document specifies AI ahaMatic's own design system, component library, style guide, or visual identity — still holds. `docs/spec/01-business-and-ux/` was confirmed, via full reads of `02-prd.md`, `04-personas-and-roles.md`, and `05-user-journeys.md`, to be product/business material with zero visual or interaction standards. CR04 was confirmed, by its own text, to supply selection criteria only, never a UI specification (§2: *"it does not conclude that adopting is generally preferable to building"*; it names no product as its conclusion).

**What has changed, and matters for prominence.** ADR-060 (`01-technology-stack-design.md` §24, landed 2026-08-14 — one day after the backlog note) selected, for the platform console's Data Administration screens specifically, a component/styling/token foundation: *"shadcn/ui's registry, defaulted to Base UI... styled with Tailwind CSS (v4), themed through CSS custom properties"* (§24.6). The ADR is explicit about what it does not decide: the mobile client's foundation, the future UI generator's foundation, and — restated — that *"AI ahaMatic has no established visual identity or design system... A foundation that treats visual identity as a parameter, not a fact, keeps that question open"* (§24.2, row 4).

**Gap (verified).** No committed visual identity, brand guideline, color palette, or typography scale anywhere; the component-foundation decision covers exactly one surface (the platform console); the mobile client's and the future UI generator's foundations are undecided; and, per Area 13, accessibility conformance is unscoped.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision, then New document.** Whether the platform commits to its own visual identity/design system — as opposed to treating it as a permanently deferred, implementation-time parameter, which is what ADR-060 currently does deliberately — is a product/brand decision no design ticket can make on the spec's behalf. If yes, the natural home is a new document in `docs/spec/01-business-and-ux/`, alongside personas and journeys.

**Prominence: downgrade from "most immediately blocking," with the correction stated precisely.** The backlog note's framing was accurate on 2026-08-13: D-31 mandates daily screens, and at that moment nothing said what tool those screens would be built with — the exact "the default outcome is not 'no decision,' it is a decision made implicitly by the first screen" risk CR04 itself describes. ADR-060 closed exactly that risk for the screens D-31's cadence actually produces (Data Administration, per `TICKET.md` Q11's settled reading, and per `DECISIONS.md` D-34 the cadence begins at the App Creator increment, not day one). What remains open — visual identity, accessibility conformance, the mobile and UI-generator foundations — is real, but it is not what was creating daily-prototyping urgency; ADR-060 deliberately chose an unopinionated foundation precisely so visual identity *wouldn't* need to be decided under that pressure. **This is a finding for the Orchestrator's next tracker-maintenance pass, not an edit this report makes** — `BACKLOG.md` §2's own text should be corrected to cite ADR-060 rather than continuing to read as though nothing has moved.

## 17. Load testing

**Coverage (verified).** `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/03-testing-and-quality-gates.md` (read in full) names seven test layers in its §4 table — unit, integration, contract, invariant-conformance, security, non-functional, end-to-end lifecycle. "Load," "stress," and "performance" are not named layers; the closest is Non-functional, defined as: *"Measured performance, scalability, availability, reliability, and resource behavior meets every applicable numeric target... as defined by `06-non-functional-requirements.md` §4–§11"* (line 67) — a real verification obligation, not merely a target with nothing attached. Its design counterpart (`03-testing-and-quality-gates-design.md`, read in full) confirms this is realized as an actual suite exercising the already-designed mechanism *"under load and failure conditions"* (line 111), and names one concrete pre-deploy mechanism: a schema-count check used as "a leading indicator" (line 119).

**Gap (verified), and it is narrower and sharper than "nothing exists."** Targets exist (NFR §5) and a named obligation to validate against them exists (spec §4's Non-functional layer). What's absent is the actual methodology: no ramp profile, no synthetic-load-generation approach, no tooling class, no distinction between a load test and a stress test, no soak/endurance dimension — the phrase "under load and failure conditions" is asserted, never elaborated. More tellingly, for the one concrete numeric threshold the design library does derive (a schema-count crossover), detection is explicitly assigned to *production monitoring*, not pre-release testing (`03-testing-and-quality-gates-design.md:119`, citing `04-observability-and-monitoring.md` as owning "the detection mechanism, instrumentation, and alert routing" for that threshold "entirely"). The words "load test," "stress test," "performance test," and "soak test" appear zero times in either library.

**Verdict: needs a lead decision on strategy, then Extend.** The honest boundary is: is "detect scale pressure in production, not simulate it pre-release" the platform's deliberate, accepted strategy — in which case the fix is to *state* that trade-off explicitly rather than add a testing regime — or is a genuine pre-release load-testing gate wanted, in which case `04-devops-and-cloud-infra/03-testing-and-quality-gates.md` §4 and its design counterpart §5 are the natural, already-identified anchors. Either way this is an extension of an existing document pair, never a new one — the only open question is which direction the extension goes.

## Overlap analysis

The seventeen areas are not independent, and treating any of the following pairs as separate tickets without reading this section risks specifying the same territory twice or missing a sequencing dependency.

**Tenant provisioning ↔ Builder onboarding (narrow half).** Both are events in the same lifecycle — a tenant comes into existence (Area 1), then subsequent builders are added to it (Area 4's narrow slice) — and both point at the same document family (`02-governance-and-security/03-access-control-and-tenancy-model.md` and its design counterpart). They are sequential, not duplicative: the tenant-establishment journey already exists and ends in a valid empty state before any builder is added, so a single ticket could plausibly extend that document family to cover both the "first admin" and "subsequent builder" cases together, rather than as two separate amendments to the same file two tickets apart.

**Application lifecycle ↔ Tenant provisioning — a structural pattern worth naming.** Both `platform.applications` and `platform.tenants` carry the identical `active`/`suspended`/`offboarding`-shaped status enum, and in both cases the *transition* that reaches that terminal status is undesigned — no document says who initiates it or on what grounds, for either row. This is the same defect shape appearing twice, independently, which is worth flagging to whoever schedules the fix: a single design pattern (an authorized-transition mechanism for a terminal-status enum) may be able to close both, rather than two bespoke workflows.

**DR/BCP ↔ Offline resilience.** Both sit under a "continuity under adverse conditions" umbrella, and the ticket that requested this audit specifically asked whether they'd merge. On the evidence, they don't: DR/BCP concerns the *platform's* recovery from its own catastrophic failure (backup, restore, region loss); offline resilience concerns *client-side* continuity when a device or browser loses connectivity to an otherwise-healthy platform. No document or mechanism is shared between them, and the agents that researched each independently reached no shared citation. Track them separately; the adjacency is thematic, not architectural.

**Accessibility/internationalization ↔ UI/UX and design-system coverage.** These share exactly one document — `docs/criteria/ui-component-foundation-tool-opinion.md` — which lists both as sibling class-level constraints (accessibility conformance and visual-identity-as-parameter) any UI-tool candidate is checked against. ADR-060 applied that document once and, in the same table, explicitly narrowed one (a tool is now chosen) while explicitly leaving the other exactly as open as before (*"This ADR narrows the second item... and leaves the first exactly as open as it was,"* §24.2). Internationalization itself is settled and out of scope (Area 13). The two open questions — accessibility conformance and visual identity — are legally- and brand-driven respectively, are not answerable by the same decision, and should be scoped as two decisions even though one criteria document touches both.

**AI model governance ↔ AI output-quality standards ↔ Bias/fairness.** Model governance and output-quality standards are two ends of one currently-undesigned pipe: the same two documents (`09-ai-assisted-builder-tooling-design.md`, `05-ai-tooling-security-design.md`) explicitly name both as residual, deliberately unaddressed risk, using near-identical language, and the provenance mechanism that gates an AI-produced artifact's crossing sits structurally between them — model governance is about what feeds that mechanism, output-quality is about what it lets through. They are tightly coupled and will likely converge on one document or a document pair, but they are not the same question (a lead could fully answer one without touching the other). Bias/fairness is not cited by, or adjacent to, either — it has no existing architectural seam to attach to, unlike the other two, which are named "not yet closed" rather than never mentioned. Treat model-governance-and-output-quality as one scoping conversation, and bias/fairness as a separate, prior legitimacy question.

**Platform usage/AUP ↔ Third-party risk management.** Both are total, clean gaps in `docs/spec/02-governance-and-security/`, both were tested against — and ruled out of — the same set of adjacent mechanisms (security policy's threat model, legal/licensing constraints), and both would sit as new siblings in the same folder if pursued. Third-party risk management reached a clean New-document verdict; platform usage/AUP is gated on the same "is this technical-spec or legal/business material" question that also gates the SLA and builder-onboarding-training areas. If the lead resolves that question once, in general, it likely resolves it for several areas at once rather than needing to be asked area by area.

**Pricing/monetization ↔ Go-to-market.** Already converge on the identical verdict and the identical structural reasoning — the charter's "what the platform is" framing has no slot for either, and the one document that mentions both frames them, together, as outside the capability library by the library's own definition. No separate tracking needed; they were audited as a pair and should stay one.

**Platform SLA ↔ DR/BCP — a sequencing note, not an overlap.** If Platform SLA is pursued, its uptime/remedy figures should *derive from* the existing NFR §6/§7 floors and the DR/BCP extension's MTTR figure, never restate them independently — the SLA document, if written, is downstream of DR/BCP's extension, not parallel to it.

**The meta-overlap.** Twelve of the seventeen areas gate, in whole or in part, on some version of the same question: does this technical specification library hold this kind of content, or is it business/legal/customer-success material this library correctly excludes? That question is not asked area-by-area in the summary table by accident — resolving it once, as a general policy, would very likely collapse several of the "needs a lead decision" rows into fast keep/extend/new-document calls without further research.
